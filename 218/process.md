# No.218 If/Then

## Concept
David Bowen's the two asks whether robots can fall in love: two identical arms, each running a network trained to recognise the other, dancing attracted and repelled; his outsourced narcissism adds the annotation box that labels what the camera sees, with a confidence percentage. This piece stages that courtship on a near-black field. Two small machines, angular two-segment arms on round bases with a single camera eye each, face each other across the dark and run a strict if/then grammar: if the eye cone finds the other, a thin green box snaps around it labelled "mate" with a climbing confidence; if confidence passes sixty, they approach; if the arm tips come within eighty pixels, they hold at ninety seven percent, then retreat fast, rest, and seek again. Each completed courtship raises a hidden familiarity, so the next approach comes a little closer. Dragging a machine aside displaces it; it re-orients and resumes seeking. No hearts, no romance iconography; the feeling comes only from the seeking and the green label.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Each machine is a round base, a post, a turret carrying the eye (dark pupil, one white glint, the only gradient-free highlight), and a two-segment arm solved by two-bone inverse kinematics toward the partner's turret, elbows bending upward. Detection is an honest angle check: the bearing to the partner against the turret direction inside a sixty degree cone, the cone itself never drawn, as Bowen never drew them. A shared courtship state machine runs SEEK, APPROACH, ALMOST (1.5s), RETREAT and REST (2s) on a roughly forty second loop; confidence eases toward per-state targets, fast in retreat, slower while a gaze holds. Approach distance shrinks by forty pixels per unit of familiarity, clamped. The annotation box is an axis-aligned rectangle around the detected partner with a tiny monospace "mate NN%" label in the same #35D461 green; it is the artwork's own content, the one text element the prompt designs. Frame one opens mid-courtship: approach under way, both boxes up, confidences at 74 and 71, arms part-extended. On phones the machines stack vertically and court across the vertical axis. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The first build failed the gate twice over: the behaviour step early-returned on the zero-delta first frame, so detection never registered and the annotation boxes were missing from frame one, and the base disc was drawn over the turret, burying the camera eye. The step now evaluates detection on the first frame, the draw order puts base first with post, arm and turret on top, and the bases grew slightly. Final renders show both machines mid-approach at every size, eyes up with their glints, green boxes labelled mate 71% and mate 74%, the space between them left as the stage.
