# No.214 Sky Scroll

## Concept
David Bowen's cloud drawing machines watch the sky and render it with a pen plotter, stroke by stroke, the finished sheets accumulating like weather history. This piece is that machine on warm paper. A procedural cloud field drifts slowly overhead; the plotter renders it in short ballpoint hatch strokes, dense and heavy where the cloud is thick, thinning to bare paper at the edges, the little red nib working back and forth in bands at a believable pace for about twenty five seconds a drawing. Then the scroll advances: the finished sky slides down into the stack at the bottom edge, where the tops of the previous skies peek, and a fresh sheet begins on a new configuration. The clouds morph continuously, so no two drawings repeat. A double tap advances the scroll early. It must read as pen on paper, never as a filtered photograph, so there is no blue sky anywhere: only ink, paper, and density.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The cloud field is four-octave value noise on a coarse lattice, thresholded into cover and multiplied by an edge falloff so masses dissolve into the paper. Each drawing is a precomputed JS stroke list, no offscreen canvas: thirty four bands worked boustrophedon, samples along each band emitting a short stroke wherever density passes the threshold, three density levels mapping to three stroke weights, strokes in #2A4FA8 with #5A6A8A shadow strokes in the thick cores, plus a second offset stipple stroke in the densest samples so the masses build up like real hatching. The machine reveals the list progressively in nib order while the nib marker rides the current stroke, dwells, then animates the whole stroke list downward into the stack, which keeps at most three finished skies, the older ones easing down one peek as the new one lands. Frame one opens with the current sky half plotted, nib mid-frame, and two finished studies already peeking below. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. Round one read as ruled dashed lines rather than clouds: samples were too sparse, strokes too long, and the density threshold too high, so only wisps survived; sampling density and band count went up, stroke length came down under two field units, the threshold dropped, and dense samples gained their second stipple stroke, after which the masses read as stippled cloud with a dark core and dissolving edges. A leftover no-op in the stack positioning was also cleaned, and the stack now shifts down a peek during the scroll so the finished drawing lands without a pop. Final renders hold at all three sizes: half a sky of blue hatching, the red nib stopped mid-band, yesterday's skies at the bottom edge.
