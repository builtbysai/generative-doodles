# No.276 Crackle

## Concept
After Njalikwa Chongwe's crackle veil: a deep cobalt field covered by a growing black craquelure network, cells like dry earth. This is the veil over a coloured ground, not the sphere piece at No.273: there is no vessel, only the field. Fresh cracks carry a faint hot edge, as if just out of the kiln, then cool to matte black. Idle, the cracks grow slowly from a few seeds and the piece is watchable on its own. Dragging across the field is thermal shock: new crack seeds spawn along the drag path and branch and propagate. A double-tap re-glazes: the network clears and a new seed pattern starts. A single tap does nothing, and nothing ever reloads.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, cover-fitted so the cobalt field fills the frame edge to edge, with the cobalt as the page ground. The palette is held to the five specced colours only: cobalt ground, crack black, the hot edge orange used only while a crack is growing, and the two cell tint blues. The ground is a seeded low-frequency patchwork: a vertex grid on irregular column and row boundaries, jittered per vertex, each cell filled flat in tint A, tint B or the cobalt itself, drawn past the design edges so the cover crop is always covered. The cracks are jittered polyline agents held purely as segment data, drawn directly each frame with no offscreen canvas. Each agent advances in 5px steps on a fixed tick, wanders with a small jitter and occasional angular kinks, splits at about 120 degrees with a fixed probability, and stops at the frame edge or when its next point would meet an existing crack, tested by point-to-segment distance against the stored segments bucketed on a coarse 24px grid. Two guards keep junctions alive: an agent is never killed by its own fresh trail, and never by a segment that already touches the point it stands on, because at a branch junction the parent trail and the child's first segment both pass through that point and are connections, not obstacles. Live agents are capped at 400 and segments at a high safety cap. A segment is drawn in the hot edge colour for its first about 2 seconds, then in crack black; cooled segments are drawn as one batched path, hot segments as a second, and the live agent tips get a small hot dot. Batching keeps the per-frame cost to two strokes no matter how dense the network gets. Seeds arrive three ways: the opening pattern, an idle spawner that drops a new seed on a clear spot about once a second while few agents are live, and the drag. The opening pattern is stratified, one seed per cell of a jittered 3 by 3 grid plus a few free seeds, and the growth is pre-advanced about 12 seconds before the first frame, so frame one opens on a developed network with a few cracks still carrying their hot edge. Pointer handlers convert client coordinates to design coordinates through the stored cover-fit transform (S, OX, OY) set by frame(). A double-tap is two presses within 380ms and 60 design px; it calls the same reset with a new fixed seed and no pre-advance, so the new glaze grows fresh from its seeds. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no text, no audio, no filters or shadowBlur.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners and the four canvas pointer listeners, and a grep confirms exactly the five specced palette colours, no shadowBlur and no offscreen canvas. Three fixes, all found by instrumenting a debug copy of the sim and counting agent deaths by cause. First, the opening render was a scatter of short wormy strands: the per-step jitter was far too strong, so the angle was cut to a small wander plus occasional angular kinks, the branch probability, agent lifespan and seed counts were raised, and the tint grid was rebuilt on irregular boundaries after the first version read as a regular plaid. The network was still sparse, and the death counts showed nearly every agent dying on the hit test in open field. Second fix: a branch child spawns at the parent's exact position, so the parent's next probe landed exactly one step from the child's first segment, inside the kill distance, and every branch silently killed its parent; segments touching the agent's standing point are now skipped as junction connections, which doubled the segment count. Third, coverage still pooled wherever the random seeds happened to land, leaving whole quadrants empty in some draws, so the opening seeds were stratified over a jittered 3 by 3 grid. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: an angular black craquelure network with Y-junctions and closing cells spread across the whole cobalt field over the quiet tint patchwork, several young cracks still orange, including a hot cluster low in the frame. On the phone the cover crop centres on the middle of the field and the network and hot edges stay legible, with cracks running off the frame edges as a field should. No further fixes were needed.
