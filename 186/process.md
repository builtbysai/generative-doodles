# No.186 Tooti Tooti

## Concept
Named for Goodman-Strauss's daughter's pronunciation of "two D, two D", this is the paper-tile end of his tiling work: tiles cut from a folded envelope, so that the creases of the envelope become fold lines on every tile and the symmetry of the cut becomes the symmetry of everything the tiles can build. Tooti tiles a table with them. The piece is a quiet desk: warm paper, a small started cluster, loose tiles scattered at the margins, and no text or instructions anywhere. The envelope chooses the symmetry; the player just puts tiles where the pattern is asking for them.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Three envelopes cycle on double-tap: square cut (D4), triangle cut (D3), hexagon cut (D6). Each has one canonical tile template, a square, triangle or hexagon whose vertices are nudged a few percent off regular, so every tile is the same slightly irregular handmade shape, with two or three fold lines drawn in ink from the template (a diagonal crease and corner folds on the square, three spokes on the triangle, a cross crease on the hexagon). Placement slots are generated per envelope on the matching lattice: a jittered square lattice for D4, hexagon centres on axial coordinates for D6, and for D3 a true polyiamond grown on a jittered triangular point lattice, triangles kept by breadth-first rings from the centre, orientation found by matching each slot against the canonical template. Lattice points are jittered once and shared between neighbouring slots, so edges agree while nothing looks machine cut. Unfilled slots adjacent to the cluster are drawn as ghost outlines in ink at whisper alpha; drag a loose tile within snap distance of a ghost and on release it settles in, rotating to the slot's cut angle over 170ms, and a fresh tile is dealt to the margins. A placed tile adopts its slot's exact outline, paper pressed into place, its fold pattern rotated by the slot's step in the symmetry group, quarter turns for the square cut, sixth turns for the hexagon cut. When every slot of a ring closes, the envelope's symmetry axes draw themselves across the pattern in indigo #2E3A9B and fade over a second or so, once per ring. The desk opens with the centre slot and two neighbours already placed and six loose tiles scattered. Palette exactly per spec: desk #E8DCC8, tiles kraft #C8A97E and cream #EFE6D2 alternating, ink #8A7A5C for outlines, folds and the soft paper shadow, indigo reserved for the axes. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, rAF typeof guarded, and no hint element, per the prompt's ban on instructions.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three. The first frame is the developed state for a placement game: the started cluster sits centre frame with its three fold patterns already differing by their symmetry steps, ghosts ring it faintly, and six creased tiles lie scattered at the margins, none clipped after the spawn range was tightened in from the desk edges. Two build bugs were caught by re-reading before the render, and fixed: the triangle layout's adjacency indices went stale after its ring filter (remapped), and a frame-start animation filter would have eaten snap completions before they registered (removed; settlement is handled in one place). The phone frame keeps the whole arrangement legible at the smaller tile size. The snap, ring detection and axe flash paths cannot be exercised by the still renderer; they were verified by tracing the state flow in the code, and this note says so rather than dressing up a screenshot as a playtest.
