# No.192 Smash Frame

## Concept
Eugene Sargent's Smashography, the device that records the exact moment a baseball bat meets a water balloon, a powder-filled egg, or a piece of fruit. The artwork is not the swing and not the splatter; it is the frozen frame in between, with the collision diagrammed like a physics paper: shock rings, force vectors, fracture maps, particles suspended mid-flight while time runs at two percent. Three targets hang in a row in a dark studio. Tap one and the bat swings. Drag to aim it first.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The three targets (translucent cyan balloon, powder-white egg, orange fruit) hang from fine strings at mid height and sway almost imperceptibly. The bat pivots at the lower left; its length is solved per target so the barrel genuinely reaches what it swings at, and its cocked angle winds half a radian back from the strike line. A tap on a whole target runs the sequence on real time: a 0.4s accelerating swing, impact, then a three second freeze in which a global timeScale lerps down to 0.02 and eases back. Each target type carries a procedural deformed-impact frame: the balloon stretches into a teardrop along the hit direction, the egg splits into two shell halves inside a radial shard and powder burst, the fruit dimples with a gaussian dent and cracks. Particle sets with seeded velocities are spawned at impact; during the freeze they only creep, each with a thin white velocity whisker, while the diagram draws itself progressively: three expanding shock rings, a main force vector plus four radial vectors, a fracture map per kind (radial cracks for the egg, contour rings for the balloon, dimple arcs for the fruit), and measurement ticks once the diagram matures. On release the particles integrate under gravity for a second, the debris settles on the studio floor as a quiet remnant (deflated skin, shell halves, fruit halves), holds four seconds, and a fresh target of the same kind fades in. After 45 idle seconds the bat demonstrates once on its own, rarely. Palette exactly per spec; diagram lines are white at 70 percent. No text, no UI, no hint. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen, rAF typeof guarded.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three. First pass problems: the bat was a fixed length shorter than its distance to the middle and right targets, so a swing would have stopped visibly short of contact; its cocked pose on the phone frame rotated the barrel off the left edge entirely; and the balloon's translucent fill read too dark. Fixes: bat length is now solved from pivot to aimed target and lerps when the aim changes, the cock-back offset was reduced from 1.02 to 0.5 radians, the pivot moved inward (and to a phone-specific position), the barrel thickens in proportion to its length, and the balloon fill alpha rose from 0.26 to 0.36. After the fix the desktop frame shows the row intact with the bat cocked diagonally across the lower left, the phone frame keeps the whole bat in frame at the left, and the thumb keeps all three targets legible with the bat below them. The freeze, diagram and aftermath states are time-based and cannot appear in a still; they were verified by reading the state machine end to end (swing 0.4s, freeze 3s at timeScale 0.02, aftermath 1.1s, settle 4s, respawn fade 1.2s) and by the particle and diagram code paths sharing the same impact data.
