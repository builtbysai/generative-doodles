# No.330 Fear Hope Field

After Arunima Dazess Wangchuk and her two pole palette, this piece is one continuous surface that runs from fear and warning at one edge to hope and wellbeing at the other. It is drawn as a grid of exactly forty cells across and twenty eight down, one thousand one hundred and twenty cells in all, counted and asserted in the code. Each cell holds a single flat colour taken from a six colour axis: warning red, ember orange, ash grey, undyed wool, wellbeing teal and hope peach. The colour of a cell is a quantised lookup on that axis at the cell scalar value, so neighbouring cells step from one dye lot to the next instead of blending. There is no smooth gradient anywhere in the piece, only flat cells edge to edge. The axis runs diagonally, from the lower left toward the upper right, so neither pole owns the composition and both poles are present in the first frame.

Under the cells is a scalar field held as data over the grid. Slow authored currents move values across the field on a forty eight second seamless cycle. The base value at each cell combines a diagonal term with two travelling waves and two eddies whose centres orbit as the cycle turns, and every time term is an integer multiple of the loop phase, so the field at forty eight seconds matches the field at zero when it has been left untouched. Five current patterns are written out in full in an authored table, each with its own tilt, wave rates and eddy positions. One pattern even runs the axis the other way, so hope gathers where fear sat before. The first frame opens mid drift, with the currents already developed.

Touch writes into the field. Pressing sets the local axis value from the pointer position along the diagonal, with a radial falloff around the point, so the colour resolves there toward the pole that part of the field belongs to. Dragging paints that value continuously across the cells it passes. Painted values hold for a while and then fade slowly back into the drift over roughly half a minute. A double tap advances to the next of the five authored current patterns in fixed order and clears the paint, starting the new current clean. Nothing is randomised at runtime and nothing reloads. All marks are flat filled cells drawn directly on one canvas each frame.
