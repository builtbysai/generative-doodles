# No.321 Scallop Field

After Debbie Sharman's scallop tiles, this piece fills the whole frame with a grid of scalloped cells, eight across and six down, for exactly forty eight cells on a sand ground. Each cell holds exactly one motif, and the two motif sets alternate in a strict checkerboard across the field. One set is the gingko fan, drawn as two lobes fanning from a single stem base with a notch at the top and veins radiating out in deep teal on leopard tan, sitting on a palm green tile. The other set is a tiny daisy with six sand petals around a tan centre with a teal heart, sitting on a terracotta tile. Every tile is a scalloped disc with a deep teal shadow offset behind it, so the field reads as overlapping printed tiles rather than flat squares.

The motion is a thirty second seamless loop. A wave of rotation travels diagonally across the field as a phase bump. As the bump reaches a cell, that cell turns, rising to one hundred and eighty degrees at the peak of the bump and settling back to zero behind it. Because every cell returns to zero and the bump leaves the field before the loop wraps, the frame at thirty seconds matches the frame at zero. The first frame opens with the wave already mid field, so cells near it are caught mid turn.

Tapping a cell flips its base orientation by one hundred and eighty degrees with a soft settle. The wave keeps adding on top of that base, so a flipped cell keeps its new orientation as the wave passes over it and the loop seam still holds. Nothing reloads and nothing is reseeded. All forty eight cells and their single motifs are counted and asserted in the code, and all marks are drawn directly on one canvas each frame from seeded data.
