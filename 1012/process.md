# No.1012 "Your Pose, Drawn"

Study #124 futa-kera, PP173 "Body Mesh" (Perfume Coachella 2019 body-to-mesh).

## Concept

A single large blue wireframe figure dances in a black void. It is never a mirror and never a camera image: it is an abstract articulated rig whose body is posed by springs, so every movement carries weight, lag, overshoot, and settle. Whenever a limb tip (wrists, ankles, head) moves fast, it sheds white hand-drawn scribble strokes that are the tip's true recorded path, fading over about 12 seconds. The mesh itself shows its speed: fast joints brighten from blue toward pale blue and thicken slightly; at rest the figure dims toward deep blue. By default the figure performs a slow seeded idle phrase. A visitor drags to choreograph it, taps to cue a gesture, and double-taps for a new dancer.

## Construction

- Rig: 11 spring joints (spine, neck, head, shoulder/elbow x2, hip/knee x2) plus a sprung root. Bone lengths are seeded per dancer with up to 6 percent jitter. Forward kinematics from the root: spine, neck, head circle, arms from chest, legs from a pelvis tilted at 0.35 of the spine angle.
- Spring model: every joint angle is an independent damped harmonic spring driven at a fixed 120 Hz step (semi-implicit Euler). Stiffness ranges 26 (spine) to 66 (elbows); damping ratios 0.55 to 0.78, all under-damped on purpose, so targets are chased with visible lag and a slight overshoot before settling. The root has its own softer spring (k = 20, zeta 0.8), so the whole body leans and sways with mass. No joint is ever set directly to its target; even IK results only become spring targets.
- Posing: an idle phrase of six seeded keyframe poses loops, with targets blended across each segment by a smoothstep. A tap plays a three-pose gesture (crouch, leap, land) blended over the idle targets on a sine envelope. Dragging grabs the nearest tip chain: two-bone IK (law of cosines) solved against the current FK shoulder/hip point gives shoulder/elbow or hip/knee targets, choosing the lower elbow for arms and the outward knee for legs; the spine target and root target also lean toward the hand, so the whole body follows with weight. Releasing lets the springs settle; the idle phrase clock pauses while dragging and resumes after.
- Scribbles: each of the five tips is tracked every step. Above 0.5 units/s a tip opens a stroke and records its actual positions (seeded jitter up to 0.025 units for the hand-drawn feel); below 0.35 units/s the stroke closes. Strokes render as jittered white polylines at 0.8 alpha with a faint offset second pass, fade linearly over 12 s, and the store is capped (260 strokes / 9000 points, oldest dropped). Every stroke is a true limb-tip history; nothing is decorative.
- Deform: a per-joint speed factor (smoothed absolute angular velocity) mixes the bone color from the activity base toward #9DBCFF and scales line width up to 1.8x. A global activity estimate (mean tip speed) mixes the base between the idle dim #1A3A8F and the figure blue #2E6BFF.
- Virtual performance (frame-one device): no visitor input exists at frame one, so the 16 s pre-roll runs a seeded scripted performance through the same grab/IK/spring path a visitor's drag uses: the right hand sweeps a wide overhead circle (3-7 s), the left hand sweeps low (7-10.5 s), the right hand traces a fast figure-eight (10.5-13.5 s), then the right ankle lifts while the left hand arcs (13.5-16.2 s). Frame one, drawn synchronously after exactly 1920 fixed steps, catches the dancer mid-kick with scribbles trailing its limbs. The frame-one pose is a performance, not a visitor's input.
- Determinism: all state derives from mulberry32 (default seed 1012, overridable by ?seed=) and the fixed 1/120 step. No Date.now, no wall clock in drawn state. Canvas 2D only; no gradients, shadowBlur, filters, or offscreen canvas; grain of the scribbles is stored data, not a filter.
- Viewport: canvas sized from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on both visualViewport and window; canvas CSS size pinned in px every draw. Phone detection via window.screen (min dimension < 700 px) with aspect fallback (h > w x 1.15); phones use a slightly tighter fit scale so the whole figure stays in frame.
- Audio: lazy AudioContext created only inside the first pointer gesture and fully typeof-guarded and try-wrapped: a 52 Hz breathing tone with a slow gain LFO for idle, a short band-passed noise brush when a scribble stroke opens (dry brush), and a soft triangle tick on a tap gesture. Silent in headless QA.

## QA log

- Rendered with render-piece.js at 1280x900 (qa-desktop.png), 390x844 (qa-mobile.png), 720x720 (thumb.png): all render cleanly in silence, no console errors.
- Determinism: thumb.png rendered twice and compared with cmp: byte-identical. Re-verified after the framing fix.
- Framing fix round: the first render placed the world origin (ground level) at screen center, pushing the head and raised hands off the top edge. Fixed by anchoring world y = 1.0 (mid-body) at the vertical anchor point, in both the draw transform and the pointer inverse transform. Re-rendered all sizes.
- Desktop read-back: large blue wireframe figure mid-gesture on black; head circle clear; one arm extended, one folded close; right leg lifted in a bent kick with the thigh segment brightened pale blue (high joint velocity); standing leg straight; big white circular scribble trails from the wrist sweeps at left and the ankle loop at lower right; body visibly leaning into the gesture.
- Thumb read-back: same composition at 720x720, figure whole, scribbles legible.
- Mobile read-back (390x844, phone path via screen size): figure whole and centered, nothing clipped, scribble field intact.
- Deviations: (1) The head chain has no two-bone IK because the head rides the spine itself; grabbing the head instead drives spine/neck lean and nod spring targets toward the pointer, which reads as the same choreography through the springs. (2) Math.random appears once, inside the gesture-gated audio noise buffer only; it never touches drawn or simulated state. (3) Idle targets blend across each full segment rather than holding then blending, so the phrase is in continuous slow motion and the springs always have something to chase.
