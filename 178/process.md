# No.178 Fractal Reptile Garden

## Concept
Fathauer's fractal reptiles: the X pentomino replicated by its own recipe until the tile's boundary itself goes fractal, then set growing across the whole frame as a garden of flat coloured organisms, blue, bone and copper, each one visibly subdivided into five smaller copies of itself, and those into five more. Watch only; the growth is the show.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The iteration is exactly the published recipe: generation 1 is the X pentomino (five unit squares); each generation replaces the figure with five copies of itself scaled by 1/sqrt(5) and rotated by arctan(1/2), about 26.6 degrees, the copies translated one unit in each arm direction plus the centre, giving 25, 125 and 625 squares at generations 2, 3 and 4. The limiting reptile tiles the plane on the index-5 lattice generated by (2,1) and (-1,2), so the frame is stamped with one reptile per lattice point, coloured from the three flat spec colours by lattice parity, all squares drawn grouped by colour as single filled paths, then the seams drawn in the ground colour #0C0C0E as a hierarchy: every generation-4 square carries its copy-index chain from the iteration, each shared edge is classified by the coarsest chunk boundary it lies on, and the levels are stroked weakest first, hairline between sibling squares inside one X, then heavier across X boundaries, heavier again across 25-square chunks and 125-square sub-reptiles, heaviest around each whole reptile. Coverage was verified numerically during the build: sampling the generation-4 construction over a test window gives full coverage with no gaps and no overlaps. The 48 second loop draws generation 1, then 2, 3 and 4 in six second stages (the first generation's reptiles stagger in radially), fades the seams in over the next six seconds, holds ten, then soft resets over eight; the first frame is offset to 32 seconds, generation 4 with seams in, mid hold. One deliberate deviation, forced by the mathematics: the prompt suggests mirror variants, but the attractor is chiral, its mirror image tiles a different lattice, and mixing mirrors onto this lattice provably opens cross shaped gaps (measured at about 91 percent coverage); rotation variants are exact symmetries of the attractor and so invisible. The shipped piece keeps the single chirality that tiles exactly, and takes its variety from the three colours, which keeps the prompt's own hard rule: no gaps, no overlaps, or the piece fails. Draws are change gated by a quantized time signature so a static hold costs nothing. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, watch only so no listeners and no hint at all, rAF typeof guarded.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three. The first desktop render, built with alternating mirrored reptiles, showed black X shaped voids between organisms; a numerical coverage check isolated the cause (mirrored copies belong to a different translation lattice), the mirror mixing was removed, and coverage re measured at 100 percent before re rendering. The final desktop frame is a continuous mosaic: large fractal edged regions in the three colours interlock edge to edge, the stepped self similar boundaries readable at two scales, seams uniform. The phone frame shows the same gapless field running off all four edges with two to three reptiles across. Honest trait: at generation 4 the finest boundary steps read as a fine stair; that is the construction's own texture at this depth, and the loop's earlier generations make the subdivision legible rather than hiding it.
Follow-up fix, same day: in the generation-4 hold the uniform 1.4px seams left the self-replication illegible at rest, the reptiles reading as flat pixel blobs under one even grid. The seam pass is now hierarchical, as described under Construction. One false start on the way: classifying by ancestor square indices put nearly every edge at the coarsest level, because chunk membership lives in the iteration's copy indices, not in parentage; a numerical census of the corrected classifier gives the expected counts per level (6,500 unit seams at desktop size, exactly the four internal edges of each of the 125 X's per reptile). Re rendered all three sizes and inspected: hairlines inside each X, medium seams stepping around the X groups and 25-square chunks, heavy outlines tracing the sub-reptiles and whole reptiles, so the nesting reads at a glance on desktop, phone and thumb alike. Palette, construction, timing, first frame and the watch-only rule are unchanged.
