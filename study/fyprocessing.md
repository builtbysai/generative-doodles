## What is known from secondary sources

fyprocessing is a reblog-style aggregator blog, "For Your Processing", run by
Justin Lincoln. It collects GIFs and videos of animations built in Processing
and reposts them with source links. Creative coder Tim Rodenbroeker calls it
one of the most important addresses of the old Tumblr creative coding scene,
alongside p5art.tumblr.com: a feed with no algorithm, just a chronological
stream of other people's sketches. As a study source it matters less for any
single technique and more as a historical firehose of what the Processing
community was actually making.

Two posts surfaced through a mirror (procedural-generation.isaackarth.com),
enough to show the blog's range:

- "Procedural Ink" (via fyprocessing, source on openprocessing.org/sketch/117624
  by Ale Gonzalez): a procedural drawing tool that takes a photo as input,
  analyzes it to detect a vector field, and generates curving lines along that
  field. Image-driven flow fields, in other words: the picture becomes the
  wind. Gonzalez has a large body of similar generative technique sketches.
- "Skyline" (via fyprocessing, a generative music video by Raven Kwok): the
  entire video is programmed in Processing. One core principle is Voronoi
  tessellation, with seeds sorted into agent types that follow behaviors and
  appearance transformations, driven by the song's audio spectrum in custom
  layouts, plus an animated sequence of the vocalist. Audio-reactive generative
  video, built from one geometric primitive (Voronoi) plus one driver (the
  audio spectrum).
