# No.204 Heat Gun

## Concept
Ward builds her creatures from one hundred percent recycled PETG sheet, welded together and shaped with a common heat gun, and the transparency of the material is the creature's wateriness. Heat Gun is a small brine-shrimp-like animal made of nothing else: a clear membrane at forty five percent alpha stretched over a darker inner body, bright weld seams along the outline and across every segment joint, the anatomy readable through the skin, segmented tail, paddling leg pairs, eyestalks. It drifts as if suspended, swaying on a twenty five second loop. Your drag is the heat gun: wherever you press and pull, the membrane softens toward your finger, bulging out along its normals, and when you let go it cools back to shape over about eight seconds. The inner body is stiffer and springs back faster, so for a while the skin hangs stretched over the anatomy, which is exactly what a heat gun does to a sheet. Double tap deals a different creature from the same sheet: a shrimp, a round tardigrade-ish blob, or a long leggy one.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Each shape is parametric data, a curved spine with a nine-station width profile, leg count, and flags for eyestalks and tail fan; the outline is generated as spine points offset along their normals. Deformation runs on a low resolution heat grid in local space: dragging adds gaussian heat, the grid decays exponentially with a time constant chosen so most of the heat is gone in eight seconds, and each membrane vertex carries a bulge value that springs toward the sampled heat, fast while heating, slow while cooling. The inner body is the same outline pulled toward the spine, a true inset, displaced by only forty percent of the bulge on a faster spring. Legs are strokes from the underside stations with phase-lagged paddle oscillation; the fan, where the shape has one, is two flat lobes off the tail base; eyestalks are stroked stalks with bead tips. Everything is drawn directly each frame, no offscreen canvas. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen, creature centred and angled about twenty degrees, spanning roughly three quarters of the width, rAF typeof guarded, no text, no bubbles, no props.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. Round one faults: the inner body was scaled toward the coordinate origin rather than inset from the outline, so the anatomy sat displaced toward the tail and poked oddly at the head; and the tail fan, built from two quadratic fills with a pixel-space control offset, rendered as two faint stray wisps detached from the body. Round two rebuilt the inner outline as a spine-relative inset and the fan as two flat lobes in leg teal, and the creature now reads as one welded hull: membrane, seams, segment bands, legs and eyestalks all in register at every size, the shrimp curled at its twenty degree drift angle with drift room around it. The heat response itself cannot fire in the still renderer, which has no pointer events; it was verified by reading the deformation path end to end, grid, decay and both spring rates.
