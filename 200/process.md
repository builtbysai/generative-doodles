# No.200 Crush

## Concept
Crush is Willshire's twelve foot man for Oskar Blues' Can'd Aid, built entirely from bike parts and steel, and he is not a statue: his torso is an open hopper, a working recycling receptacle, art that takes your trash in. The piece keeps him on duty. Tap anywhere and an aluminium can arcs from your finger into the hopper mouth. Cans rest visibly in the hopper, and every fifth one fires the crush: chainring hips spin, spoke ribs fold inward, the whole torso squashes toward the hip line with a frame shake, the cans burst into aluminium, red and blue flecks, and he re-expands, empty and ready. Drag his arm and the arm swings to aim, lobs coming off the next throw higher or flatter. His face is a headlamp bracket. He has no other face, and he sways one pixel on a seven second cycle, breathing at the hopper rim, while he waits.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page, every part drawn directly each frame (no offscreen sprites). The figure is assembled from part glyphs in H-relative units: chainrings (toothed circles with a grease-black hub and five glinting bolts) serve as shoulders, hips and knees; the spine is a dashed chain curve; the arms are two-segment handlebar tubes ending in grip curls; the feet are crank arms and flat pedals. The hopper is a grease-black trapezoid with a steel rim, five spoke ribs curving down its front, and an elliptical black mouth whose rim breathes at three percent. Resting cans are drawn in the mouth at seeded rest spots, aluminium with a red or blue band. A toss is a parametric arc from the tap point to the mouth, flight time and arc height biased by the aim value, the can rotating as it flies; landing increments the count, and the fifth landing starts the crush clock, from which squash factor, hip spin, rib fold and shake are all derived. Flecks are simple gravity particles that fade inside a second, so crushed cans vanish instead of piling up. Arm drags are detected near the hands and shoulders and rotate that arm on an eased spring; taps are gated on small movement and short duration so a drag can never fire a toss. Frame one has the count pre-seeded at two, cans resting in the mouth, the next crush three cans away. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen, rAF typeof guarded, no text.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three, through two rounds. Round one fault: the right arm's droop was signed opposite to the left, so its hand solved inside the torso and the arm appeared to cross the hopper and end at a dot on the ribs; the aim mapping also ran backwards, a raised arm flattening the lob. Round two unified the droop convention (outward positive on both sides), dropped the hands lower, and corrected the aim sign. Final frames: the man stands symmetrical and legible, chainrings at every joint, hopper open with two banded cans resting in the mouth, headlamp bracket floating on its dashed chain neck, pedal feet planted, the phone frame keeping the full figure with the hopper wide.
