# No.227 Crash Stripes

## Concept
After Matthew Kluber's 2006 Untitled (green), the origin piece of his stripe paintings: thin horizontal bands of lime green and charcoal on a near-white ground, with a column of narrower stripes at the far left quoting the 2006 work. The composition is frozen crash data, carefully edited, flat and hard-edged. One lime band, off-centre, carries the crash: on its own seeded schedule it stutters, doubles, flashes cyan for a beat, then settles, and nine to fourteen seconds later it fires again. Nothing about it is clickable. The viewer watches the crash re-happen, which is Kluber's subject exactly: a still painting with one programmed fault in it.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The band layout is precomputed once from a seeded PRNG (mulberry32) in a design space three hundred units tall: each coloured band gets a hairline of bare ground after it, so stripes stay individual even when the sequence repeats a colour, and wider ground gaps open breathing spaces. Band heights run three to fourteen units, scaled to the panel. The glitch band is chosen at build time: the taller lime band nearest fifty eight percent of the panel height. Glitch events are precomputed on a seeded schedule, every nine to fourteen seconds, lasting 0.3 to 0.6 seconds; during an event the band is redrawn with two offset duplicates whose jitter is quantised into 55ms steps, plus a cyan overlay, all under a sine envelope that eases the fault in and out. The panel is a 2:1 rectangle centred on the piece's own ground colour at every size. Frame one opens on the settled field with the glitch at rest, three seconds before the first scheduled firing would even be due. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, the seed fixed so the piece never re-randomises on resize, rAF typeof guarded, no text, no handlers at all.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The first layout let long runs of one colour merge into solid slabs, more poster than stripe field; the generator was rebuilt so every coloured band sits on a ground hairline, and the second render showed the intended field of individual thin stripes at all sizes. The glitch state itself was verified with a temporary clock-shifted copy (not shipped): at the midpoint of the first scheduled event, exactly one band stands flashed cyan over the settled field, everything else untouched. The phone frame keeps the 2:1 panel centred on the near-white ground, the left column still legible.
