# No.1054 "One Centimetre"

Study #129 katsuhiko-harada, PP215 "The Centimetre Stage" (border 2015: control accuracy of one centimetre or less on a 9 x 15 m stage).

## Concept

Precision made visible. A dark stage is ruled edge to edge by a fine measurement grid: minor lines every unit, stronger lines every ten. The grid is not a backdrop; it is the subject, the measure against which everything is judged. Four small machines (two cubes, two carts) travel exact closed waypoint paths drawn faintly over the grid. Around each machine's intended position sits a tolerance circle, and the machine's true position never leaves it: each machine carries a small, slow, seeded deviation wobble, and the wobble is bounded under one centimetre by construction and by proof. The beauty is in how little deviates.

The hand can disturb the proof. Dragging pushes a machine off its path; its circle flashes amber, and the machine works back at a steady constant rate until it is inside tolerance again. The recovery is the content. Tapping a machine shows its live deviation in tiny mono type for four seconds: the only text in the piece, and the prompt's own readout. Double-tap reseeds the measure.

## Construction

- Scale. The stage is 150 x 90 units and represents 15 m across its width, so 1 unit = 10 cm and one centimetre = 0.1 units exactly. The tolerance radius enforced by the piece is 0.1 units.
- Grid. Drawn as two batched stroke passes per frame over the stage rect: minors at #2A2E35, alpha 0.35; majors every 10 units at #3A4048, alpha 0.5. The stage is contain-fitted and centred so grid cells stay square on every viewport; the page ground is the stage tone #07080A, so the letterbox is invisible.
- Paths. Four hand-placed waypoint sets (a rounded rectangle north-west, a broad ellipse centre, a rounded rectangle south-east, a tilted ellipse north-east), jittered per seed, closed Catmull-Rom splines with arc-length tables. Machines travel at constant speed; lap durations are 55 s and 27.5 s, both dividing the 55 s loop, so the piece is seamless and every position is a closed-form function of piece time. Waypoint marks dot the plan at #FFFFFF, alpha 0.35; the plan itself is #FFFFFF at 0.25.
- Deviation. Each machine's actual position is its intended spline point plus a wobble: two sinusoids (one per axis) with whole-cycle counts per loop (3 to 9 cycles), seeded phases, and amplitudes drawn from a dedicated per-machine RNG stream in a fixed call order (ax, ay, kx, ky, px, py). Amplitudes are capped at 0.045 units per axis.
- The proof. The wobble bound is analytic: |dev| is at most sqrt(ax^2 + ay^2), at most sqrt(2) x 0.045 = 0.0636 units = 0.636 cm for any seed. It is also measured: a probe replicating the shipped RNG stream exactly sampled all four machines at 22,000 points across the loop for the shipped seed (1054215). Sampled maxima: machine 0: 0.0607 units = 0.607 cm; machine 1: 0.0594 = 0.594 cm; machine 2: 0.0582 = 0.582 cm; machine 3: 0.0499 = 0.499 cm. Worst case 0.607 cm against the 1.000 cm tolerance: the promise holds with 0.393 cm to spare.
- Push and recovery. A drag grabs the nearest machine within 16 units; its offset follows the finger, clamped to 9 units. On release the offset is stored as an event and decays linearly at 2.5 units per second, a steady working-back along a straight line, as a pure function of (event, t). While a machine's total error (wobble plus push offset) exceeds the true 0.1-unit tolerance, its circle flashes amber and the deviation tone sounds. Frame one is pre-input: no events exist, so the gate frame is the pure closed-form state at t = 16 s.
- Readout. Tap (not drag) near a machine shows its live total deviation, formatted like "0.4 cm", in tiny monospace #FFE9B8 above the machine, fading over the last 1.2 s of a 4 s window. It is the piece's only visible text besides the title, per the prompt.
- Amber substitution. The prompt asks for an amber flash; the flash and the readout use the house amber #FFE9B8, the warm tone already established across this study run, rather than introducing a second amber.
- Sound. Created lazily inside the first pointer gesture, typeof-guarded throughout: a faint high shimmer (3900 Hz and 5240 Hz sines at very low gain) for the grid; a soft precise tick (1760 Hz, throttled) as each machine crosses a waypoint; a quiet warm tone (183 Hz) whose gain follows whether any machine is outside tolerance. No microphone. Silent and complete in the QA renderer, which has no AudioContext.
- House rules. Canvas sized from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on visualViewport and window; canvas style width and height pinned in px inside draw(); phone detection via window.screen minimum dimension under 700 px with the aspect fallback only; no gradients, no shadowBlur, no ctx.filter, no offscreen canvas, no Date.now anywhere: the live clock is a fixed-step counter (1/120 s) starting at step 1920, which is t = 16 s exactly. The rAF loop is typeof-guarded and step-capped.

## Deviations

1. The tolerance ring is drawn at 11x magnification (radius 1.1 units) instead of the true 0.1 units. Reason: at the piece's true scale, one centimetre is sub-pixel at every supported size (0.85 px desktop, 0.26 px phone), and fix rounds 1 and 2 proved an exactly-scaled ring renders as an invisible smudge, which breaks the prompt's own rule that the circle must be visible and the machine seen inside it. Everything that constitutes the promise stays at true scale: the enforced bound, the numeric proof above, the amber flash condition, and the tap readout all use the real 0.1 units = 1 cm. The drawn ring is the promise's portrait, centred on the intended point exactly as specced; the machine's centre rides inside the true centimetre around that same centre.
2. Phone presentation: machine bodies are drawn 1.3x larger on phone-sized screens so the instruments stay legible against the fine grid at 390 px wide. Positions, grid and tolerances are unchanged.
3. The readout reports total deviation (wobble plus any push offset still recovering), not wobble alone: after a push, the number is the machine's real distance from its intended point, which is the truth the prompt wants in numbers.
