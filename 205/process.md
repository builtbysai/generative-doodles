# No.205 Tun State

## Concept
Ward's Messengers treat tardigrades as time capsules, and the animal's own trick is the piece: the tun state, the ability to press pause when things get tough. This water bear ambles in muted amber on a low horizon, plump, segmented, eight clawed legs working in sequence, blunt snout forward. The mechanic is idleness. Leave it untouched for twenty seconds and it dehydrates over six seconds, legs folding in, body wrinkling and collapsing inward and down in colour from amber to dark umber, until what remains is the tun, a small dark blob on the horizon. It is not dead. Every seven seconds the blob gives a single faint pulse, a slight swell, still alive, waiting. A tap is rain: over four seconds the blob unfolds, the legs reach back out, the amber plumps full, and the amble resumes until the next long silence.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The animal is parametric: a spine curve, a plumpness profile thin at the rear and fat through the middle, the body drawn as sixteen overlapping segment ellipses alternating the two amber shades, four leg pairs drawn as two-segment strokes from their spine stations with a gait phase per pair and three small pale claw strokes at each foot, and a snout ellipse with a dark mouth ring. Everything hangs on one hydration parameter h. It drives the colour ramp from #C8A24B down to the blob's #5C4A2E, the spine length from full down to thirty percent, the body plumpness, the leg extension from reaching to stubs, the claw visibility, and the wrinkle creases, dark arcs across the segments whose strength peaks late in the drying. h eases toward its target at one sixth per second going down and one quarter per second coming back up, which gives the six second collapse and four second bloom. The idle timer is the input: any pointer down counts as rain and resets it; twenty untouched seconds set the target to zero. In the tun, a gaussian bump on a seven second cycle scales the blob by up to five percent, the pulse. Frame one opens hydrated and mid-amble, legs mid-sequence. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen, rAF typeof guarded, no text, no face beyond the snout's mouth ring.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The hydrated frame reads immediately: a fat amber segmented water bear facing right, alternating segment shades, clawed feet planted on the horizon line, snout and mouth ring clear, ground shadow under the body. Round one had the animal too small for the prompt's monumental micro-fauna presence; round two raised the scale so the body spans about two thirds of the desktop width and the creature stands at a good height against its horizon, with the mobile frame keeping the whole animal in view. The dried state was checked by reading the h-driven geometry at zero: spine at thirty percent, legs at stub length, wrinkles at full strength, pulse term active, the blob sitting on the same horizon so the collapse reads as the same animal, paused.
