# No.854 "Ventriloquist's Violin"

EXPANSION #104 laurie-anderson, PP15 "The Dummy": the self-playing violin of 1975, a violin with a loudspeaker inside it, playing prerecorded music while she plays live. A duet with herself; she called it a ventriloquist's dummy.

## Concept

Two violin forms face each other across a black stage. On the left, the machine: warm wood, a speaker cone where the body's heart should be, playing a fixed looping phrase entirely on its own, forever. On the right, the user's violin in chalk white, silent until bowed. The machine never stops, never waits, never accompanies; it is a duet of equals. The user bows the white violin by dragging across it: stroke position sets pitch, stroke speed sets swell. Pitches snap to the machine's current key, so the user can follow it, until a hard stroke breaks the quantization and the line detunes continuously: the clash. Every 16 loops the machine shifts key and the ground moves under the user's fingers. A tap makes the machine answer: it quotes the user's last bowed phrase back, transposed, as a one-off insertion, then resumes its loop exactly where it left off. A double-tap reseeds the machine's phrase. A row of sixteen amber dots above the machine violin's top edge tracks the phrase position; the cone pulses on every machine note and sheds faint expanding rings.

## Construction

Single self-contained index.html, canvas 2D, strict five-tone palette (wood #7A4A28, cone #3A3A3A, amber #C98A2B, chalk #F0EDE4, black #0A0A0A). No text anywhere except the document title. No gradients, no shadowBlur, no filter, no offscreen canvas; glows and fades are flat-alpha strokes and fills.

Violins: one shared silhouette, sampled from a two-ellipse bout profile (upper and lower bouts as overlapping ellipse widths) with a Gaussian pinch at the waist for the C-bout figure, plus neck, scroll, fingerboard, two strings, tailpiece and f-hole hints. The machine is filled wood with the speaker cone (dark disc, black core, amber rim whose alpha and radius ride the pulse envelope) at body center; the user's is a chalk outline over a faint chalk wash. Both tilt a few degrees toward each other. Phrase position is shown as the amber dot row in the machine's tilted frame, resting steps as hollow dots, the current step fully lit.

Phrase and key model: the machine phrase is 16 steps (scale degrees in natural minor, base 196 Hz, rests included) generated by mulberry32 seeded per epoch (base seed 85415, reseed on double-tap increments the epoch). Step duration is a fixed 0.42 s; the loop is 6.72 s. Key is a closed-form function of loop count: every 16 loops the transposition advances through a fixed interval cycle (+2, -3, +4, -2, +5, -4 semitones, prefix-summed), and both the machine's frequencies and the user's quantization base follow it. Cone pulse and rings are computed from phrase onsets in the machine-time domain: machine time equals clock time minus the total duration of any quotes that have fired, frozen during a quote, so the phrase clock genuinely pauses for an insertion and resumes after it.

Quote-back mechanic: while the user bows gently (non-clash strokes), quantized degree changes are recorded as notes with durations; the last completed stroke (up to 16 notes) is the user's phrase. A tap (short, nearly still press) is held 360 ms so a double-tap can cancel it into a reseed; then the quote fires: the phrase is transposed up two scale degrees, its total length clamped into 1 to 5 s, and it is inserted at the next phrase-step boundary. The machine's audio scheduler plays the quote notes instead of phrase steps for its duration; the cone pulses from the quote onsets while the dot row holds on the frozen step. If the user has not bowed anything yet, the dummy quotes the machine's own previous four sounding steps, transposed the same way.

User voice: drag samples are recorded events (time, position, speed, clash flag). Pitch is a closed-form function of the latest sample: vertical position along the body maps to scale degrees 0 to 14, rounded to the machine's current key when the stroke is gentle, continuous (interpolated semitones) when the sample's speed exceeds the clash threshold (1.1 times the smaller viewport dimension per second). The bow trail draws the last 2 s of samples, chalk for gentle strokes, amber for clash samples, with a bow stick drawn across the newest sample perpendicular to its motion.

Audio: created lazily inside the first pointerdown, all AudioContext use typeof-guarded and wrapped in try/catch. Machine notes are two detuned saws through a lowpass (a filtered, woodwind-ish reed) scheduled a step ahead from the same step clock that drives the visuals; the user voice is one persistent filtered saw whose frequency and gain are eased toward the bow-derived targets each frame. A compressor acts as limiter; master gain is modest. The piece renders complete and silent where no AudioContext exists: the machine still plays itself visually, the cone still pulses, the trails still fade.

Sizing: canvas follows the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on both visualViewport and window; canvas style width and height are pinned in px inside draw(). Phone detection uses window.screen physical size (min dimension under 700 px), aspect h > w*1.15 as fallback only. Both violins scale from one length unit so they always fit side by side.

## Deviations

- Quote transposition is fixed at two scale degrees up (the brief says "transposed" without an interval); two steps keeps the quote inside the user's own register family while clearly not being a copy.
- The clash threshold is a concrete speed value (pointer speed above 1.1 viewport-minimums per second); the brief's "drags hard (fast/long)" is realized as speed only, since a long slow stroke is still a bow stroke, while a fast one audibly breaks pitch discipline.
- Phrase position is shown as the amber dot row in the machine violin's own tilted frame above its scroll (the brief's "along the top edge" option), rather than on the body itself, so the dots never fight the cone rings for the same space.
- Audio begins on the first pointer gesture, per house rules, though the machine plays visually from load; the brief's "sound starts on the first pointer gesture only" sanctions exactly this split.
