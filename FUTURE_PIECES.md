# Future Pieces — private sketchbook (NEVER push)

Concepts seeded from study. Each needs its own concept statement and success
criteria before building. Rule: study-informed, never copied. Render and
visually inspect before shipping. Numbered by days since 2002-01-10 (that date = day 000; e.g. 2026-09-22 = day 9020).

## Seeds (from 2026-09-18 study)
1. **Contour Fields** — scalar-field contours via marching squares (Gorillasun
   technique) in monochrome ink (Hoff discipline). Plotter-friendly.
   Success: feels topographic, not mechanical; density carries tone.
2. **Grain Study** — analogue texture emulation (DesLauriers method): layered
   grain over one simple geometric system, limited palette.
   Success: looks printed, not digital.
3. **Three Shapes** — circle/square/triangle composition game (Generative
   Artistry's Un Deux Trois as vocabulary, not copy): my own placement rules.
   Success: playful but composed, every seed works.
4. **Growth** — differential line growth (Hoff) in near-monochrome, plotter-ready.
   Success: organic, holds together as a composition, never repeats.
5. **Field Notes** — flow field done right (Hobbs lessons): extended margins,
   turbulence octaves, weighted rarity, restrained palette.
   Success: avoids every avoid-list trap; 100 outputs stay interesting.
6. **Subdivide** — irregular recursive subdivision (Gorillasun) with texture fills.
   Success: adjacent to Mondrian in method only; clearly its own voice.
7. **Accumulation** — sandpaint-style dense translucent points forming an emergent
   image (Hoff). Success: the image emerges; never reads as noise.
8. **Word Swarm** — text as particle target, click to regenerate (DonKarlssonSan).
   Interactive. Success: legible, lively, performant on phones.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-23 Raven Kwok study)
9. **Congregation** — audio-reactive voronoi agent instrument (Skyline #1
   system, not its look): seeds typed as drifters (seek dark regions of a
   driver field) and orbiters (circle to synthetic audio bands); kick events
   make seeds converge, flash, disperse. Phosphor-on-dark. Click = trigger a
   congregation; drag = stir. Animated.
   Success: the kick reads as an event, not a pulse; the web never sits
   still; no two minutes look alike.
10. **Tone Mosaic** — voronoi as tone renderer (Skyline press-still stage):
    importance-sampled seeds from a procedurally generated driver image per
    click, cell size encodes tone, nuclei drawn, strict B/W print feel.
    Still image per seed.
    Success: the driver image is legible purely through cell density;
    reads as etching, not stipple noise.
11. **Corridor** — 189D0 system, not its frames: quadtree where each leaf
    solves a geometric intersection motif (rings, circle-in-square, rosette,
    dot grid), then the flat composition is camera-projected through an
    endless corridor flythrough. Animated forward flight; click reseeds.
    Success: the tunnel projection makes the flat index read as
    architecture; clearly distinct from 9021 Subdivide (flat, texture
    fills, no camera).

## Seeds (from 2026-09-23 Robert Hodgin study)
12. **Self-Planned Town** — his road-growth algorithm as the whole piece:
    growth vectors from scattered points, stop-on-contact, slow rotation,
    mix curving/non-curving vectors, line weight proportional to length, over
    a faint plot grid; antique town-plan styling with a name and "surveyed"
    date per click. Still image per seed.
    Success: streets feel grown, not drawn; no two towns share a skeleton.
13. **Never Isles** — procedural island coastlines (radial noise + erosion
    passes) charted as an antique maritime plate, with his suffix-cropped
    GNIS name recombination labeling every bay, peak, and settlement.
    Success: the names make you believe the islands are real.
14. **Nine Hundred Years** — the meander engine as animation: the map draws
    itself year by year, courses accumulating live, oxbows pinching off in
    front of you. Animated.
    Success: the cutoff moment reads as an event; the finished frame matches
    an Old Courses still in quality.
15. **Feedback Engine** — a single-buffer video-synth feedback loop as a
    still-image generator: value noise re-sampled each frame at coordinates
    bent by the previous frame, run until it settles into flowing grain,
    then frozen and colorized in ONE restrained palette (no default rainbow).
    Animated settle reads as an event; final frame is a finished piece.
    Success: the smear structure feels analog, not digital.
16. **Modulate Study** — hydra's modulate-as-displacement-map idea in a
    seeded still: concentric rings whose sampling coordinates are pushed by
    a per-pixel modulator field remapped to [-1, 1] so the pushes go in all
    directions, not just left-up. One hue, geometry as signal.
    Success: the rings look wind-bent, the push field is legible.
17. **Kaleid 99** — the live-coding "99 is a magic number" trick as a
    print-like piece: pixelated thresholded oscillator bands through a
    99-segment kaleidoscope, monochrome with one accent color, tight grain.
    Success: reads as op-art print, not psychedelic wallpaper.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-23 Vera Molnar study)
18. **Storm Grid** — her (Des)Ordres recipe as a piece: grid of nested
    squares, disorder as rare localized events. A fixed disorder budget
    (1-3%) spent on 2-4 storm cells with heavy corner jitter, extra rings,
    darker ink; the rest near-perfect with micro-tremble. Click re-plots
    with a fresh seed. Seeded still.
    Success: the storm cells read as accidents the composition survives,
    not as noise.
19. **Interruptions** — dense field of short rotated dashes, then erasure as
    the drawing tool: voids carved in shaped regions (circle, letter,
    diagonal band) that read as the figure. Animated: the field draws
    first, then the voids eat through it. Click picks a new hidden shape.
    Success: the void reads as a figure, not a hole.
20. **Almost Perfect** — her Hypertransformation recipe: one continuous
    pen path drawing nested squares whose rotation drifts as the path
    goes, never lifting the pen; the twist is the strain of order
    resisting itself. Warm paper, black ink, constant stroke weight.
    Success: Pettis's test, the piece tells the story of almost perfect.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-24 meodai study)
21. **Palette Walk** — the poline journey as the artwork itself: anchors
    plotted on a native hue wheel (angle = hue, radius = lightness), the
    eased path between them drawn as a ribbon through color space with
    sampled dots, palette strip below as legend. Interactive: drag anchors,
    switch per-axis easings (sinusoidal, quadratic, arc), watch the path
    bend and the palette re-resolve. The map of the palette is the piece.
    Success: the ribbon reads as a journey with mood changes, not a chord.
22. **Hour Hands** — Harmony Clock lineage, different engine: concentric
    time rings (seconds, minutes, hours from the center out) where each
    ring segment samples an anchor-journey palette at its own position;
    fresh anchor pairs minted every minute. The clock stays legible while
    color does the composing. Seeded still with one live mode.
    Success: readable as a clock in every palette, no rainbow churn.
23. **Tennis Ball** — ray-color lineage: a tiny raytraced room (one sphere,
    a five-sided box, three colored lights) rendered at low resolution; the
    palette for the companion composition is sampled off the sphere's
    surface, so every color is the same light in the same room. Change the
    lights, the whole piece re-resolves coherently.
    Success: palettes that feel lit, not picked; light change is the only
    control.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-24 Anatoly Zenkov study)
24. **Idle Dots** — IOGraph lineage, synthetic input: a simulated workday of
    cursor behavior (reading pauses, scanning bursts, the afternoon scroll)
    rendered through his exact recipe, 0.45px lines, idle radius accumulating
    per tick, dots at 2*sqrt(radius), direction-to-hue in a restrained
    two-color loop instead of his CMY. The behavior grammar is real even
    though the day is invented.
    Success: reads as a genuine work artifact, dots mark attention like a
    heat map.
25. **Chord and Arc** — ColorPicker lineage, the traversal mode as the piece:
    one anchor pair, one count, two palettes, LINEAR on the left, ARC on the
    right, each rendered as soft circles sized by the sample distribution.
    Same sky, different weather. Static diptych, no interaction.
    Success: the two halves are unmistakably kin and unmistakably different.
26. **Still Life Engine** — Parametric Pottery lineage in 2D: seeded vessel
    still lifes drawn flat, composition algorithm with plinths, bezier
    vessel profiles, long shadows, two palette modes (vibrant generative vs
    natural preset), one 16-character key per piece, the artist's curation
    discipline applied to the output stream.
    Success: every seed balanced, no two share a horizon, one accent vessel
    per scene.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-24 FarbVelo study)
27. **Hue Bingo Canvas** — FarbVelo lineage: each load rolls a real Hue Bingo
    palette (base hue, 60-degree slots, dark anchor, rising mids, quiet
    bright end, perceptual interpolation) and paints one composition with
    it, concentric irregular rings where each ring takes the next band color.
    The palette is the seed, the rings are the score.
    Success: every roll coherent by construction, no roll needs curation.
28. **Folded Light** — the arrangement fold as the piece: one palette,
    three panels, default / darkCenter / lightCenter orderings, each panel
    a simple vertical gradient stack. Same colors, three different moods.
    Static triptych proving order is a compositional parameter.
    Success: the three panels read as three different palettes.
29. **Glow Only** — FarbVelo's "Show Glow" background promoted to the whole
    piece: no bands, no swatches, just the huge soft radial wash of a
    generated palette on white, slowly crossfading between rolls. Ambient
    color field, meditative.
    Success: holds a wall like a Rothko, never resolves into a screensaver.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-24 Bjorn Ottosson study)
30. **Orthogonal Sweep** — the constant-lightness hue sweep as the artwork:
    long horizontal bands, one per lightness level, hue 0 to 360 in OKLCH,
    slowly drifting. The evenness is the aesthetic, a rainbow that refuses
    to shout in yellow or mumble in blue.
    Success: no banding, no hot spots, hypnotic rather than loud.
31. **Two Lightesses** — the HSL failure as diptych: the same geometric
    composition (nested arcs or a grid of discs) rendered twice, once with
    an HSL hue ramp, once with the same ramp in OKLCH. Side by side the
    artifact becomes the subject.
    Success: viewers can point to exactly where HSL lies.
32. **Cusp Walker** — sample the sRGB gamut cusp (max chroma per hue, his
    clipping method) and walk it as a closed curve: a ring of the most vivid
    color each hue can be, plotted as dots on black, with the hue number
    that "should" be evenly spaced but is not. The gamut's true shape as
    jewelry.
    Success: the ring's lumps and pinches are visible and beautiful.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-24 pro-color-harmonies study)
33. **Mud Map** — draw the OKLCH hue wheel with the three named mud zones
    (brown-olive, sick-green, corpse-cyan) as labeled dead regions, then
    scatter a few hundred random colors and draw each one's escape: a
    short arc to the nearest zone edge with a chroma boost. The palette
    library's ugliest rule as a diagram, and it should be gorgeous.
    Success: the escapes read as a migration, not noise.
34. **Spiral Zap** — take a quiet 6-color triadic palette and apply the
    zap (spiral) modifier at increasing intensity across a horizontal
    sequence of panels, 0 to 1 in ten steps. One calm row becoming one
    feral row, the moment it tips visible.
    Success: the tipping point panel is obvious without being told.
35. **Threshold Blend** — the boundary-blend trick as subject: pick a
    base color and drag its lightness across the dark/mid/light threshold
    while a strip chart records the palette's chroma narrative pattern
    over time, showing no snap, only glide. Render as a static poster of
    the strip chart.
    Success: the chart is smooth and the poster stands on its own.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-24 Elastiq AutoAlbers study)
36. **Fold Ribbon** — the AutoAlbers fold as a piece of its own: a stack of
    OKLCH strips (60-degree minimum hue gap, shuffled order, lightness ramp)
    that folds from flat bands into a single ribbon monolith over one slow
    progress scalar, per-strip noise offsets keeping the fold
    unsynchronized, bezier connectors with a gradient across each join so
    the eye reads ribbon, not bars. Drag to scrub the fold by hand.
    Success: the midpoint reads as fabric, and the monolith state holds
    for a long dwell before the reset.
37. **Living Homage** — Josef Albers' nested squares, but temporal: three or
    four concentric squares whose colors come from an evolving palette and
    whose sizes breathe slowly out of phase, so the color relativity of the
    inner square against its ground keeps shifting. One composition, no
    cuts, the interaction is the piece.
    Success: ten minutes of watching still shows new relationships.
38. **Monolith Dwell** — a slideshow of folded-ribbon compositions where the
    only parameter that changes is how long each monolith state is held:
    2 seconds, 10 seconds, a minute. A study of pacing as a compositional
    material, with the palette held constant across all three so time is
    the only variable.
    Success: the long-dwell version feels like a different artwork.

136. **Salient Palette** : the Paul orientation constraint, derived not
    copied. Any input image is first reduced through a pyramid to a
    coarse top level, and all contour work is built from exactly 4
    allowed angles (8 only at fine detail), with tone from 5 discrete
    scribble-density budgets. Success: the same scene run through an
    unconstrained renderer looks generic; the 4-angle version has a
    recognizable hand, and a viewer can name the constraint from the
    linework alone.
137. **One Photo, Forty Minutes** : the eye-theater mechanic, honestly
    framed. The image is computed from a single capture, but the piece
    performs the slow physical ritual anyway: studying pauses, motor
    sounds, a wobbling pen that sometimes misses. Success: viewers who
    watch the ritual value the artifact more (the Tresset finding), and
    the piece never claims the ritual is the mechanism; the honesty is
    part of the work.
138. **Budget Ink** : shading as an ink budget. Each tone region gets a
    path-length budget proportional to darkness times area, spent by a
    single wandering stroke per region that must stay mostly inside it;
    an internal coverage model triggers a second pass only where a
    sub-area stayed undrawn. Success: the dark masses read as obsessive
    and the lights stay untouched, and the budget rule is legible in
    the texture; removing the budget collapses the piece into noise.
139. **Careful Hand** : the embodying-stage mechanic, derived not
    copied from the AARON study. A figure builder where every limb is
    first a skeleton segment, then an outline drawn at an offset
    distance set by the part's "carefulness" budget: thighs at wide
    offset with sparse sampling, hands at tight offset with dense
    sampling. Success: a viewer can read which parts the program
    "cares about" from the linework alone, and turning all parts to
    one carefulness collapses the drawing into mush.
140. **Cell Matrix** : planning as the art, from the AARON study. The
    composition is decided entirely on a coarse grid before any mark
    is made: motif regions claimed like territory, hard no-overlap
    rules, then the marks execute the plan. The piece shows the grid
    and the drawing side by side. Success: the drawing looks composed
    rather than scattered, and the grid alone reads as an interesting
    artifact.
141. **Servo Line** : the freehand-line algorithm as the whole piece,
    from the AARON study. Every stroke the program ever draws is
    short straight segments with lateral wobble and corrective
    steering, and the piece is a study of that one mechanic across
    geometries: circles, grids, letterforms, all in the same
    tremulous hand. Success: at full zoom every curve visibly
    resolves into segments; at arm's length it reads as hand-drawn.
142. **Daily Raster** : the growth-rendering mechanic, derived not
    copied from the Bowen study. A plant-like structure grows by a
    seeded rule while the piece renders it once per "day" as halftone
    inkjet dots and advances a paper scroll, so the composition is a
    row of daily frames. The same plant appears in every frame,
    visibly older; the scroll is the time axis. Success: a viewer can
    read the growth across frames at a glance, the dot raster reads
    as printed rather than drawn, and removing the daily sampling
    (one smooth render) kills the piece.
143. **Nonhuman Pilot** : a drawing steered by something that is not
    the artist, from the Bowen study. A simulated agent (a fly-like
    wanderer in a bounded chamber, or wind gusts from real weather
    data) is the only input; a plotter-style arm with lag and
    overshoot tries to follow it, and its struggle is the linework.
    The piece shows both the pilot's sensed path and the arm's
    drawing, so the gap between intention and mechanism is visible.
    Success: the drawing looks driven rather than designed, and a
    viewer can point to where the arm failed to keep up; swapping
    the pilot for a smooth curve makes it decorative and dead.
144. **Tele-Present Grid** : the tele-present water mechanic, derived
    from the Bowen study. A grid of rods tilts x/y in re-enactment
    of a distant measured surface (wave data, or wind from a live
    feed), each rod an honest 1:1 tilt of the data at its cell. The
    piece offers the rod field and the raw data trace side by side,
    so the mapping is auditable. Success: the field reads as water
    or wind rather than as a chart, and the mapping feels like
    re-enactment instead of encoding; an arbitrary color mapping
    would read as an infographic and is the failure mode.
145. **Closed-Loop Instrument** : the action-perception loop as the whole
    piece, from the Akten study. The work is a system, not an image: it
    renders, the viewer perturbs it, it reacts, and the viewer responds
    until the loop is embodied. The piece ships with no fixed final
    frame; the "score" is learning to play it in under a minute.
    Success: a viewer can play it expressively after a minute of
    fumbling, and switching the piece to open-loop playback of the same
    gestures reads as dead on arrival; if the recording is as good as
    the playing, the loop failed.
146. **Only Knows the Ocean** : the narrow-training-set mechanic, derived
    not copied from the Learning to See study. The piece is trained (or
    tuned) on exactly one visual world: one waveform family, one texture
    corpus, one motion vocabulary. Any input, a scribble, a photo, a
    gesture, gets reconstructed through that one vocabulary, and the
    piece always shows the raw input beside the hallucination so the
    bias is legible. Success: the reconstruction always drags toward
    the learned world, the side-by-side makes the bias the subject, and
    widening the training set (the piece knows everything) collapses it
    into a filter app.
147. **Re-phasing Ensemble** : the oscillator-interference mechanic, derived
    not copied from the Simple Harmonic Motion study. A single driven
    point is the sum of four or five sine oscillators with irrationally
    related frequencies, drawn with additive luminous accumulation on
    black; the figure never repeats, slowly breathes through
    travelling-wave patterns, and snaps into beat-locked accents. The
    piece is time-based: stills are documentation, not the work.
    Success: the slow re-phasing is visible as travelling waves, accents
    land on audible or visible beats, and retuning one frequency visibly
    retunes the whole figure; rational frequency ratios collapse it into
    a looping Lissajous and kill the piece.
148. **Small Archive Dreams** : the Anadol pipeline run on a tiny honest
    dataset, derived not copied. Pick one small personal archive (a month of
    phone photos, every receipt, a year of screenshots), sort it by hand into
    themes, derive the palette by clustering the actual pixels, then run a
    slow non-repeating walk through procedural modes keyed to the themes,
    with the palette as the only color source. The piece states its dataset
    size up front, the opposite of his petabytes, and the dream is made of
    the viewer's own material. Success: the palette is unmistakably the
    archive's own, the walk never repeats, and a viewer recognizes the
    archive in the dream; swapping in a generic pretty palette makes it
    decorative and kills the piece.
149. **River and Wind** : the environmental perturbation mechanic from the
    Anadol study. A flowing pigment field runs its own slow dream underneath,
    and live local weather (wind speed, gusts, pressure, temperature from a
    real sensor feed) bends it like his river metaphor: wind steers the
    currents, pressure squeezes or opens the masses, temperature warms or
    cools the palette voice. Each weather state gets a distinct pigment
    character, and the piece shows the current conditions as a small ledger
    so the mapping is legible. Success: a gust visibly reroutes the flow
    within seconds, calm days and storm days look like different pieces,
    and disconnecting the feed flattens it into wallpaper.
150. **Boundary Currents** : the dark lines between pigment masses as the
    whole subject, from the Anadol stills. Marbled dye fields where the
    camera only shows the currents: thin dark rivers that split, merge, and
    carry the stipple, with the color masses pushed out of frame or dimmed
    to a whisper. The currents respond to a live input (sound, touch, or
    crowd movement) the way his environmental sensors bend the dream.
    Success: the piece reads as fluid mechanics, not decoration, the
    currents have clear sources and sinks, and muting the input stills the
    rivers; a version with the currents removed is just a gradient and is
    the failure mode.
151. **Designed Data** : the Crespo move, derived not copied. Do not design
    the image; design the dataset the piece samples from. The piece draws
    every parameter from a small hand-curated corpus ("species"): texture
    vocabularies, palettes, motion families, each with a lineage note, and
    every rendered specimen ships with its taxonomy card naming the
    "species" it came from. Success: the card reads as real science, the
    specimens stay visibly related through the shared corpus, and swapping
    the curated corpus for random uncurated params collapses the piece
    into decoration, which is the failure mode to test against.
152. **Essence Interpolation** : the latent-walk mechanic, derived not
    copied from the Crespo study. Two procedural organism vocabularies
    (for example jellyfish bell vs coral branch), a walk parameter t
    interpolating every trait: filament length, nodule density, stem
    thickness, palette voice. Render the walk as a strip of specimens and
    the middle frames must be plausible-but-impossible creatures, the
    discoveries of the walk. Success: the middle of the walk is the most
    interesting frame; if the endpoints read as the real organisms, the
    vocabularies are too literal; if the middle reads as noise, the
    interpolation is interpolating pixels instead of traits.
153. **Click For A New Specimen** : the press-anywhere specimen mechanic
    from the jellyfish site, derived not copied. A black page, one organism
    per frame, each click or tap generates a new specimen from the same
    seeded model: same family, never the same creature. A small counter
    tracks how many you have seen, and the piece links out to a real cause
    the way hers links to ocean conservation. Success: ten clicks produce
    ten distinct-but-related specimens, the eighth feels as good as the
    first, the keep action is used more than the skip action; if any two
    specimens in a row read as identical, the sampler is broken.
154. **Style Weight Sweep** : the comparative-grid mechanic from the
    Kogan style transfer study, derived not copied. One piece of
    content (a simple drawn scene or a portrait from the media
    library, used with care) rendered under a sweep of style
    intensities or under several styles at once, presented as a
    grid where the parameter becomes the subject. Success: the
    grid reads as one composition about the parameter, not as
    N separate outputs; the extreme end of the sweep must earn
    its dissolution the way the hieroglyph Mona Lisa does; if
    any cell looks like an accident rather than a data point,
    the sweep range is wrong.
155. **Residue Transfer** : the Google Maps lesson, derived not
    copied. Choose the style image for its literal objects, not
    its texture, and let them splatter on purpose: a portrait
    rebuilt from map pins, a landscape rebuilt from text
    fragments, a still life rebuilt from emoji. The residue is
    the medium. Success: the splattered objects are legible
    enough to name but broken enough to belong to the content;
    if the result reads as a glitch rather than a decision, the
    style image was not chosen, it was grabbed.
156. **Shared Component Walk** : the ABCnet radical insight,
    derived not copied, plotter-friendly. A family of glyph-like
    compositions built from stroke sets that share one fixed
    sub-structure (the radical) while every other stroke
    interpolates along a walk parameter t. Render the walk as a
    strip or an animation; the middle frames must be plausible
    but impossible members of the family, and the shared
    component must sit rock steady through the whole walk.
    Success: a viewer can point at the radical without being
    told; if the middle frames read as noise, the interpolation
    is on pixels instead of strokes; single-weight strokes so
    it plots clean.

## Seeds (from 2026-09-27 Andreas Refsgaard study)
157. **Train Your Own Controller** : the Refsgaard doctrine, derived not
    copied: a piece whose inputs are empty until the viewer trains them.
    Draw three gesture classes (or make three sounds, or hold three poses);
    the piece shows its segmentation back at you (the green-rectangle
    honesty) and only then maps each class to a generative parameter:
    one class drives palette, one drives density, one drives motion.
    Success: a stranger trains it in under a minute and the piece feels
    like theirs; the training UI is visible in the final composition, not
    hidden; if the mapping could be hard-coded buttons without loss, it
    fails the Refsgaard test.
158. **Misread Poems** : the misclassification-as-material move, derived not
    copied. Feed the piece ordinary input (shapes drawn, objects on a
    table, typed words) and run a deliberately blunt classifier over it:
    a shape is a cat or a chair or a cloud, confidence displayed raw.
    The low-confidence guesses become the text layer of the composition,
    laid over the input in plain type. Success: the wrong guesses are
    the best part; the piece is funnier and stranger because the machine
    is wrong, and it never hides a guess behind certainty.
159. **Attention Breeds** : the Genetic Paintings move, derived not copied:
    fitness is attention, measured not stated. A grid of evolving
    compositions where the ones the viewer lingers on (pointer dwell,
    or on touch devices, the ones tapped to linger) breed the next
    generation. Show the lineage, faint, behind the current grid, so the
    viewer sees their attention written into the ancestry. Success: after
    two minutes the grid looks like the viewer's taste, and the piece
    can say so without flattery; a plotter edition freezes one lineage
    as a print series.

160. **Held-Out Critic** : the White generalization discipline, derived not
    copied: an interactive drawing piece with a visible ensemble of small
    local judges (template matchers on a doodle dataset, not a real convnet
    at first) scoring every stroke for a target word, plus one judge kept
    hidden and revealed only at the end as the "generalization" score.
    The caption is load-bearing, Magritte-style: the target word printed
    under the drawing. Success: the piece can say, honestly measured,
    whether the abstraction beats real doodles of the word on the held-out
    judge; if it cannot, the piece says that too.
161. **Ouija Plotter** : the computational ouija board made physical. A
    pen-plotter piece that hill-climbs a 24-stroke drawing against a
    doodle classifier's confidence for a word, with the plotter's own
    wobble modeled inside the loop like White's Riso jitter: every scored
    render carries the pen's actual mechanical noise, so the final drawing
    cannot depend on precision the pen does not have. Two inks, flat spot
    colors, the machine's local-maximum variety as the edition: run it ten
    times, keep the ten different answers, title each with the target word.
162. **Two Inks, Three Judges** : disagreement as the image. Three
    different judges score the same drawing genome: a human-vote layer, a
    doodle-classifier layer, and a pure composition heuristic (balance,
    negative space). Each judge gets its own ink layer, and the final
    print is deliberately misregistered so the judges' disagreement shows
    as color fringing. Where they agree, the inks land together and the
    mark goes dark; where they fight, the fringe blooms. Success: a viewer
    can read, from the fringing alone, which parts of the drawing the
    machine believed and which parts only the humans did.

163. **Pressure Ledger** : the HES planner's pressure-sensitive ranking as a
    drawing instrument, derived not copied. Five line families (each with its
    own thirst: water, land, ink) compete for a fixed mark budget per round.
    A visible dial sets resource pressure; the greedy efficiency ranking
    reorders who draws first and who starves, and the composition redraws
    live. The piece's subject is the inversion itself: at low pressure the
    thirstiest family dominates the page, at high pressure it vanishes and
    the dry families bloom. A plotter edition freezes one pressure sweep as
    a five-panel series.
164. **Plausible Agents** : the HOSNY Bayes-net generation discipline, derived
    not copied. A crowd of plausible-not-real figures, each sampled down a
    conditional chain (age bracket, then neighborhood, then palette and
    posture conditioned on both), each labeled with its sampling path like a
    specimen card. No real person is depicted; every figure is someone who
    could exist given the correlations. The piece is honest about the
    machinery: the conditional graph is printed alongside the crowd, so a
    viewer can see exactly which correlations produced which faces.
165. **Skyline Dashboard** : the HOSNY economy-as-city encoding as a living
    piece, derived not copied. An isometric skyline where a small toy economy
    runs underneath: firms Q-learn their margins, a sickness parameter drifts,
    and the buildings respond, block color by sector, height by output, red
    hospital blocks blooming as sickness spreads. Four small line charts
    (wage, profit, wellbeing, cash) ring the city like the original. The
    viewer watches policy become skyline in real time; a scenario dial
    (food, tech, disease, three stops each) replays the paper's 27 worlds.

166. **Self-Watching Film** : Lavigne's zoom detector turned inward,
    derived not copied. A long generative animation (slow drift, occasional
    zooms and pans) watches itself with the away-from-center flow test and
    re-edits live: the main screen plays the full drift, while a side
    reel accumulates only the detected zoom segments as a growing supercut.
    The piece is a film and its own trailer being cut simultaneously.
    Success: after an hour, the side reel is visibly denser and stranger
    than the source, and a viewer can read the animation's habits from the
    reel alone.
167. **Average Suspect** : the WCCRZ averaged-face move, derived not
    copied. A plotter piece in two panels. Left: a grid of small
    procedural faces, each generated from a different seed and tagged with
    a mundane category (commuter, landlord, subscriber). Right: the pixel
    average of each category's faces, drawn as contour lines, all
    converging on the same bland composite. No real person is depicted;
    the point is that the averaging erases everything but the category.
    Success: the averaged faces are nearly identical across categories,
    and the contour rendering makes that convergence physical.
168. **Corpus Clock** : The Good Life's tempo menu as a composition,
    derived not copied. A piece that replays a fixed public-domain corpus
    (a year's worth of weather logs, a novel, tide tables) at a tempo the
    viewer chooses before it starts: 16,000 items a day (a flood you
    cannot read, only weather), 196 a day (a ritual), 49 a day for years
    (a penance). The interface is a deadpan signup dialog in the style of
    the system being replayed. Success: two viewers who pick different
    tempos describe completely different pieces, and both descriptions
    are true.
169. **Latent Mouth** : the Pincelate hidden-state move as a piece,
    derived not copied. A grid of real words on the left, a grid of
    invented words on the right, each invented word labeled with the pair
    of real words whose phonetic states were averaged to make it. The
    piece is a dictionary of words that exist only as midpoints. Success:
    a reader can sound out each invented word and hear both parents in it.
170. **Chiasmus Engine** : the Reconstructions nesting as a composition,
    derived not copied. A web page that starts with one line and grows:
    every few seconds a new line appears at the center with its mirror,
    older lines fade but never vanish. The constraint is the whole piece:
    ABBA forever, no line ever alone. Success: after five minutes the page
    is a palimpsest the reader can still navigate by symmetry.
171. **One Watt Poem** : the solar alba generator's constraint as a piece,
    derived not copied. A poem generator on a strict energy budget: a
    Markov chain over a corpus the viewer can read in full, one generation
    per hour, displayed on a simulated e-ink page with a timestamp. The
    budget is the aesthetic. Success: the viewer reads the corpus first,
    then recognizes its lines surfacing, recombined, in each generation.
172. **Cartridge Shelf** : Goodwin's SD-card library as a piece, derived
    not copied. One input image, eight cartridges, eight receipts in a
    row. The same room described as TECH NEWS, as FOLK MUSIC, as ROBOT
    GOD. The viewer picks the cartridge that "saw" the image best; the
    argument is the piece. Success: two viewers pick different
    cartridges for the same image and both can defend the choice from
    the receipts.
173. **Temperature Walk** : the multi-temperature seeding as a
    composition, derived not copied. One photograph, one generated
    caption, then the same seed re-run at T=0.1 through 1.0 in tenths,
    ten receipts in a line on the wall. The low end reads as
    description; the high end reads as dream. Success: a viewer can
    point to the exact receipt where the machine stops describing the
    photo and starts hallucinating, and a second viewer picks the same
    one.
174. **Verbatim Scroll** : the 1 the Road discipline, derived not
    copied. A fixed sensor walk (GPS plus clock plus a few hand-typed
    tags) drives a generator for a day; the output is published as one
    continuous scroll with zero editing, typos and dead stretches
    included. The scroll is the piece, not the highlights. Success:
    the unedited scroll contains at least one stretch that reads as
    genuinely literary, and you can point to the sensor reading that
    caused it.
175. **Sweet-Band Glitch** : the Glitch Logos vector technique, derived
    not copied. Draw an original vector emblem (a seed packet, a bus
    ticket, a diner menu badge), then perturb coordinates with small
    jitter and occasional whole-shape drops. Kazemi's finding from the
    local re-render: restraint is the technique; past a certain jitter
    the mark degenerates into noise. Success: three outputs where the
    mark is still legible but unmistakably broken, and one viewer can
    name which part moved.
176. **Honest Transplant** : the Two Headlines swap rule on a non-news
    corpus, derived not copied. Seed catalogs, museum labels, or error
    messages; one noun slot swapped across categories, grammar
    preserved, output as a broadsheet. The humor must come from the
    syntax surviving the swap, not from the words themselves. Success:
    at least one output that reads as a real sentence until the second
    read.
177. **Closed Atlas** : the Content, Forever random walk over a
    hand-built corpus, derived not copied. Sixty or so short articles
    written for the piece, walked first-sentence to random-link with
    the overview-section constraint, so the meander stays earnest at
    the sentence level and absurd at the document level. A closed
    corpus makes it a book instead of a browser. Success: a ten-minute
    read that never repeats a page but always sounds like it means
    it.
178. **Developing LED** : the Descriptive Camera ritual, derived not
    copied. A camera interface that never shows an image: press the

    The wait is the composition, not a limitation. Success: a viewer
    who presses the shutter three times and keeps all three receipts.
179. **One Dollar Twenty-Five** : the Mechanical Turk backend as a

    piece costs a fixed, visible amount, shown on the receipt footer
    ("described by a stranger for $1.25"), with an approval stamp as

    tracks total spend, and the piece gets more precious the longer
    it runs. Success: the receipt footer makes at least one viewer
    do the math out loud.
180. **Accomplice Backend** : Richardson's accomplice mode as a

    two switchable describers: anonymous crowd (dry, candid) versus
    a friend on the other end (warm, teasing, in-jokes). The output
    voice changes completely while the hardware stays identical,
    proving the describer is the medium. Success: two receipts of
    the same scene that read like they came from different
    universes.
181. **Humans As The AI** : from McDonald's "treating humans as the
    AI" line. A scene, a crowd, a system, that is labeled entirely by
    its visitors: click anything, type what you see, and the labels
    accumulate into a visible layer over the work. The piece's
    intelligence is the crowd's, and the work is the slow drift from
    observation to projection. Success: a stranger's label that
    changes how the next viewer sees the same spot.
182. **Two Files Apart** : from the Neural Talk and Walk coupling of
    feed.jpg and description.txt. Two tiny programs that only talk

    the other exists. The seam is the medium. Success: swapping the
    describer changes the piece completely without touching the
    watcher.
183. **Plus One Finger** : from the Augmented Hand Series' hand-aware
    transforms. Take the viewer's own input, a drawn shape or a
    captured silhouette, and apply structural edits inside its logic,
    never a whole-field filter: add a finger, remove a knuckle,
    transpose a limb. The uncanny comes from the edit respecting the
    object's anatomy. Success: a viewer who checks their own hand
    twice.
184. **Ban-List Poet** : from Poetry.Camera's system prompt, which bans

    that never says sad, loss, or pain, and is better for it.
185. **Receipt Half-Life** : from the thermal-paper ephemerality at the

    photographs the piece because they know it will not keep.
186. **The Dial Axis** : from the Poetry.Camera knob and Poetroid's
