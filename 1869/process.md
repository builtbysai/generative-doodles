# No.1869 "Kreis, the Sonic Box" (FN6, PP1116)

Source prompt: TASKS.md, francesca-naibo block, PP1116 (expansion study #244 francesca-naibo; reference frame f15, Kreis with Simone Massaron, Berlin 2025). The study file studies/francesca-naibo.md does not exist in the muse-soul repo (404), so the hexes below were hand-picked from the prompt's named colors.

Driver: one system with two faces, negative and positive taking turns; the felt phenomenon is alternation itself, two voices trading the lead around a shared empty center.

Palette: warm near-black ground #12100E with a slightly lighter field #171512, classical honey wood #B57E3E edged #E0A95C with glow #D9A45B and dark sound hole #5E3A1A, archtop cool steel blue #5F7E96 edged #8FB0C6 with glow #7FA3BC and dark sound hole #2C3E4C, shared circle and plates in chalk white #EDE6D6, overlap noise in static gray #8A8A86, hint gray #9A9184, dark plate #1A1713.

Layout: field-like composition, full-bleed on phones and a centered square on desktop, with the composition scaled up 1.3x on phones so the orbit keeps its presence in a tall viewport. The shared empty circle is center frame, a chalk ring with a fainter inner ring and nothing inside. The two guitar forms orbit it like a binary star, the classical slightly larger than the archtop, their necks pointing radially outward and their bodies grazing the circle's edge. Title and date plate top left, "Watch only. Two voices trade the lead." hint bottom left, both drawn on the canvas in dark plates with chalk text.

Motion: watch only, no interaction. Two orbital agents on a shared ring: the classical on a 48 s orbit, the archtop on a 56 s orbit starting opposite, each with a slow speed wobble, so the orbit never stops and only varies in speed. Their relative cycle is 336 s; once per cycle their paths meet at the circle's edge and then re-separate. The lead alternates every 24 s: the leading form glows faintly and the other dims to a listener at 0.55 alpha. Draw order follows depth, so the lower form is foreground and the higher form background, and the two trade foreground and background as they orbit. When the angular separation closes below 0.45 rad, the outlines merge into noise: both forms are stroked in static gray and a seeded field of static gray specks blooms at the meeting point, pulsing at 2 Hz, then fades as the phases re-separate. Verified numerically: minimum angular separation 0.0038 rad at t about 171.5 s, overlap window about t 152 to 185 s per 336 s loop.

Fixed-phase frame one: t = 18.0 s, classical lower left and leading with its glow, archtop upper right and dimmed, angular separation 2.753 rad so no noise at frame one. All motion is a pure closed-form function of t; all randomness is seeded mulberry32 (overlap noise specks, ground dust), never wall clock, Date, or unseeded Math.random. One synchronous frame is drawn at load; requestAnimationFrame is guarded with a typeof check so node-canvas QA works. Visual Viewport sizing with resize listened to on both visualViewport and window; canvas element pinned in draw() via canvas.style.width/height in px; phone detection via window.screen (min dimension under 700px) with aspect fallback h > w*1.15; page ground is the piece background color. Reduced motion renders a still frame. Noise pulse and orbit wobbles stay well below 14 Hz by construction.

Deviations from the prompt: the prompt's technical approach calls for WebAudio (nylon plucks vs muted archtop lines, a noise-burst duet at overlaps); per the build brief this piece is visual-only, so no WebAudio at all, no sound toggle, and no sound of any kind. The lead alternation, the dimming listener, and the noise burst are all carried visually: glow and alpha for the voices, static gray specks for the duet. The guitar forms are stylized silhouettes (figure-eight body, sound hole, three strings, frets, headstock) rather than literal instruments, per the prompt's own avoidance of literal guitars rotating like a screensaver. Everything else follows the prompt: two forms orbiting one shared empty circle, trading places and foreground, overlapping into noise at the edge and separating, a circle, a negative and a positive, always turning.
