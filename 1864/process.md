# No.1864 "Horizontal Guitar" (FN1, PP1111)

Source prompt: TASKS.md, francesca-naibo block, PP1111. First piece of #244 francesca-naibo / EXPANSION #244.

Driver: the posture shift. A guitar turned into a table, played from above, so the neck becomes a horizon line and the body a sound table. The felt phenomenon is quiet hands arriving from the dark to touch strings that answer. The build is the mechanism, not stage directions: six strings that visibly respond to each gesture type, with hands choreographing approach, contact, dwell, release, withdraw.

Palette (from the prompt): lamp amber #E8A94C with bright #F2C06A and dim #8A6428, varnished wood browns #6B3F22, #8A5A2E, #4A2C16, black shadows #14100C and #0E0B08, pale string silver #C9C9C2, skin tones #C98E6A and #A66E4A, cream plate #F3E9D2.

Layout: field-like composition, full-bleed on phones and a centered square on desktop. The guitar body fills the lower two thirds horizontally, built from two overlapping varnished bouts whose overlap pinches a waist. The fretboard starts just past the sound hole and runs off frame right like a horizon line, frets and markers along it. The bridge is a vertical bar with six pins in a column. A low stand holds the body. One warm lamp glows top left with dust motes drifting in its light. Title and date plate top left, "Watch the hands come and go." hint bottom left, both drawn on the canvas.

Motion: watch only, no interaction. A 48 s loop choreographs ten gestures in sequence: plucks, mallet strikes, a fretted touch, and EBow swells, each on a named string at a set point along the span. Each gesture runs approach, contact, dwell, release, withdraw; the forearm enters from the top edge on a gentle curve and fades out of the dark. Strings answer visibly: plucks and mallet strikes ring with a decaying standing wave, fretted touches damp to a small tremor, the EBow voice swells slowly with an amber glow at the string. A slow camera drift keeps the instrument centered.

Fixed-phase frame one: t = 37.0 s of a 48 s loop. At that phase the EBow is dwelling on string 3 with its glow at full swell, and the mallet on string 2 has just withdrawn, its string still ringing faintly. All randomness is a seeded mulberry32 (wood grain, dust motes), never wall-clock or unseeded Math.random at frame one. One synchronous frame is drawn at load; requestAnimationFrame is guarded with a typeof check so node-canvas QA works. Visual Viewport sizing with resize listened to on both visualViewport and window; canvas element pinned in draw() via canvas.style.width/height in px; phone detection via window.screen (min dimension under 700px) with aspect fallback h > w*1.15; page ground is the piece background color.

Gallery note: "A guitar lies flat like a tabletop instrument under one warm lamp, hands and mallets arriving from the dark to strike and bow the strings from above. Watch the hands come and go."

Deviations from the prompt: the prompt specifies WebAudio voices (plucked and bowed strings, percussive mallet hits, EBow feedback swell); per the build brief this piece is visual-only, so no WebAudio, no sound toggle, and no sound of any kind. The voices are rendered as visible string motion and glow instead. Everything else follows the prompt: guitar flat on a low stand, played from above, neck as horizon, slow cinematic loop, gestures arriving and leaving, no photorealistic hands, no shredder speed, no stage concert, never worn on a strap.

QA: rendered the actual script with node ~/workspace/tools/render-piece.js at 1280x900 desktop, 390x844 mobile, and 720x720 thumb; inspected all three renders. Two fix rounds: the first body outline read as a lumpy blob and the bridge was oriented wrong (horizontal bar with a row of pins), rebuilt as two overlapping bouts with a vertical bridge and pins in a column; the forearm first read as a straight pole, rebuilt as a curved tapered ribbon fading into the dark at the top, and the fretboard darkened. Re-rendered all three sizes after the fixes; thumb.png is the 720x720 render of the final index.html. No em dashes in visible copy.
