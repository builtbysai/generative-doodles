# No.299 Never Clock Off Wall

## Concept
After Ruby Waters' NEVER CLOCK OFF show: a gallery wall of five creature-clocks, salon hung on a warm white wall, each telling real time with black hands over its painted face. Top row: a spiky yellow puffer with a cream belly face, a vermilion tengu with a long orange nose and angry cream eyes under a black hair cap, and an orange boar with bristles, ears, a yellow snout and cream tusks. Bottom row: a cobalt octopus whose curled tentacles frame a cream face, and a kokeshi-face with a black bob, a vermilion hair flower, blush cheeks and red lips over a small cobalt doll body. Below each clock hangs a tiny brass plate carrying only the creature name, the sanctioned text of the piece: Puffer, Tengu, Boar, Octopus, Kokeshi. There are no numerals and no digital readout anywhere; the faces carry twelve plain tick marks and black hour, minute and second hands. Tapping a clock makes it jump down for a closer look: it springs up in scale with a kick, the other four dim, and its face wakes, eyes widening through a three second happy wobble, then everything settles back. Every minute, on the minute, all five do a tiny synchronized tick-nod.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred fit on every screen with the page ground in the wall colour, so the salon hang keeps its shape and generous wall space. The palette is held to exactly the eight specced colours: wall, vermilion, cobalt, yellow, orange, black, cream and brass. Nothing else. The five clocks are asserted in code: the CLOCKS table has exactly five entries (puffer, tengu, boar, octopus, kokeshi-face) and the script throws if that count ever changes. Each creature is its own draw function sharing a common grammar of tick ring, eyes and hands; the kokeshi and octopus draw their tick rings clipped inside their face shapes, the tengu draws its long nose behind the hands, and the boar's hands pivot just above its snout. Hands are computed from a fresh Date call on every frame, never cached: hours, minutes, seconds and milliseconds feed smooth hour, minute and second hands, all black with a brass centre dot. Tap handling converts the pointer to design coords through the stored centred-fit transform (S, OX, OY) and hit-tests the nearest clock within 1.3 times its current scaled radius. A tap sets a three second focus window: the tapped clock's scale is a spring (stiffness 110, damping 13) with a velocity kick on tap, overshooting toward 1.34 and settling back when the window closes; the other clocks ease a dim factor that lowers their alpha; the wake envelope is a sine over the three seconds, widening the eyes by up to 65 percent and rocking the clock with a decaying 16 radian wobble. The minute nod is driven by real time: the draw pass compares the current hour-minute key against the last frame's, and on a change starts a 0.7 second sine nod that lifts all five clocks together by 6 design units. Each clock also hangs from a small nail and string drawn on the wall. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame showing real time as-is with the faces already awake, rAF typeof guarded, no text except the five brass plate names, no UI chrome, no audio, no offscreen canvas, no shadowBlur, no gradients, no em dashes.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners and the canvas pointer listener, and a grep confirms exactly the eight specced palette colours and no em dashes, shadowBlur or offscreen canvas. The hands differ between renders because they show real time from Date, which is expected for this piece. One fix round: the tengu's hair cap initially swallowed its eyes, the octopus tentacles hid behind its head, and the kokeshi bob crowded out its body; the cap was raised and the eyes lowered, the tentacle curls were lengthened past the head silhouette, and the bob was shrunk and lifted. Final renders read as specced at all three sizes: five distinct creature-clocks at varied heights on the warm wall, each with tick ring, black hands and brass name plate legible, the octopus tentacles curling clear of the head, the tengu nose and brows clear of the cap, and the kokeshi body visible below the bob. On the phone the centred fit keeps the whole hang in frame with the wall colour filling the screen.
