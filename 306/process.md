No.306 Wagtail Loop takes Ali Fisher's willy wagtail as a white on navy flash presentation. The bird sits upper centre on a diagonal eucalyptus branch drawn as a thick white stroke with short bark ticks, sage leaves on thin twigs along its length, and a field of flat ochre banksia pods scattered through the lower third like a specimen arrangement, each pod a spiky crowned barrel with rows of dark follicle pits. The wagtail itself is built from white filled shapes for the belly, face and beak, with the back, wing, head cap and bib left in the navy ground colour and held by white outlines, so the black and white plumage reads in linework alone. Its long tail is a chain of five segments cocked up behind the body, finished with a white tip band.

The motion runs on a twelve second seamless loop and the piece is watch only, with no pointer interaction at all. The tail pump follows a hand authored keyframe table written into the code: three quick pumps, a held pause, two more pumps, then a long settle, eased only inside each pump stroke and never driven by a sine. The body bobs in counter phase from the same keyframe value, rising as the tail dips. Every pump keyframe also kicks the leaves, whose tremble is the summed response of spring damped oscillators, strong in the first moment after a kick and decayed well before the loop wraps. One banksia pod hangs under the branch at rest. At eight seconds it lets go, drops to the pod field, rolls a short fixed distance to the right while turning, and fades away across the end of its roll, gone by eleven and a half seconds. Because every element is a function of the loop phase, the frame at twelve seconds is the frame at zero again.

The first frame is already developed, starting partway into the opening burst with the tail cocked mid pump and the leaves mid tremble. The palette is held to four colours only: deep navy, off white, ochre and sage. Everything is drawn from fixed data on a single canvas each frame, with no randomness at runtime.
