# No.296 Squiggle and Dot

## Concept
After Ruby Waters' two-tone pattern split: a pure pattern study with no creature and no face. The screen splits into two hard fields. The top field is cadmium yellow filled with black squiggle and snake marks, her puffer-top grammar. The bottom field is white filled with orange polka dots, her puffer-bottom grammar. A wavy seam near the vertical middle separates them and slowly migrates. The seam breathes up and down on a 40 second loop, squiggles crawl slowly along their own paths, and dots pulse in traveling waves down the rows. Dragging pushes the seam: it deforms around the pointer and springs back. The seam is never softened into a blend.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, cover-fitted full-bleed edge to edge with no margins, on all screens. The palette is held to exactly four colours: cadmium yellow, squiggle black, white, and dot orange. Nothing else. The seam follows the data-only adaptation: no offscreen canvas and no mask bitmaps. The seam is a polyline of 19 spring control points running past both edges of the design space. Each point springs toward a breathing target (a two-harmonic sine field with 8 percent amplitude over the frame height, phased across the width) and is coupled to its neighbours, so the line stays smooth and a drag dent travels and settles. Each frame both fields are drawn as path clips built from the current seam curve: the bottom path fills white and the dot grid is clipped inside it, the top path fills yellow and the squiggles are clipped inside it, and a crisp black stroke along the seam curve keeps the boundary hard. The squiggle field is 150 fixed random-walk strokes held as point data from a fixed seed, each with its own line weight. Their anchors crawl on seeded wander paths whose periods (40, 20 or 10 seconds) all divide the 40 second loop, so the loop is seamless, and each anchor is clamped against the seam height at its x position so strokes stay in the yellow field as the seam moves, with the top clip as the final guard. The dot field is a fixed grid over the bottom field: rows spaced 62 design units, columns 68, alternate rows offset by half a step, dot positions and radii jittered from a fixed seed. Dot scale pulses with a traveling sine wave running down the rows on an 8 second period, five cycles per loop. Drag interaction converts the pointer to design coords through the stored cover-fit transform (S, OX, OY): while dragging, each seam point's target is pulled toward the pointer height with a gaussian falloff of 230 design units across the width, so the seam bulges around the pointer and springs back on release through the same spring and neighbour coupling. Frame one opens at T equals 9 seconds, so the seam is already displaced from the middle by its breathing field and the dot wave is mid-travel. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no text, no UI chrome, no audio, no shadowBlur, no gradients, no em dashes.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners and all four canvas pointer listeners, and a grep confirms exactly the four specced palette colours, no em dashes, and no shadowBlur or offscreen canvas. One fix round: the first pass squiggles read as thin cracks, so the walks were smoothed (smaller turn increments, longer strokes), thickened, and the count raised from 110 to 150. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: the top field cadmium yellow under a dense field of bold black squiggle walks, the bottom field white with orange dots in clear traveling pulse rows (alternating large and small rows at this phase), and the seam a hard black-edged wave already displaced from the middle, sloping across the frame with no blending. On the phone the cover fit crops the field sides and both patterns stay legible edge to edge.
