# No.327 Cheater Quilt

After Debbie Sharman's patchwork revival work: a quilt that is printed rather than pieced, a 5 by 5 grid of 25 squares each pretending to be sewn. Every square holds one mini pattern at a time, drawn flat in five inks: quilt red, indigo, mustard, cream and sage. The four patterns are buffalo plaid as a woven check of filled cells, a daisy-scallop of repeated scallop arcs around a small centre daisy, a starburst of radiating beams on indigo, and berry stripes with clusters of red berries over mustard and sage bands.

Each square is framed by a dashed stitched border, and the whole quilt carries a dashed outer edge. The piece runs on a watch-only 30 second loop. One square at a time re-sews itself, following a fixed authored rotation that visits all 25 squares before repeating, its pattern drawing in step by step over 3 seconds while the other squares rest fully sewn. Ten re-sews run inside each loop, and idle re-sewing never changes a pattern, so the arrangement of types at the end of the loop matches the start.

Tapping a square advances it to the next pattern in a fixed cycle: plaid, then daisy-scallop, then starburst, then berry stripes, then plaid again. The new pattern draws in with the same step by step animation. A tap on a square that is already mid-draw-in is ignored for that square only. Nothing reloads.

Built as a single self-contained canvas piece in the design space of 1280 by 900, centred on any screen with the page ground in the quilt cream. Frame one shows all 25 squares sewn with one square caught mid-draw-in.
