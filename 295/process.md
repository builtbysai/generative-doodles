# No.295 Wave Ground

## Concept
After Ruby Waters' onna-musha painting: a mounted warrior in flat folk-tattoo colour blocks rides a still white folk horse left to right while the banded cobalt wave ground scrolls beneath her. The horse never moves; the world moves, parallax-style, near wave rows faster than far ones. The rider wears a vermilion robe with white crane motifs and mint-green panels over a mint saddle, the horse carries a vermilion bridle with mustard studs, and handmade-paper grain shows through everything. Watch-only, a 20 second seamless loop, her two robe hem panels fluttering subtly while the waves travel.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred-fitted so horse and rider keep their proportions in the middle of the frame, with the ground and wave field drawn wide in design coordinates (from x -720 to x 2000) so the sides bleed full on wide screens and the page ground is held to the specced paper. The palette is held to the specced eight colours only: paper, wave cobalt, wave pale, robe vermilion, crane white, mint, rider black and mustard. The wave ground follows the DATA-ONLY adaptation in the batch spec: there is no offscreen canvas anywhere. Each wave row is a repeated arc motif function, a seigaiha scale of concentric semicircles in alternating cobalt and pale with a cobalt rim, tiled each frame with a per-row translation. Five rows run across the lower third, radii growing from 40 at the horizon to 68 at the front, with alternate rows staggered by half a period. Parallax is by construction: each row's speed is an integer number of motif periods per 20 second loop (2, 3, 4, 5 and 7 periods from far to near), so far rows crawl and near rows travel, and every row wraps seamlessly by its own motif period at the loop seam. Frame one opens at T = 6.4 seconds, so every row is already offset from its tile origin and the scroll reads immediately. Paper grain is likewise data only: a fixed seeded set of 1100 low-alpha grain marks (short strokes in black, a fifth of them cobalt, alpha 0.03 to 0.08), redrawn over the whole field each frame, never a pre-rendered overlay. Horse and rider are layered flat paths in a static pose with black folk outlines: the horse in crane white with a black strand tail, block legs with black hooves, a black mane band, vermilion bridle straps with four mustard studs, and a vermilion saddle blanket under a mint saddle panel with mustard trim and two white cranes. The rider's vermilion torso carries a diagonal mint chest panel, a mustard sash and three white cranes, built from a small shared crane motif function also used on the saddle. Her arm reaches forward to two black rein lines running to the bridle. Two robe hem panels hang either side of the saddle, one mint and one vermilion with a mint edge, and they flutter on two sine offsets whose frequencies are whole cycles per loop (6 and 9 cycles per 20 seconds, amplitudes 6 and 5 design pixels), so the flutter is subtle and the loop seam stays exact. The horse's legs are never animated. Her face is the fixed folk grammar: paper face, black bob with a topknot and mustard pin, one closed eye, a soft vermilion blush and small vermilion lips. Flat fills only, no gradients, no filters or shadowBlur, no text, no sky treatment. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no em dashes.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners, and a grep confirms exactly the eight specced palette colours and no em dashes, gradients, shadowBlur or offscreen canvas. Two fixes were made after the first pass: the mane band was pulled in so it sits on the neck crest instead of slabbing over the head, and the blush was moved to the left cheek so it no longer collides with the lips. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: the wave band fills the lower third edge to edge with five staggered seigaiha rows, small and dense at the horizon and large at the front, every row visibly offset from its tile origin. The white horse stands large and still in profile facing right, vermilion bridle and mustard studs clear, mint saddle with white cranes peeking between the rider's two hem panels, and the rider sits tall in vermilion and mint with white cranes on her robe, black topknot, closed eye, blush and lips all separate and legible, reins running from her hand to the bridle. Paper grain freckles the whole field softly, sky included. On the phone the centred fit sets the whole horse and rider in the middle of the tall frame on the paper ground with the wave band complete beneath them and nothing cropped.
