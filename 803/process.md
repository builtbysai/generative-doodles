# No.803 "TBT" (EXPANSION #98 olivier-mosset, PP4 "TBT (Chameleon)")

## Concept

Mosset's TBT behaviour, 2023: a square canvas in chameleon paint, hung as a diamond, whose colour is a function of where the viewer stands. Walk one way and it reads teal-green; shift and it goes copper; shift again and it turns violet. The painting does not change. The viewpoint does, and here the viewer's motion is the colour: drag horizontally and the surface answers, the ground staying dark and constant behind it. Untouched, the piece drifts through the same readings on its own, slowly, the way a room's light moves a parked viewer past the flip without anyone walking.

## Construction

- Scene: one diamond (a square canvas rotated 45 degrees), centred, on a flat dark ground. Ground choice: plain charcoal #26242A, recorded here per the brief's allowance. It is drawn once per frame and never varies: no vignette, no wall detail, nothing else in the frame.
- The chameleon LUT: 512 readings precomputed at load. The first half mixes teal #2E9B8C into copper #C06A2E channel by channel; the second half mixes copper into violet #7A4FB0. Every surface colour is one of these mixes of two neighbouring pigments only. There are no other stops and no hue wheel, so the surface cannot rainbow: it can only slide along the observed teal-copper-violet band.
- The surface is drawn as 56 vertical strips. Each strip takes its LUT reading at the global viewpoint shifted by the strip's own position across the canvas (plus or minus 0.16 of the band), so at any single viewpoint the diamond already holds a spread of neighbouring readings, warmer at one side, cooler at the other, like the real paint under a fixed light. The strips are filled full-height inside a clip of the diamond path, so the clip, a single continuous antialiased path, defines the silhouette and the diagonals land smooth (see the gate-fix round in the QA log).
- Sheen: layered flat alpha bands clipped to the diamond, no gradients. A primary vertical light band of seven white slices (alpha 0.03 up to 0.16 at the core) sits left of centre and drifts slightly with the viewpoint; a faint broad white band (0.05) lies right of centre; four black shade bands (0.04 to 0.14) gather toward the right vertex. A drop shadow is the diamond redrawn a breath lower in black at alpha 0.35, and a thin black outline at alpha 0.28 holds the edge at every reading.
- Geometry: the half-diagonal R is 0.40 of the frame's smaller dimension (0.44 on phones, where the tall frame has wall to spare), so the diamond is sized from its diagonal and never clips at any aspect.
- Viewpoint mechanics: the viewpoint v is a closed-form function of recorded state and t. Before any drag, v follows the watch-only fallback v = (1 - cos(2 pi t / 30)) / 2 over a 30 s integer loop: teal at 0, copper at 7.5 s, violet at 15 s, copper again at 22.5 s, teal at 30 s. A horizontal drag records an anchor (the viewpoint value settled at the event's frame time) and a target (pointer x mapped across the canvas width, clamped 0 to 1); the viewpoint then approaches the target exponentially with a 0.14 s time constant, evaluated in closed form each frame from the anchor. On release the target simply persists, so the canvas stays at the reading where it was left, and the fallback never resumes.
- Frame one: viewpoint 0, the TEAL end of the band, chosen because the brief lists teal first in the flip sequence and it reads clearly against the charcoal. The diamond sits centred, sheen band visible left of centre, shade gathering at the right vertex. Frame one is the synchronous draw at t = 0; the animation is elapsed-since-load from that anchor.
- House rules: canvas sized from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on both visualViewport and window; canvas style width and height pinned in px inside draw(); phone detection via window.screen (min dimension under 700 px) with the aspect fallback; palette strict (the three pigments, their pairwise mixes, the charcoal ground, plus black and white used only as low-alpha sheen and shade overlays, the house layered-alpha technique); no gradients, no shadowBlur, no ctx.filter, no offscreen canvas; no audio; no visible text except the title. No randomness is used anywhere, so no RNG was needed.

## QA log

- Rendered with node ~/workspace/tools/render-piece.js: qa-desktop.png (1280x900), qa-mobile.png (390x844), thumb.png (720x720).
- Determinism: thumb.png rendered twice and byte-compared with cmp: identical. The duplicate was deleted.
- Desktop read-back: dark charcoal ground, centred teal diamond with generous wall on all sides, the sheen visible as a lighter vertical passage left of centre, shading deepening toward the right vertex, the drop shadow lifting the lower edges. Nothing else in frame.
- Mobile read-back: the diamond fits the narrow frame whole, sized from its diagonal, nothing clipped; the sheen and shade read the same at the smaller scale.
- Thumb read-back: same composition at gallery size; teal reading is unambiguous.
- Range verification: two patched throwaway copies were rendered at 720x720 and deleted. At T0 = 7.5 s (viewpoint 0.5) the diamond reads copper, with the expected teal-ward tinge at the left vertex and violet-ward blush at the right from the per-strip angle shift. At T0 = 15 s (viewpoint 1) it reads violet with copper warmth remaining at the left vertex. The full teal-copper-violet band is reachable and stays inside the three pigments.
- Deviations: none from the prompt's substance. Two recorded choices: the ground is the plain charcoal #26242A (one of the brief's two offered options), and frame one sits at the teal end of the band (the brief's first-named reading). The prompt's "subtle vertical sheen gradient" is built from layered flat alpha bands rather than a canvas gradient, per the house renderer rules; the prompt's precomputed LUT instruction is followed exactly.
- Gate fix round (fly's visual gate): the first build set each strip's height to the diamond's exact vertical extent at the strip's centre x. That made the slanted silhouette advance in strip-width steps (~14 px at desktop size), and the canvas read pixelated rather than coated. Fix: the strips are now filled full-height inside a clip of the diamond path, so the clip alone defines the edge. Nothing about the colour system changed: same LUT, same 56 strips, same plus-or-minus 0.16 per-strip viewpoint shift, same sheen and shade bands, same shadow and outline. All three renders redone; thumb.png rendered twice and cmp-compared byte-identical (duplicate deleted). Verification: read back qa-desktop.png (smooth diagonals, teal reading, sheen left of centre, shade at the right vertex, shadow intact), and a pixel scan of the top-left edge over 80 sampled rows found zero boundary jumps over 6 px (the stepped build jumped ~13 px at every strip) and a blended antialiased pixel just outside the edge on all 80 rows.
