# No.222 Telegraph

## Concept
Kathy McTavish's railway prophecies ran a cross-screen type and telegraph aspect that operated with or without a human typist, and in Blue atlas the core text is intended to dissolve like sand before the reader. This piece is that ghost typist at work on near-black ground. Short phrases in phosphor amber are typed character by character with an irregular rhythm, held a moment, then each letter jitters and crumbles into falling dots. Between phrases, Morse-like dot and dash pulses blink dim green along the bottom edge. The phrase bank is part of the piece: sixteen terse, strange fragments written for it, in her spirit ("the machine renders infinitely", "counts & probabilities", "the train arrives at no station"). There is no cursor and no smooth fading: the typing rhythm itself is the cursor, and dissolution is particulate or it is nothing. Tap reseeds the phrase source, a new shuffle of the bank; the machine types on regardless.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Four line slots run independent per-character state machines on a shared clock: typing (each character lands after its own precomputed interval, mostly 35 to 145 milliseconds with occasional longer pauses), hold (about two to three seconds), jitter (half a second of small random offsets), then crumble, in which characters burst in a shuffled order, each into six to ten dot particles with gravity, until the line is sand. Finished slots rest, then take the next phrase from a shuffled order that reshuffles when exhausted; a tap reseeds the shuffle and restarts the slots staggered. The bottom pulses are a fixed dot/dash message laid out on a cumulative timeline, swept by an eight and a half second playhead: marks under the head burn full green, recent marks smoulder, the rest wait dim. Frame one is pre-run nine and a half seconds at a thirtieth of a second per step, so the page opens mid-cycle: one phrase crumbled to its last letters with the sand still falling, one holding, two being typed. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, the field full-bleed on phones, rAF typeof guarded. On narrow screens the type shrinks until the longest phrase in the bank fits the line.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The first pass was already honest: amber monospace lines centred-left in generous dark, one line mid-crumble with its letters gone to falling dots, the green pulse row blinking along the bottom. One adjustment at the gate: on the phone render the longest bank phrase (forty-four characters) would have run past the right edge when fully typed, so the layout now steps the font down until the longest phrase fits. Final renders show the full cycle at every size, four lines in four different states, and nothing on the ground but the two palette colours.
