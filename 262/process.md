# No.262 River Shadow

## Concept
After Stanzin Tsepel's 2026 Henasku work: a deep Himalayan canyon in layered rock strata, and the pale, winding shadow of a river that no longer flows, a light ghost-channel cutting through the gorge floor where water once ran. The ribbon breathes on a 30 second loop, widening, narrowing to a thread, widening again: a memory of flow, not water. There is no blue anywhere in the piece; the river is a shadow, not a stream. Watch only.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, cover-fitted so the canyon fills the frame. The walls are two stacks of horizontal strata bands, each band a seeded polygon whose inner edge is jittered for the carved look, alternating canyon rock and deep strata tones with a dusty lit lip on the bands the light catches. The gorge floor is a dark perspective trapezoid with converging strata seams; a muted ridge closes the gorge in the distance under the dust sky. The ghost river is one meandering path down the floor drawn as four stacked stroke passes, a wide faint halo under narrower, stronger cores, its width scaled by perspective and by a single breathing sine over 30 seconds. Thirty seeded lichen patches cling to the strata, two flat shafts of dusty light rake in from the upper left, and 36 dust motes drift inside the light. The opening clock starts at 4 seconds, the ribbon mid breath and partly widened, motes already in the rake. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no interaction handlers, no audio, no text, no water.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported resize listeners only, confirming no interaction handlers. Frame one reads as specced on desktop and thumb: strata walls left and right, the pale ribbon winding down the dark floor with its luminous halo, lichen spots on the rock. On the phone the cover crop pushes the walls mostly out of frame and the view becomes the gorge floor and the river itself, which remains centred and legible; accepted as the cover behaviour for a landscape scene, the subject survives the crop. No fixes were needed after the first render pass.
