# No.698 "Palimpsest Floor" — process

## Concept

Doris Salcedo's *Palimpsest* (EXPANSION #85 doris-salcedo, prompt block 3): a floor of stone slabs where names well up as water and seep away, while other names persist faintly in sand, a floor that remembers and forgets at the same time. This piece keeps the mechanism and removes the names entirely: every mark on the floor is an invented glyph, generated by a seeded stroke grammar, never a letter of any real alphabet and never a real name. A 6x6 grid of pale stone slabs sits in a dark frame. On every slab, one sand glyph persists, drawn as dotted strokes, its alpha drifting slowly between 0.25 and 0.40 on a 90-second cycle. Against that persistence, water glyphs come and go on a roughly 20-second cycle: two to three at a time, each a cloud of 60 to 120 droplets that gathers onto a glyph skeleton, holds briefly, then disperses and seeps away. The water marks are deliberately no brighter than the sand ones (peak alpha 0.30 against the sand's 0.25 to 0.40); the point is the cycle, not legibility. Watch-only; a tap reseeds the glyph set. There is no text, no caption, and nothing to read.

## Construction

- Single self-contained `index.html`, canvas 2D only, no external assets. Strict five-colour palette: stone #D6CFC0, sand #8A7B5C, water #4A6B8A, slab seams #A89F8C, background frame #0D0C0B. The only text is the `<title>`.
- Canvas size comes from the Visual Viewport API, with resize listeners on both `visualViewport` and `window`; `draw()` pins `canvas.style.width/height` in px every frame. Phone detection uses `window.screen` (min dimension < 700px), with the aspect rule (h > w*1.15) as fallback only. The grid is a centred square: 88% of the smaller viewport dimension on desktop, 94% on phones; the page ground is the frame tone at every size.
- Glyph grammar (the abstract-only rule): a glyph is a random walk on a 4x5 lattice with free 8-way steps (diagonals allowed, heading reversals allowed), 4 to 7 steps, plus a branch stroke half the time and an occasional lone mark. Vertices are jittered off-lattice by a seeded amount. Because glyphs are grown as walks rather than assembled from letter parts, and because each slab's glyph is an independent seeded stream, the field never spells anything: marks are letterform-like in weight only. Sand glyphs are sampled along their skeletons into dotted point lists once per epoch.
- The sand cycle: one glyph per slab, persistent for the whole epoch, alpha = 0.325 + 0.075 * sin(2 pi * lt / 90 + phase) with a per-slab seeded phase, so the floor's memory brightens and dims unevenly across the grid.
- The water cycle: events on a repeating staggered schedule, one starting every 7 seconds, each living 20 seconds (gather 8, hold 4, disperse 8), so two to three are alive at once. The schedule is offset by -3.5 s so that frame one already holds one event mid-gather, one holding, and one dispersing. Each event picks a slab and a glyph from a splitmix-finalised seeded stream (neighbouring event seeds decorrelate) and carries 60 to 120 droplets. A droplet's position is a closed-form function of the event's local time: a seeded scatter offset that eases to zero (easeOutCubic) over the gather, a small sine wobble that tightens as the droplet lands, stillness during the hold, then a seeded drift outward with growing jitter and decaying alpha during the disperse. Alpha ramps in over the first part of the gather, peaks at 0.30, and fades to zero across the disperse. Droplet positions are clamped to their own slab (the water wells up inside one slab, as in the source work).
- Slabs: a seams-coloured field shows through the gaps; each slab is stone with a seeded tonal overlay (seams tone at alpha 0.02 to 0.065) and 22 to 32 seeded speckles in seams and sand tones at low alpha. Palette tones only, no photographic texture.
- Epochs and the tap: a pointerdown increments the epoch and restarts local time; the sand set, slab textures, and the whole water schedule are rebuilt from the new epoch seed. There is no other state. A tap is a reseed gesture, never a reload.
- Determinism: frame one is the synchronous `draw(bootT)` at load with local time exactly 0. All motion is a pure function of local time on an anchored clock (performance.now offset at boot, never `Date.now`), the epoch seed, and per-event seeded data. No integrated physics, no `Math.random`, no offscreen canvas, no `ctx.filter`, no audio, no microphone. The animation loop runs only when `requestAnimationFrame` exists (typeof-guarded). A `?seed=tNN` query pins the boot draw to loop time NN for QA renders; it is inert in ordinary browsing.

## Deviations

- The prompt's technical sketch gives the water droplets spring physics. They are implemented instead as closed-form eased interpolation (seeded scatter offsets driven by the event's phase), because this series' determinism gate requires every frame to be an exact function of the anchored clock and the seed; an integrated spring system cannot guarantee a byte-identical frame one across renderers. The visible behaviour (migration onto the skeleton, hold, dispersal with jitter and alpha decay) is unchanged.
- The prompt asks for slab texture as "low-contrast noise"; it is realised as seeded speckle data and tonal overlays in palette tones only, redrawn each frame, since the palette is strict and the renderer has no image assets or offscreen canvas.
