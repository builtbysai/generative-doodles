# No.294 Dala Engine

## Concept
After Ruby Waters' Dala horse: a blocky carved wooden-toy horse in profile, straight-cut block body with chamfered joints and no smooth anatomy, painted her way. The body is black with white polka-dot fields and a big white S-curve flourish along the flank. A teal saddle band carries yellow dash marks and an orange saddle medallion with navy spots. The mane is striped navy and yellow, the muzzle is orange with a navy triangle, the eye is her white crescent, and the tail is fine black raffia: exactly 100 verlet strands swaying per strand. A 40 second seamless loop carries one stamp, front hoof lift, head dip and tail flick together. Dragging across the tail parts the strands and they spring back; tapping the saddle medallion ripples its spots once in a dot scale cascade. Nothing ever reloads.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred fit so the shaped horse keeps its proportions in the middle of the frame, page ground held to the specced ochre, which also carries the flat black ground line at the lower third that the horse stands on. The palette is held to the seven specced colours only: black, white, teal, yellow, saddle orange, navy and the ochre ground. The horse is built from straight-cut polygons throughout: a chamfered rectangle body, two block legs, a diagonal neck block and a chamfered head block, with a small upright ear block, so it reads as a carved folk toy rather than an animal study. The polka-dot field is a fixed seeded scatter of white dots with a relaxed minimum distance, clipped inside the body path recomputed each frame and kept clear of the neck overlap; the flourish is one full sine period plotted as point data and stroked thick in white across the flank, passing behind the saddle. The saddle is a teal block over the back with yellow dash marks along its top, bottom and side edges. The medallion is an orange disc outlined in black with seven navy spots, one centre and a ring of six; a tap on it, detected in design coords through the stored centred-fit transform (S, OX, OY), starts a ripple in which each spot scales by a sine pulse delayed by its distance from the centre, so the swell cascades outward once and settles. The mane is seven contiguous blocks laid exactly along the back edge of the neck, alternating navy and yellow with their tips pointing up and back, and the eye is a white disc cut by an offset black disc so only the crescent shows. The tail is the engine of the piece: exactly 100 strands, asserted in code, each a verlet rope of six segments with its own seeded length, fan angle and wind phase, anchored at the tail root and integrated each tick with gravity, a seeded per-strand wind and three distance-constraint passes. Before the first frame the simulation is pre-rolled 90 steps, so frame one opens with the strands already settled into curved hanging raffia and mid-sway. Dragging parts the tail directly in the physics: points inside the pointer disc are pushed out radially each step, and once the pointer lifts, gravity and the pinned roots settle the strands back. The stamp is keyframed on the 40 second loop at 28 to 31 seconds, a single sine envelope driving the front leg lift and tuck rotation, the head group dip around its neck-base pivot, and an extra oscillatory wind burst through the tail; frame one starts the clock at 6 seconds, well clear of the stamp. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no offscreen canvas, no shadowBlur, no text, no UI chrome, no audio, no em dashes.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners and all four canvas pointer listeners, and a grep confirms exactly the seven specced palette colours, no em dashes, and no shadowBlur or offscreen canvas. Three fixes were made during QA. First pass: the tail clumped into a solid wedge, the mane ribbon floated off the neck edge, the flourish read as a shallow wave, and one body dot clipped into a stray crescent under the neck; the strands were given fanned hang angles and ragged per-strand lengths, the mane was reseated exactly on the neck edge, the dot field was kept clear of the neck overlap, and the flourish was redrawn. Second pass: the fanned strands opened as straight needles because frame one drew the un-simulated layout, so the verlet simulation was pre-rolled 90 steps before the first frame and the strands now settle into curved hanging raffia, and the flourish was replotted as one full sine period so the S reads at a glance. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: a blocky black toy horse centred on the ochre ground line, white polka dots across the body, the white S flourish sweeping the flank behind the teal saddle, the orange medallion with its seven navy spots, the navy and yellow striped mane on the neck edge, the white crescent eye, the orange muzzle with its navy triangle, and the raffia tail hanging in fine curved strands at the rear, already mid-sway. On the phone the centred fit sets the horse in the middle of the tall frame on the ochre ground and every element stays legible, tail strands and medallion spots included.
