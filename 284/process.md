# No.284 Slow Dissolve

## Concept
After Sophia Golovanevskaya's Slow Dissolve: a tall dark vessel on a near-black ground, its green-blue crackled glaze pouring down from the shoulder in long drips, like the ocean receding over rocks, and its base fading to rough pale stone. The drips grow slowly on their own. Dragging across the surface smears new drips downward and lengthens the crackle; releasing lets the glaze settle. A double-tap reseeds the drip map. Nothing ever reloads.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred fit on the dark ground colour, so the shaped vessel keeps its proportions at any size. The palette is held to the six specced colours only: glaze, glaze deep, drip shadow, ground, pale base and crackle line. The vessel is a lathe profile held as control points of y against half-width, cosine eased between points, giving a narrow mouth, a swelling shoulder, a long body and a small foot; the mouth is drawn as a dark opening with a glazed rim. The pour is pure column data: the silhouette is sliced into vertical strips 5px wide, and each strip stores its silhouette top and bottom, a natural drip tip and a current tip. Natural tips come from a seeded random walk across 24 drip units, smoothed between unit centres and clamped inside the silhouette, so the drip edge is uneven but coherent. Glaze is drawn per strip, a short drip-shadow underlay at each tip, a glaze-deep overlay whose strength varies smoothly per drip unit over the full run of each strip for depth, and a rounded bulb at each unit tip. Drip length is measured from a pour line that follows the shoulder, not from each strip's silhouette top, so the front of the belly gets long drips even though its strips start at the mouth. A fixed tick advances time: each tip creeps toward its target at about 2px a second and carries any drag velocity with strong damping, so the glaze settles once a drag ends. Frame one opens with every drip already grown 55 to 75 percent of the way to its target, so the pour reads immediately. The crackle is a two-scale network stored as polyline data, about 120 large wandering lines and 320 small ones, generated from the same seed; at draw time each line is tested at its midpoint against the drip region (inside the silhouette and above the strip's current tip) and only drawn if it falls there, in the crackle colour at low alpha. A crackle-grow fraction controls how much of the network shows; it starts at 0.7, creeps up slowly, and dragging lengthens it toward the full network. The pale base is layered translucency only: horizontal bands across the clipped silhouette whose pale alpha rises toward the foot, a ragged band of shoreline dots where glaze gives way to stone, and about 560 seeded speckle dots in pale, drip shadow and glaze deep, each tested against the silhouette, for the rough stone grain. Dragging converts pointer coordinates to design coordinates through the stored centred-fit transform (S, OX, OY) set by frame(); strips within 135px of the drag point take downward velocity from the drag's downward share plus a gravity share of any sideways travel, scaled by a smoothstep falloff and capped, and the crackle fraction grows with drag distance. A second pointerdown within 380ms and 60px of the previous one is treated as a double-tap and reseeds the whole pour (strips, crackle and speckle) from a new fixed seed instead of starting a drag. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no text in the piece, no audio, no offscreen canvas, no filters or shadowBlur, no gradients.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners and the four canvas pointer listeners, and a grep confirms exactly the six specced palette colours, no em dashes, and no shadowBlur, gradients or offscreen canvas. Three fixes across the render passes. First, crackle lines strayed outside the silhouette because only each polyline's midpoint was tested; the network is now tested segment by segment against the drip region and clipped to the silhouette. Second, the opening drip edge read as a barcode because the grown fraction was random per strip; the fraction is now a smoothed per-unit walk, and drip length is measured from a pour line following the shoulder rather than from each strip's silhouette top, which had left the front of the belly bare. Third, a full-height shadow underlay between strips striped the glaze; the underlay is now a short band at each drip tip only, and the glaze pass is clipped to the silhouette. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: a tall vessel centred on the dark ground, green-blue glaze over the neck and shoulder pouring into long uneven drips with a faint crackle held inside them, a dark body band, and a rough pale stone base with a ragged shoreline. On the phone the centred fit keeps the whole vessel in frame and the drips and speckle stay legible. No further fixes were needed.
