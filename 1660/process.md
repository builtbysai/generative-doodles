# No.1660 — Glass Study, Quantum Sheet

Study #218 eric-de-giuli, prompt PP907 (EDG5). Gallery date 2006 · 07 · 29.

## Driver

The feeling is looking at a sheet of glass and not being sure what you are
seeing through. Two pale circles cross and the crossing turns solid, a
colored lens you could almost touch; keep watching and it thins back into
a ghost, just hatching over hatching, and then it firms up again. Nothing
is decided. The ambiguity is the subject, and it needs time, so the piece
moves slowly and asks nothing of the viewer.

## Mechanism

Six large translucent circles drift on slow closed-form sinusoidal paths
over a pale luminous ground (#F2EFE8). Each circle carries its own fill
(pale blue, peach, or cream at alpha 0.34) and a field of fine diagonal
hatching in blue-grey (alpha 0.55), clipped to the circle. Where two
circles overlap, the lens is recolored from a small incongruous palette
(coral, teal, gold): a low-alpha lens wash whose alpha breathes slowly
(0.10 plus or minus 0.07 at 0.10 rad/s), so the overlap reads solid, then
ghost, then solid again, and an accent hatch offset by half a spacing, so
the lines break into short colored segments against the blue-grey field
beneath. Thin glass rims outline each circle. Hatch phase shimmers slowly
(0.06-0.07 rad/s). All motion is sinusoidal and well under 14 Hz by
construction; prefers-reduced-motion renders a still frame.

## Asserted counts

- Circles: 6 (five would leave the field sparse, seven would crowd it).
- Hatch spacing: 7 px at the 720 reference size, scaled with the frame.
- Overlap pairs at frame one: 7 (1-2, 2-3, 1-4, 3-4, 3-5, 4-5, 4-6), so at
  least two overlaps are visible in the first frame, as required.
- Interaction: none. Watch only.

## Deviations

- The spec calls the composition landscape; the build keeps the centered
  geometric square on both sizes per the standing house rule, with the
  off-center circle placement and generous pale margins carrying the
  landscape feeling inside the square.
- Title, date, and hint are drawn on canvas as small plates at the foot
  of the square, per the build brief, rather than in a DOM caption.
