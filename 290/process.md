# No.290 Test Tiles

## Concept
After Sophia Golovanevskaya's thousands of glaze test tiles: a neat museum grid of small square tiles on a warm dark ground, exactly 4 by 5, twenty tiles, each one a different special-effects glaze from her family of crater blacks, gloop greens, lichen dots, layered blues and gold fleck. No tile is a picture of anything; each is a material sample, and each one is alive, running its own micro-glaze loop: craters breathe, gloop pools creep and drip, lichen colonies pulse, blue bands flow sideways, gold flecks twinkle. Dragging swirls the tiles the pointer crosses, their glaze fields churning; double-tapping reseeds the whole grid with a new dominant glaze family. Nothing ever reloads.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, cover fit so the field fills the frame edge to edge, with the warm dark ground as the page ground. The palette is held to the six specced colours only: the ground, crater black, gloop green, lichen, layered blue and gold fleck, with all shading done as layered translucency of those same colours. The grid is exactly 20 tiles, asserted in code at build time: the dominant glaze family takes 8 tiles and the other four families take 3 each, the families are laid out by a seeded shuffle, and every tile's marks are generated from its own seed, so no two tiles in a family share a layout. Tiles are independent mini-scenes held purely as data and drawn directly each frame: crater tiles carry 8 to 10 gold-rimmed craters with near-black floors and offset pits over a field of fine gold pores; gloop tiles carry layered translucent pools that drift on sine paths so the creep loops exactly, plus drips whose length swells and eases back; lichen tiles carry five seeded dot colonies in the family colours over bare ground patches; blue tiles carry six wavy layered bands whose phase runs sideways under a speckle of gold and green; gold tiles carry some seventy flecks, dots and short dashes, twinkling on fast individual phases over crater black. Layout is computed over the visible design rect each frame, so the cover-fit crop never cuts a tile: 5 across by 4 down in landscape, 4 across by 5 down in portrait, tiles square and sized to the tighter axis, with one even gutter measure between tiles everywhere and the outer margins taking up the slack. Each tile sits on a flat dark offset plate and is finished with a fine edge line so it reads as its own object. Drag interaction converts client coordinates to design coordinates through the stored cover-fit transform; every crossed tile gains swirl in the direction the drag curls around its centre, each mark is rotated around the tile centre by the accumulated swirl angle with the angle growing toward the rim, and the swirl decays over about a second while the field keeps churning. A double tap, two taps within 340 milliseconds and 90 design pixels, advances the grid seed and steps the dominant family one place in its fixed cycle (crater, gloop, lichen, blue, gold), rebuilding all twenty tiles in the same grammar. Frame one opens at clock time 11.3 seconds with each tile's phase staggered by a golden-angle spread plus jitter, so all twenty are already mid-loop. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no text, no audio, no offscreen canvas, no gradients or shadowBlur.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners and all four canvas pointer listeners, and a grep confirms exactly the six specced palette colours and no em dashes. One fix during QA: the lichen colony dots first read as sparse speckle, so their size was raised and their clusters tightened, and the colonies now read as lichen against the gloop tiles. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: twenty square tiles in a 5 by 4 grid (4 by 5 on the phone) with even gutters on the warm dark ground, gloop green dominant in the opening seed with three tiles each of crater black, lichen, layered blue and gold fleck, every tile's micro-glaze visibly different from its neighbours. On the phone the grid turns to portrait, keeps all twenty tiles in frame across the full width, and the crater rings and blue bands stay legible at the smaller tile size.
