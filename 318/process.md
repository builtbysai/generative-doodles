# No.318 Three Architectures

After Coral Cockatoo's botanical screens: three Australian flower architectures standing side by side on a near black ground, each built by a different generative grammar, in flat screen-print fills. Exactly three vertical panels are asserted in the code, separated by thin gutters of the ground itself, with no labels and no text.

The left panel is a banksia. Its spikelets are placed by phyllotaxis on a cone surface and projected flat: each spikelet turns by the variant's divergence angle, climbs the cone, and widens with the cone as it goes, drawn as a small gold diamond over a faint gold wash of the cone silhouette, with two leaf green leaves and a stem at the base. The middle panel is a bottlebrush: a central rachis in vermilion with filaments radiating out all around it, each filament a line ending in a small dot, over a short stem. The right panel is wattle: a brown trunk that branches recursively through two levels, every branch tip carrying a pom-pom cluster of small gold dots gathered around a centre.

Idle, the piece runs a seamless 26 second loop. The bottlebrush filaments sway on their own phases, the wattle poms tremble by a pixel or two, and a shimmer travels down the banksia cone in waves, so the frame at 26 seconds is identical to the frame at zero. Frame one opens with all three architectures fully grown, each already at a different staggered point in the loop. Each panel carries an authored rotation of exactly four variants, asserted in the code, with its divergence angle, filament count, or branching and cluster sizes set per variant and all phases seeded per variant. Tapping a panel advances only that panel to its next variant with a grow-in, the architecture regrowing from its base while the other two panels keep their phase untouched. Nothing reloads, and the palette is only the ground, the two golds, the leaf green, the vermilion, and the stem brown.
