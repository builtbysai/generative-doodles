# No.303 Swan Quartet

After Ali Fisher, a tall porcelain vase carries one swan motif repeated four times around its body in cobalt underglaze, with sparse sage reeds in the spaces between the swans. The vase is drawn first as a lathe silhouette with a flared lip, a narrow neck, a swelling shoulder and a small foot, filled in porcelain white and finished with thin cobalt bands at the lip, neck and foot. The ground is a flat sage grey, so the pale vessel sits quietly in the frame.

Each swan is a single linework motif built from the same parts every time: a rounded body, layered wing arcs nested inside it, a small tail flick, and a neck drawn as two parallel S curves that meet a small oval head with a filled beak. The four copies are placed at ninety degree intervals on a virtual cylinder around the vase. Projection maps each copy angle to a horizontal position with the sine of the angle and compresses its width with the cosine, so a swan squarely at the front reads at full width, a swan near the side is squeezed thin at the edge of the vessel, and a swan on the back is hidden. All of the ring is clipped to the vase silhouette and drawn back to front by depth, reeds included, so nothing spills onto the ground.

Dragging horizontally turns the vessel by mapping the drag distance directly to the rotation angle. On release there is no fling or free spin: the rotation eases to the nearest quarter turn and settles there, so a swan always comes to rest facing forward. While the vase rests, time drives the one repeated action in the piece. Every nine seconds the next swan in a fixed rotation through the four bows its neck, the head lowering and the S curve deepening on a smooth envelope before rising again. The first frame is set partway into the first bow with a swan front and centre, so the curve of the neck reads at once.
