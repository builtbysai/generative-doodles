# No.672 — El Códice Perdido

EXPANSION #81 jose-perez-de-arce, prompt block 8 "El Codice Perdido". His 1998 tempera series *El codice perdido del Popol Vuh* rebuilt as an endless, scrollable codex.

## Concept

A horizontal band of codex panels crosses the middle third of an amate-paper ground. Each panel holds one small looped vignette drawn from an invented glyph vocabulary in flat tempera shapes with charcoal outlines. The margins above and below hold sparse drifting glyph dust. The codex has no beginning or end: drag horizontally and the panel sequence wraps forever. Double-tap and a new narrative sequence is generated. No real Maya glyphs were copied; every mark is invented from the grammar the prompt describes (stepped frets, profile heads, birds, serpents, dancers, suns, water). No readable text appears anywhere.

## Construction

- **Glyph vocabulary (7 invented kinds):**
  - *Stepped fret (greca)* — a mitered stepped-spiral stroked wide in charcoal, then narrower in terracotta or ochre, so the color sits inside a charcoal outline; a small bone bead travels the steps.
  - *Profile head* — bone-filled profile (brow, nose, lips, chin as one quadratic chain) with stacked headdress bars, a disc, and an ochre earflare; it nods gently (±0.05 rad) and can be mirrored per panel.
  - *Bird* — ochre body, terracotta wing and tail feathers, bone head on a perch; the head turns in a three-position stepped cycle.
  - *Serpent* — a sine-wave body (charcoal under-stroke, terracotta/ochre over-stroke) with bone back-diamonds, a bone head, forked tongue, and a rattle tip; the wave phase animates the undulation.
  - *Dancer* — terracotta torso, ochre skirt with bone dots, raised arms; the legs interpolate between two stepping poses on a smoothstep beat.
  - *Sun disc* — ochre disc with a simple face and twelve alternating terracotta rays; the ray ring rotates a quarter turn and back on an eased ping-pong while the face stays upright.
  - *Water stack* — four wavy horizontal strips (bone, terracotta, ochre, bone) whose wave phase drifts; a bone droplet bobs above the stack.
- **Sequence:** 9 panels per narrative — the seven kinds shuffled (every kind guaranteed present) plus two extra seeded draws, each panel with its own seeded phase offset, mirror flag, and color variant, so the band never animates in unison. Generated by mulberry32 from seed 672001 + generation × 7919; double-tap advances the generation and reseeds.
- **Maya blue:** exactly one panel index per sequence carries a horizontal #4E7D7A stripe behind its vignette, edged with charcoal rules. The blue is used nowhere else; because the sequence wraps, the stripe recurs once per period, as a codex motif would.
- **Wrap method:** scroll offset is reduced modulo the sequence period (9 × panel width). Panels are drawn by absolute slot index k, with the panel chosen as k mod 9 and the slot placed at k × panelW − offset, starting one slot before the visible edge — so the band tiles seamlessly at any offset, positive or negative.
- **Scroll:** pointer drag moves the offset directly and records velocity; on release the offset integrates the velocity with exponential inertia decay. Drag events are timestamped on the anchored clock; vignette phases and dust drift are pure functions of anchored elapsed time.
- **Paper grain:** 1,500 seeded speckles (mulberry32, fixed seed) stored as data and drawn directly each frame at low alpha in charcoal/terracotta — flat tempera, no gradients, no offscreen canvas.
- **Glyph dust:** 30 seeded marks (dot, hook, mini-fret) in the top and bottom margins, each drifting on a slow sine/cosine path with its own phase and amplitude.
- **House rules:** canvas sized from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on both visualViewport and window; canvas style pinned in px inside draw(); phone detection via window.screen min dimension < 700px (aspect h > w × 1.15 as fallback only) — on phones the panel width factor tightens so panels stay legible; rAF guarded by a typeof check; frame one is a synchronous draw at t = 0, offset 0; no Date.now anywhere; no audio, no microphone; palette strict (amate #E3D3AE, terracotta #B4552D, ochre #D9A441, charcoal #23211E, bone #EDE6D6, Maya blue #4E7D7A in the one stripe only).

## QA log

- Rendered with `node ~/workspace/tools/render-piece.js` at 1280×900 (qa-desktop.png), 390×844 (qa-mobile.png), 720×720 (thumb.png).
- **Determinism:** thumb.png rendered twice and compared with cmp — byte-identical; the second render was deleted. Re-verified after the head fix (frame-one byte sizes unchanged, since the head panel sits offscreen at offset 0).
- **Desktop frame one:** dancer, sun, water stack, bird (carrying the blue stripe), and serpent panels visible in the band; invented dust marks sparse in both margins; nothing clipped.
- **Mobile frame one:** dancer and sun panels refit cleanly to the tall frame, band centered, dust present, nothing clipped.
- **Offscreen-panel check:** a throwaway copy with the initial offset forced to 1300px was rendered to inspect the panels that start offscreen (fret, head, second serpent, second dancer). This caught a real bug: the profile head drew outside its panel clip because its local coordinate helpers added the panel origin on top of a context transform that already included it — the panel rendered empty. Fixed by making the head's local coordinates purely vignette-relative (and giving the headdress bars proper strokeRect outlines). Re-render confirmed the head reads in profile with headdress, disc, and earflare. The throwaway copy was deleted.
- All seven glyph kinds visually verified across the two checks. The blue stripe appears in exactly one panel of the sequence.

## Deviations

- None from the prompt. Two notes: (1) the sequence is 9 panels rather than an unspecified count, chosen so every vocabulary kind appears at least once per period with two seeded repeats for rhythm; (2) the sun's quarter-turn is applied to the ray ring only, with the face kept upright, so the vignette reads as a turning disc rather than a tumbling face.
