# No.989 "Ride the Stage"

Study #121 motoi-ishibashi, PP150 "border": the audience rides WHILL personal-mobility devices (omniwheels, moving in any direction) through a dance, wearing HMDs that mix real-time dancer motion with virtual dancers. Here the stage is seen from above and the ride itself is the piece: no goals, no score.

## Concept

A dark top-down stage. Six warm-white dancer-dots perform seeded phrases, each trailing a faint thread of where it has been. Four rider platforms wander among them, strafing sideways, gliding diagonally, and occasionally spinning in place, because omniwheels have no heading to respect. Each platform carries a small viewport showing a mixed feed: the real dancers near it, miniaturised, plus cyan ghost dancers performing different phrases on their own clocks. When a platform's viewport sweeps over a real dancer, the nearest ghost snaps onto that dancer's position for a moment and a warm ring flashes: the virtual and the real touch. Three backdrop panels slide along hidden rails at the top and bottom edges between phrases, so the room itself quietly rearranges. Tap a platform to board it and its feed takes over a corner of the frame at larger size for 15 seconds; drag to drive the boarded platform; double-tap for a new ride.

## Construction

- Coordinates: everything lives in a unit-square stage, scaled by min(viewport width, height) and centred; the page ground is the stage tone #07080C so the letterbox is invisible. Canvas size comes from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on both visualViewport and window; draw() pins canvas.style.width/height in px every frame. Phone detection uses window.screen physical size (min dimension < 700 px), with the aspect fallback h > w*1.15 only when screen is unavailable. On phones the platform/dancer scale factor rises by 1.22 so viewports stay readable.
- Dancers (closed-form): each dancer is a two-harmonic Lissajous phrase (seeded centres, amplitudes, frequencies, phases) evaluated as a pure function of stage time. Trails are the same function sampled at 14 earlier times with falling alpha (base 0.15) and shrinking radius, so a trail can never disagree with the phrase.
- Platforms (fixed-step sim, 120 Hz): each platform is an agent with position, velocity, body angle, and a target point. In glide state the desired velocity points at the target with magnitude min(0.055 units/s, 1.5 x distance); velocity blends toward it, so the platform strafes in any direction while its body angle stays independent: the omniwheel signature. Reaching a target picks a new seeded one. On a seeded schedule a platform enters a spin state (1.6 to 3.0 s): translation damps out and the body rotates with a sine-envelope angular velocity, then it glides off again. Platform 0 is scripted to start a 2.4 s spin at t = 29.2 s so frame one (t = 30 s) catches it mid-spin.
- Viewports (mixed feed): drawn as clipped rects on the main canvas inside each platform's rotated frame (no offscreen canvas). The window covers plus/minus 0.16 stage units around the platform: real dancers inside it are drawn as small white dots, and three per-platform ghost dancers (their own seeded two-harmonic phrases in viewport space) are drawn cyan at 0.5 alpha, constantly. Both realities are therefore on screen at all times, in every viewport.
- Sync: during the sim, when a dancer comes within 0.105 units of a platform centre and that platform's 6 s cooldown has passed, a sync fires: the event is recorded (stage time, dancer position), a warm #FFD88A ring expands and fades over 0.9 s at the dancer, and for 1.5 s ghost 0 in that platform's feed is drawn at the synced dancer's mapped viewport position instead of its own phrase point (falling back to its phrase if the mapped point leaves the window). A soft two-note chime marks it when audio is live.
- Panels (closed-form): two panels ride the top rail, one the bottom. A seeded move list (first move at t = 27 s, then one every 20 s, round-robin, 4 s smoothstep glides) is generated at build time; a panel's slot at any t is computed from that list, so slides are exact functions of time.
- Interaction: tap (pointer down/up without drag) hit-tests platforms within 0.07 units and boards one for 15 s of stage time: its feed redraws in a large corner inset (window plus/minus 0.24 units) with a centre marker for the platform itself. Drag boards the nearest platform if none is boarded and then steers it by retargeting it to the pointer each move (it strafes/spins after the hand). A double-tap reseeds the whole world (seed offset increments) and re-runs the pre-roll.
- Audio (lazy, first gesture, typeof-guarded throughout): a low air bed (looped filtered noise), one triangle wheel hum whose pitch and gain follow mean platform speed, a pentatonic melodic line stepping every 2.4 s of stage time using each dancer's assigned note, the sync chime, and a low rumble when a panel slide starts. All audio triggers fire only on live steps, never during pre-roll, so the QA render is silent and complete.
- Determinism: world building uses mulberry32 seeded streams (one for layout, one for sim-time choices). Frame one is the synchronous draw after exactly 3600 fixed steps of 1/120 s with silent=true. No wall clock feeds any drawn state; the rAF loop (guarded by a typeof check) only advances the same sim with an accumulator and a 24-step cap.

## Deviations

- The prompt's per-platform wheel hums are simplified to one shared hum pitched by mean platform speed (house precedent for multi-voice prompts; the bed, melody, chime and rumble remain distinct).
- The boarded corner inset is placed bottom-left rather than left unspecified by the prompt ("a corner of the frame"); bottom-left keeps it clear of the top rail panels in the default view.
- The ghost "phase snap" is realised as ghost 0 taking the real dancer's mapped viewport position for the sync window rather than re-phasing its whole phrase, which makes the touch readable at viewport scale; the ghost returns to its own phrase when the window ends.
