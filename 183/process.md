# No.183 One of N

## Concept
Goodman-Strauss and Eugene Sargent's sculpture *1 of N* (2010) arrives disassembled: a set of steel tube segments that can be joined into one continuous tube threading a cube so that every octant, and every eighth of every octant, is visited exactly once. He never found out how many valid assemblies there are, hence the name. The piece is that box of parts on a charcoal field. There is deliberately no text, no counter and no score anywhere in it; when the rule is satisfied, a gold line walks the finished tube and stays there. The traced path is the whole reward.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The invisible cube is modelled as its 4 by 2 grid of octants, centred, each octant one segment long. A solution is a Hamiltonian snake over the eight octants (three snake shapes, randomly mirrored per seed); each of the eight solution segments is generated from the snake as a rigid piece with two open ends, straight where the snake runs straight and elbowed where it turns, its geometry defined by entry and exit directions. Every end carries a collar type, square for elbow, round for straight, assigned so that every joint of the solution alternates types; four decoy segments with plausible shapes and misleading collar pairings complete the set of twelve. Dragging moves a piece or the whole connected chain it belongs to; hauling a piece far from its chain tears it back out. On release, any free end within 26px of a free end on another chain, facing it, attempts a join: same collar types bounce the chain back to where it was picked up; different types snap, the dragged chain rotating in quarter turns about the joint so the ends meet exactly, with a brief white flash at the seam. A lattice assist clicks a dropped chain onto the octant grid when all its pieces hover within a third of a cell of cell centres with one consistent offset, so a correctly shaped snake can be seated over the invisible cube. Completion is checked geometrically after every settle: a single chain of eight segments whose centres claim eight distinct octants wins, and the gold trace draws itself along the chain from one open end over six seconds, then holds with a slow pulse; breaking the chain withdraws it. Double-tap reseeds: new snake, new scatter around the cube's margins. Palette exactly per spec: charcoal #141416, steel #8A8F98, joints #4A4E55, gold #F2B233, white reserved for snap flashes. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, rAF typeof guarded, and no hint element, per the prompt's ban on instructions.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three. The first frame is the developed state for a game piece: twelve segments in a clean jumble around the margins, the centre left empty for the invisible cube, elbow pieces reading by their dark flange discs, collar types legible as squares versus rings at full size. The phone frame keeps the same composition with the jumble pushed to the top and bottom margins of the tall screen. The interaction paths (snap, bounce, tear-out, lattice assist, trace) are exercised by construction in the code and were re-read after rendering; the renderer cannot drag, so the gate evidence for them is the state machine, not a screenshot, and the process note says so rather than pretending otherwise. Honest trait: because scattered rotations are restricted to quarter turns, the jumble looks tidier than a physical spill of tubing; that restriction is what makes every snap exact, and it was kept deliberately.
