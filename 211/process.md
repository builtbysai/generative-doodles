# No.211 Tele-Present

## Concept
David Bowen's tele-present wind puts a single wind sensor outdoors and lets it drive 126 tilting mechanisms holding dried stalks in a gallery far away: the distant system is the brain, the stalks are the dumb body. This piece keeps that mapping exactly. One simulated remote signal, sampled from noise at a distant offset coordinate, drives every stalk in the field; no stalk sways on its own. In the upper-left corner the mechanism is exposed: a steel accelerometer box with its own sensor stalk, which twitches first, and the field answers a beat later, the beat stretching with distance so gusts visibly sweep across the stalks. The weather changes every forty five seconds or so, calm shivers, sweeping gusts, deep storm bends, each regime fading into the next. Drag anywhere to throw a local gust through them; they bend away from your hand for a few seconds, then rejoin the field.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The field is exactly 126 stalks on a jittered 14 by 9 grid, sorted so inner stalks stand a little taller; each stalk is one quadratic bezier stroke in #C9A86A with two to four darker #8A6F3E seed-head ticks at the tip, flat colour, no ground line. The wind is a pure function of time: a regime machine crossfades amplitude, wave and frequency parameters between gusts, storm, calm and gusts over ten seconds at each forty five second boundary, and the tilt samples layered sines at a distant offset. Because the driver is a pure function, each stalk samples its own delayed past directly: the sensor stalk draws the present sample at slightly larger amplitude, the field draws samples delayed by distance from centre, plus a horizontal stretch in the gust regime that turns the delay into a travelling wave. Dragging spawns gust puffs along the pointer path; stalks within radius bend away from the puff's motion under a gaussian falloff decaying over three seconds. On phones the grid is thinned by skipping alternate stalks, per the prompt, never by shrinking strokes. Frame one opens in the gust regime with the field already leaning in unison. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. Round one crashed outright: stalks sampling delayed time before zero drove the regime index negative, a fault the browser would also have hit in the first frames of any delayed stalk. The regime lookup now wraps negative segment indices properly. Round two framed wrong: every row stood on the same bottom line at full length, so the field read as diagonal rain and the sensor stalk ran off the top of the frame. Bases now step up with depth and lengths scale with row, giving a near field of tall stalks and a far field of short ones under open black sky, and the sensor box sits fully in frame, its stalk leaning visibly further than the field behind it. Final renders hold the unison lean at all three sizes.
