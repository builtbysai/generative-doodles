# No.633 El Bosque Infinito

EXPANSION #77 seba-ilabaca, prompt block 1 "The Unfolding Forest" (verified verbatim from TASKS.md). Sebastian Ilabaca's FANTASTIC FOREST / EL BOSQUE, a 3-metre wordless concertina book, reimagined as an endless canvas strip the viewer unfolds by hand.

## Concept

A full-bleed night forest panorama in three depth layers that never ends. Near trees stand large and black-green in front, a softer middle layer behind them, and thin blue-gray far trees at the back, each layer on its own wobbly ground ridge. Every tree grows from a seeded grammar, so no two canopies are alike. One small creature hides in each screen-width segment of the near layer, dark on dark with a single bright mark: an owl with amber eyes, a mouse with a pale tail, a fox with one warm ear, a hovering hummingbird. Nothing announces them; they are found by scrolling. A slow day/night drift passes over the whole strip on a 120 second cycle, mostly dark, with a brief warm dawn glow at the horizon, stars and fireflies in the night phase. Dragging horizontally unfolds the concertina with parallax (near 1.0, mid 0.55, far 0.25); released, the strip keeps drifting slowly on its own. Double-tap reseeds: a new forest regrows behind a brief fade.

## Construction

- Single self-contained index.html, canvas 2D only, no external assets, no text anywhere except the title. Strict palette: ground #0B0E11, near #12211A, mid #24382E, far #3E5A52, warm accent #D8A94E, owl amber #E8B93B, firefly white #F2EDDA. Layer tones are strength variants of the same hexes, per series convention.
- Tree grammar (seeded per tree via mulberry32): trunk is a 5-point wobbly polyline with cumulative hand-line jitter, drawn as segments of decreasing width; 3-5 branches curve from the upper trunk into the canopy; canopy is 10-40 overlapping ellipse blobs (count and size by layer) scattered around the canopy center, each with its own rotation and tone (base, light, dark, or, rarely, a small warm accent fleck).
- Toroidal wrap: strip content is a pure function of (seed, layer, content segment index). Each layer's content repeats with a period of 12 screen widths: the segment drawn for world segment s is generated from index s mod 12, and visible segments s-1, s, s+1 are drawn each frame so canopy overhang crosses joins naturally. The ground ridge is a sum of sines whose phases are whole cycles per 12-width period, so the ridge is continuous across joins and repeats exactly. Stars live in strip space too (one seeded set per segment, content period 6, parallax 0.075, which is 3/40) and fireflies are positioned modulo the 12-width span. Because 240 screen widths of near-layer travel is a whole number of periods for every layer (240, 132, 60 and 18 segments respectively), the frame at offset X + 240 widths is identical to the frame at X.
- Fauna: one per near-layer segment, type cycling owl, mouse, fox, hummingbird by segment index (the 4-cycle divides the 12-segment period exactly). Placement picks a host tree from the segment's own seeded stream and anchors to its canopy (owl, hummingbird) or its base (mouse, fox).
- Day/night: daylight is a smoothstep bump on a 120 s cycle (full value 0.55, deliberately modest); it mixes the sky gradient toward the far blue-gray, adds a warm accent glow at the horizon during transitions, raises layer brightness, and fades stars and fireflies out. Frame one is at cycle phase 0: deep night.
- House rules: canvas sized from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on visualViewport and window; canvas.style pinned in px inside draw(); phone detection via window.screen (min dimension < 700 px) with the aspect fallback. Determinism: the synchronous first frame is drawn at offset 0 and cycle phase 0 with the base seed; all motion is anchored elapsed time (performance.now minus load time, rAF typeof-guarded) plus drag deltas; no Date.now, no Math.random, no offscreen canvas, no filters, no audio.

## Deviations

- Toroidal period is 12 screen widths rather than the brief's 3. Reason: the fauna cycle has 4 types, and 4 does not divide 3, so a 3-width period would either repeat fauna types unevenly or break the wrap; 12 is the smallest period divisible by both, and it also makes repetition far less noticeable while keeping the wrap exact. The brief's requirement (a toroidal segment, seamless wrap) is preserved and verified byte-for-byte.
- Star parallax is 0.075 instead of a rounder figure, chosen so the star field also repeats exactly over the common 240-width wrap period (18 whole segments). No visual difference from 0.08.
- The day/night swing is capped at modest brightness (peak daylight factor 0.55). Reason: the brief asks for a full gradient drift but also for a mostly dark, subtle read with frame one at deep night; the cap keeps every phase recognizably the same night forest.
