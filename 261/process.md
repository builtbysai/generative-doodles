# No.261 Path of Hope

## Concept
After Stanzin Tsepel's 2024 print on chiffon, made at the banner's own 1 by 4 metre proportion: a winding trail of footprints in snow, large boot prints and small light prints side by side, curving between dark rocky mountainsides toward distant blue-tinged peaks. The trail itself is still; it is a record, not an event. Only a slow snowfall and a faint breathing of light move, across 40 seconds. Watch only.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 400 by 1600 design space, the true 1:4 scroll, contain-fitted: on desktop it centres at its own ratio with the page ground in the sky tone; on phones it fills the width and nearly all the height. The scene is layered flat: sky, two bands of distant blue peaks with snow caps, the snow field with soft shadow drifts, and dark rocky walls whose inner edges follow the valley as it narrows with height, finished with a pale rim and soft contact shade on the snow. The trail is a sine-wound path up the scroll. Footprints are a single JS path (a sole wedge plus a heel pad) replayed under affine transforms along the path: sixty prints in two lanes, heavy boots and lighter steps side by side, feet alternating, size and rotation jittered by a seeded generator, shrinking with perspective from large in the foreground to small at the peaks. Every print is present from the first frame; none appears as a drawing gimmick. Snow sparkle is masked to the open field between the walls, 110 seeded flakes drift down inside the scroll, and a translucent white wash breathes on a 40 second sine. The opening clock starts at 6.2 seconds, the full trail in place and the snow already falling. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no interaction handlers, no audio, no text, no figures.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. Three fixes. The prints first read as a small dotted ribbon; the foreground scale and lane separation were increased until the boots enter large at the bottom and the two walkers read side by side. A first attempt at wall contact shadow rendered as two pale icicle streaks down the rock faces; it was replaced by a pale rim on the rock edge plus soft shade steps on the snow, then softened again when the steps read as a pattern. Snow sparkle originally fell on the dark rock as well; it is now masked to the open field. Frame one reads as specced: the complete twin trail winding from big foreground boots up between the rock walls to the blue peaks, snow falling.
