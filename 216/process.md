# No.216 Night Pilot

## Concept
In David Bowen's plant drone work, the electrical resistance of a living plant's leaves becomes the flight signal for a drone: the plant is the pilot, the aircraft is the body, and the flight path is a drawing no human composed. This piece is that flight at night, seen as a long exposure. One bright amber point crosses deep black, steered by two slow procedural channels standing in for the leaves' resistance, one for horizontal velocity, one for vertical, with inertia and soft turns at the frame's edge. Behind it, a phosphor trail: a JS point list with per-point ages, the colour sliding from #E8B45C down to #3A2A12 as each point ages, everything gone by about twenty five seconds, so the drawing is always half dissolved. Flights run about thirty five seconds; when a flight's light has fully faded behind the drone, it hovers for three seconds, trembling, and then a new plant, its channels rephased, takes over. A double tap calls a new plant at once. Dragging near the drone nudges it off course; the plant's signal reasserts within about two seconds. The point stays small; the trail is the composition.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Each leaf channel is layered sines at incommensurate rates, so the velocity vector keeps rotating through the quadrants and the flight resolves into loops, dives, hovering slows and long glides without ever repeating; a plant change adds a thousand to the phase offset, which rephases both channels into a genuinely different hand. The drone integrates velocity toward the signal with a first-order lag, boundary steering bends the target velocity inward inside a margin, and positions are kept in normalised coordinates so the flight is resolution independent. The trail is capped at 2600 points, drawn as short segments coloured by the head point's age, thinning as it fades; no offscreen buffer. The head is a #FFF2CC point with a small radial glow, drawn last, riding the trail's head. Frame one pre-runs twenty five seconds of flight, opening mid-flight with the trail's older reaches already brown and thin. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three, over four tuning rounds. The first signal design, smooth value noise through heavy velocity smoothing, produced a single lazy swoosh per fade window, and its successor, faster value noise, still dwelled at extremes long enough to pin the drone against the ceiling in a dead straight run. The channels were rebuilt as layered sines, which rotate the velocity vector honestly; their rates then doubled so a fade window holds more than one turn of the flight, and cruise speed rose so the loops widened into full-frame excursions instead of one small patch of circling. The head glow also came down a step so the point stays a point. Final renders show a developed loop wandering the upper frame, bright at the head, dissolving to brown at the tail, at all three sizes.
