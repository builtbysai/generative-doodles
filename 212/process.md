# No.212 Station 46246

## Concept
David Bowen's wave forms put a networked buoy out at sea and let its two numbers, wave height and wave period, drive a lattice of tilting rods in the gallery through small motors. This piece keeps the mapping whole: the ocean is never drawn. A simulated buoy reports height H and period T; a lattice of seventeen columns by three rows of wooden rods hangs from near invisible threads under faint motor stubs, and each rod answers the same wave a fraction later than its neighbour, 0.35 radians of phase delay per rod, so a crest rises at the left, travels, and dies at the right. Every minute the buoy reports a new sea state and the machine eases into it over eight seconds: mirror-calm, choppy, swell, storm, where some threads hang slack and a few rods lag erratically. About every three minutes the buoy goes missing for five seconds; the wave freezes, the lattice settles, and waits. A tap forces the next report early; a double tap skips straight to storm. Nothing is labelled; the lattice is the readout.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The wave phase is integrated from the eased period each frame, so period changes never jump. Sea states live in a small table cycled on a sixty second schedule with smoothstep blending of H, T and a slack factor; the lost-buoy window is a pure function of the clock, decaying amplitude exponentially while the phase holds still. Rods are rounded lines in #D8B46A on #060809, with a 1px #F0D488 highlight on the upper edge that strengthens near the crest; threads are 1px verticals in #1A1C1E from motor stub to rod end, the stubs #4A4A4E bobbing with the same wave slightly ahead of their rods. The lattice spans eighty percent of the width in the upper-middle band, with generous dark below. Taps are disambiguated by the standard delayed-commit: a lone tap advances the sea state once the double-tap window passes. Frame one opens mid-wave in the choppy state, the clock pre-started so a crest sits partway across. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The first build scheduled forced reports through a time-walked tap list whose minimum spacing silently swallowed rapid double-tap advances; it was replaced with plain mutable sea-state state (index, start time, commit-on-window logic), which behaves identically in the browser and is honest to reason about. Final renders show the wave mid-passage at every size: three rod rows stepping through the same crest at their delays, stubs faint above, threads barely there, and two thirds of the frame left dark beneath the lattice.
