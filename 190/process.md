# No.190 Mock Back

## Concept
Sargent's Mock Back is a creature that performs what it hears: the Mimic Octopus crossed with a mockingbird, except everything it plays back comes out transformed. This build assembles it from scrap, true to his fabricated menagerie: stacked rusted plates for a body, rebar ribs, a mesh patch, one enormous funnel ear, and a beak that is actually a speaker grille. It dozes with an ember breathing in its chest, stirs, turns its ear toward you, and runs its little repertoire (bird-like chatter, a clang, a low hum) as gold light tracing along its ribs. Drag tilts its head the way a listening animal follows a sound. It is never cute: no eyes, no smile, just apparatus.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The body is drawn in one consistent coordinate system (horizontal offsets in units of the smaller viewport dimension, vertical positions as fractions of height) as layered vector plates: three torso plates in weathered steel #6E737B and rust #8A4B2A with dark seams, rivets and seeded weathering speckles, a crosshatched mesh patch, a neck strut, and a head carrying a rust patch, the slatted grille, and the funnel ear on a spring hinge with concentric rim rings and a cyan listening arc. Five rebar ribs are cubic beziers bowed across the torso, their sample points precomputed; every light effect plays along those samples. A 40 second attract loop runs the performance: fourteen seconds of sleep with the chest ember breathing on a six second cycle, a three second stir as the ear swings toward the viewer, then the motif trio: staccato gold dots hopping rib to rib for the chatter, an expanding gold ring from the grille with one full rib sweep for the clang, and a slow travelling gold wave for the hum, before it settles and sleeps again. The first frame opens mid hum, traces already moving. Dragging turns the head on a spring toward the drag; a tap rebases the loop clock so the stir and a full performance begin at once. One deliberate omission, recorded plainly: the original prompt gives the creature a microphone and a record step, and this build has neither. No audio is captured, no mic permission is requested; the prompt's own no microphone attract loop, plus the tap trigger, is what ships, which also matches the gallery's standing no microphone rule. Palette exactly per spec. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, centred composition on the piece's own ground at phone size, rAF typeof guarded, hint lowercase and short.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three. The first build failed compositionally: vertical positions mixed height fractions with size units, so at desktop size the head floated a hundred pixels above the torso and the ribs hovered in the empty gap like a spring, three disconnected parts instead of one creature. The geometry was rebuilt in a single coordinate system with the head overlapping the torso top on a visible neck strut and the ribs seated inside the plate stack. After the fix the desktop frame reads as one assembled animal: funnel ear up and turned, cyan arc lit in the hum phase, gold wave dots running the ribs, ember glowing in the chest. The phone frame keeps the whole creature centred at about eighty percent of the height with the same details legible, narrower in proportion as expected when size keys off the smaller dimension.
