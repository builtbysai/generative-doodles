# No.280 Calabash

## Concept
After Njalikwa Chongwe's harvest calabash: a squat pot centred and large on a near-black ground, banded in earthy brown and sea-green, breathing very slowly while its colour bands slide like a slow tide and fine speckle drifts up its surface. The mouth is a dark ellipse, and the only handles are two small lug nubs at the shoulder, kept in silhouette so nothing reads as cartoonish. Watch only: there is nothing to touch.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred fit so the vessel keeps its shape at any aspect, with the near-black ground as the page ground. The palette is held to the five specced colours only: the near-black ground, earthy brown, sea-green, the dark interior of the mouth, and speckle cream at low alpha. The vessel stands 540 design pixels tall, from y 190 to y 730, centred at x 640, and swells to about 670 pixels wide at the belly, so it reads squat with generous near-black margins. Its silhouette is a piecewise lathe width function: seventeen knots of height fraction against half width, smoothstep interpolated and sampled at 84 steps per side, giving a narrow mouth, a slight neck, a wide round belly and a settled flat foot. Small lug nubs are part of that silhouette: a narrow outward bump of 24 pixels centred at 25 percent down the height, added to the half width on both sides at the shoulder. Breathing scales the width by 1 plus 0.022 times a 36 second sine times a weight function that is plus one at the belly and negative at the neck, a very slow and slight swell. The bands are horizontal strips 90 pixels tall, clipped to the profile path and filled flat, alternating brown and sea-green. Each band boundary is offset vertically by 14 pixels times a sine over the 36 second loop, with a staggered phase of 0.95 radians per boundary, so the boundaries drift independently like a tide instead of marching together; each band is filled between its own two drifting boundaries. Fine speckle is 200 particles stored as data, each with a seeded height fraction, a lateral fraction of the local half width, a size, a low alpha and an upward speed. Height is reduced by speed times the clock and wrapped with a floor modulo, so speckle drifts slowly up the surface and wraps from mouth back to foot, drawn each frame as small cream circles clipped to the silhouette. The mouth is a dark ellipse at the rim, narrower than the rim width and 19 pixels deep, drawn after the clipped body. Flat fills only: no gradients, no glow. The opening clock starts at 9 seconds, a quarter of the loop, so frame one opens with the band boundaries already offset from their rest positions and the breath partway through its swell. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no interaction handlers, no audio, no text, no offscreen canvas and no filters or shadowBlur.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported resize listeners only, and a grep confirms no interaction handlers, no shadowBlur, no gradients and exactly the five specced palette colours in the script, with no em dashes in either file. One fix. The first render read stepped at the shoulder and its lug nubs blended into the flare, and the mouth ellipse sat as wide as the rim. Extra intermediate profile knots smoothed the belly into a rounder calabash, the lug bump was narrowed and strengthened so the nubs protrude as small silhouette bumps at the shoulder, and the mouth was narrowed to sit inside the rim; re-rendered and re-inspected at all three sizes. Final frame one reads as specced: a large squat calabash centred on the near-black ground, banded in brown and sea-green with the boundaries visibly offset, a dark mouth ellipse at the top, small lug nubs at the shoulder, and fine low alpha speckle across the surface, nothing clipped. On the phone the centred fit keeps the whole pot in frame with the bands still legible.
