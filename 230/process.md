# No.230 Melt Together

## Concept
Kluber's ambiguity game, after his 2019 Friday I'm in Love. Orange, yellow and teal thin lines stripe the upper and lower sections of the panel. Between them sits a central rectangular band on a pale yellow ground, filled with fragmented teal-green and orange geometry, broken rectangles and triangles in loose rows. Across the whole panel, once every forty seconds or so, a single diagonal wave of luminance passes, deliberately desynced from every painted edge. Where it passes, band edges seem to shift colour, though the paint never moves. The viewer cannot settle which colours are painted and which are projected, and that undecidability is the piece. There is no framing, no label and no explanation on the surface: subtlety is the point, and if the wave were obvious it would be wrong.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The paint is precomputed layout data with a fixed seed: one stripe stack cycling orange, yellow and teal over the panel height, drawn only in the upper and lower sections by clipping, and a fragment table for the band, five rows of segments whose lengths, colours and occasional triangular forms come from a second seeded stream, clamped so no fragment crosses the band's outline. The band itself is a pale ground rectangle with a thin teal-green outline. The projection is a single rotated linear gradient band, additive with 'lighter' at a peak alpha of 0.10, angled at twenty five degrees, its centre travelling back and forth across the panel on an eighty second round trip so a crossing takes about forty seconds. Frame one opens with the wave partway across, one flank of the panel reading a shade lighter than its paint. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, page ground in the band's pale yellow, rAF typeof guarded, no handlers, no text.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The first render had two fragment segments spilling past the band's right outline into the surrounding ground; segment lengths are now clamped against the band width at generation time, and the second render shows every fragment contained, the rows reading as broken geometry inside a clean rectangle. The wave at frame one is a gentle brightening on the left flank only, easy to miss at a glance, which is the correct register for this piece. Phone and thumb frames keep the structure legible.
