# No.316 Three-Part Registration

After Coral Cockatoo's three-part screens: one large botanical composition printed in three separate layers on cream paper. The first layer is the blossom fills in vermilion, a cluster of five lobed blossoms, one large and central with four smaller ones around it. The second layer is the leaf fills in teal, eight narrow lanceolate leaves fanning below and beside the blossoms. The third layer is the monoline detail in mustard, drawn at one weight throughout: branching stems, blossom rings with radial stamen ticks and centre dots, leaf midribs with side veins, and small registration crosses at the corners of the composition.

Exactly one composition and exactly three layers are asserted in the code. The two fill layers are drawn with multiply blending over the paper, so where vermilion and teal cross they make a darker new colour, exactly as overlapping screen inks do. The mustard line layer draws last in source-over, staying crisp on top. The layers are data sets drawn each frame on a single canvas, with no offscreen canvases.

Frame one opens in perfect registration, so the intended image reads before anyone plays. Dragging hit-tests the topmost layer under the pointer first, the line layer, then the leaf fills, then the blossom fills, and drags that layer's offset. On release the layer stays where it was left, so a misregistered print is the resting state. A double-tap, a second tap within 350 milliseconds, snaps all three offsets back to zero with a short soft settle ease. Idle, the composition holds still in whatever registration it was left in. Nothing reloads, and the palette is only the paper and the three inks.
