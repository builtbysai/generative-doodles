# No.259 Glacier's Retreat

## Concept
After Stanzin Tsepel's 2024 Disko Valley land work: a lime-white hillside scored with a branching maze of low blue scrap-wood walls pierced by rust iron rods, marking where the Parkachik and Drang Drung glaciers used to be. Blue is the data layer: blue is former ice, white is exposed earth. Over 35 seconds the blue thins and retreats upslope, then the hillside restores itself; the work dismantles and reimagines itself, never a restart screen. Watch only.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, cover-fitted so the hillside fills the frame with no sky anywhere. The hill is flat lime bands following the slope's fall, 2400 seeded speckle points of lime texture, and a soft rock-grey vignette at the edges. The maze is grown recursively in slope space from two trunks, one per glacier: each branch carries on near-straight or strikes off at a hard built angle, tributary fashion, to five depths, plus seven lone wall rows in the same building habit. Walls are drawn straight, plank to plank, as a deep-blue shadow side offset downslope under an organic-blue top. A single retreat parameter, eased over the 35 second loop, thins each wall's width and fades it, shallow branches going first and the trunks last; the rust rods at the junctions shrink and fade more slowly, outlasting the walls as the real markers do. Forty-two dust motes drift across the hill. The opening clock starts at 8.6 seconds, mid retreat, the maze partly thinned with the rods still standing. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no interaction handlers, no audio, no text, no water animation (the water is gone; that is the point).

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. Two fixes. The first maze grew long curving chains that read as rivers, not walls; the growth was rebuilt with shorter runs and hard tributary turns, the walls drawn straight instead of curved, and the trunks moved mid-frame so the two clusters read as the twin glacier traces. The slope bands and corner vignette were also too strong, folding the hill into graphic stripes and paper corners; both were faded back until the hill reads as chalky lime with bare rock creeping in at the edges. Frame one reads as specced: twin branching wall clusters on white lime, rust rods at the junctions, the shallow branches already thinning.
