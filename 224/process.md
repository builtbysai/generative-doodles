# No.224 Blue Atlas Scroll

## Concept
McTavish's Blue atlas field notes keep their vector origin: hand-built, slow-geared re-enactments of rusty machine-learning tools, counts and probabilities, drawing and re-shuffling, and at the centre of it a broken hero, a bird with a worn suitcase travelling by train. This piece is a long horizontal scroll of that atlas on paper white: thin Prussian blue line drawings only, no fills, no shading, deliberately low-bandwidth. Coastline contours ring three fictional islands, two open coasts run the length of the sheet, a railway curves through the middle with eight stations, and the bird rides above the line with its suitcase, bobbing a breath. Equations and text fragments are set into the scroll and dissolve as they pass, letters scattering into dots and dropping before the text re-forms. Sparse annotations in faded red are the only other marks. The scroll drifts slowly left, seamlessly; drag scrubs through the atlas by hand. It is not a map of any real place.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The whole scroll is precomputed JS data in an abstract 2400 by 800 space and drawn directly each frame at a drift offset, twice across the wrap; no offscreen canvas. The coasts and the railway are sums of sines with integer cycle counts over the scroll width, so the wrap is seamless by construction. Islands are wobbled contour rings; stations are ticks and small circles on the rail curve; the bird is a stroked line glyph of body, wing, beak, leg and wheeled suitcase. Text fragments dissolve on a slow per-fragment cycle: inside the dissolve window each letter is drawn at a deterministic scattered offset with falling drift and reduced alpha. The clock opens at 13.7 seconds and the scroll offset at 72 units, tuned so frame one has the railway and the bird in frame with the equation fragment ("x = counts + probabilities") caught mid-dissolve. Dragging subtracts pointer movement from the scroll offset and pauses the drift; release resumes it. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded. On phones the scroll zooms so the bird and the line keep their presence in the taller frame.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. Two adjustments at the gate. First, the fragment tuned to be dissolving in frame one sat outside the opening view, so its dissolve phase moved to the equation fragment the opening actually frames: the desktop and thumb renders now show its letters mid-scatter above the railway. Second, the phone render was too zoomed out, the bird small in a band of empty paper, so the phone scale tightened and the scroll band fills the tall frame properly. Final renders: thin blue lines on paper white at every size, red annotations sparse, the hero riding his line with his suitcase.
