# No.207 Twisted Landscape

## Concept
Asia Ward builds dreamscape reliefs from stacked aluminum sheets, each layer twisted, fanned or pinched differently, so one landscape reads from several viewpoints at once: a cave hollow here, a flying ridge there, a river cut, grass as fine strokes. This piece is that diorama: seven sheets floating in black, their top edges carrying terrain profiles, lit by a highlight that drifts across the relief on a thirty second loop. Drag sideways and the stack fans open like a deck; drag down and it pinches shut. Let go and it settles into a new distortion, never exactly the original. Double-tap deals another dreamscape.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Four precomputed variants (cave, flying ridge, river cut, grass field), each seven layer recipes with two-sine terrain profiles, a depth tone mixed between shadow #2A2D32 and sheet #8A8F96, and features drawn from the palette only: black cave arches clipped into a face, a teal river channel #1E4A5A meandering down five faces, grass as fine #4E6B4A strokes standing on the profiles. Each layer is drawn back to front under its own transform: a settle rotation, a fan offset proportional to its distance from the middle sheet, a pinch offset and vertical squash, plus a slow idle sway. Fan and pinch are spring values; dragging sets their targets from the drag delta, and on release the targets relax to a seeded fraction with small per-layer nudges, so every released arrangement differs from the last. The lit edge #D8DCE0 is stroked segment by segment with alpha peaked where the drifting light phase is passing. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, the slab near full width on phones, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. Round one fault: the grass was drawn inside the layer clip, and since the blades rise above the profile edge, the clip cut every blade off; the cave variant's grassy sheets rendered bare. The grass pass moved into the unclipped edge pass. Round one also had the slab bleeding past the phone frame edges once fan offsets were added, so phone width came down to 86 percent. Final renders show seven fanned sheets with two black cave hollows, grass standing on the lower profiles, edges lit brightest mid-slab where the light phase sits at frame one, and the whole relief floating over its soft shadow.
