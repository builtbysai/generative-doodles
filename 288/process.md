# No.288 Never Clock Off

## Concept
After Sophia Golovanevskaya's NEVER CLOCK OFF timepiece, made with Ruby Waters: a ceramic vessel fused with a clock face. The black pot body sits behind a bold yellow dial with a white rim, twelve plain markers stand in for numerals, and the hands keep real time. One small orange and blue fish swims a slow circle around the whole piece, never clocking off itself. The piece is watch-only: nothing to tap, drag or read, and nothing ever reloads.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred fitted so the shaped timepiece keeps its proportions in the middle of the frame, with the near black ground as the page ground. The palette is held to the six specced colours only: the ground, the dial yellow, the rim white, the fish orange, the fish blue, and the body black. The vessel is a squat pot silhouette, a narrow mouth, swelling shoulders and belly, and a tucked foot, drawn behind the dial so the black belly and foot show below and at the sides of the face. A faint rim coloured edge stroke in low alpha lets the black body read against the near black ground, and a blue glaze stripe crosses the lower belly below the dial. The dial is layered: a white rim disc, a thin black ring, then the yellow face. Its centre sits a little off the vessel centre. Twelve plain markers, longer and thicker at the quarters, sit on a ring inside the face; there are no numerals and no other ticks. That marker ring drifts three degrees one way and back over the loop, a sine of the loop phase, so it returns exactly at the wrap. The hands read real time from Date each frame: the hour hand eased by the minutes, the minute hand stepped to the whole minute with no seconds drift, and the second hand sweeping smoothly with the milliseconds, thin and blue with a short counterweight tail. A black centre pin with a blue core caps the hands. The fish is one cartoon shape drawn directly each frame, an orange elliptical body, a blue wagging tail, a blue fin, and a white eye with a black pupil. It follows a circular orbit around the dial centre, one full orbit per 60 second loop, rotated onto the tangent of the circle so it always swims forward. The tail wag runs at two cycles per second, a whole number of cycles per loop, so the wrap is seamless. Frame one opens at 21 seconds into the loop, so the fish is already mid-orbit at the lower right and the dial already reads the actual time. No offscreen canvas, no gradients, no filters or shadowBlur, no extra fish and no readout. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed loop offset, no text, no hint, no pointer handlers, no audio, no em dashes.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners and no pointer listeners. One fix during QA. First render: the vessel neck rose a few pixels above the top of the dial and read as a flat dark cap, and the fish was small at phone size. The neck was dropped behind the dial so the shoulders flow out from behind the rim, and the fish was enlarged, then the piece re-rendered. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: a black vessel body with a blue belly stripe, fused with a yellow clock face inside a white rim and a thin black ring, twelve plain markers with no numerals, black hour and minute hands and a thin blue second hand showing the render time, and one orange fish with a blue tail, blue fin and white eye already mid-orbit at the lower right, swimming down and to the left along its circle. Nothing clips at any size. On the phone the centred fit sets the timepiece in the middle of the tall frame on the ground colour and the dial, markers and fish stay legible. No further fixes were needed.
