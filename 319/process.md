# No.319 Crackle Screen

After Coral Cockatoo's Marble Crackle screen, taken as a flat printed graphic rather than a glaze. A black crackle network sits on white paper and repeats as a printed tile, and the frame shows only one window of a field that runs on past every edge.

The tile is built as a voronoi network. Forty seeded sites in one tile period give exactly forty cells, and the cell edges are drawn as ink lines in a warm black on a warm white paper. Two of the forty cells in each tile are tinted flat celadon, exactly five percent, filled under the lines so the network stays unbroken over the colour. The accent is celadon for the whole piece.

There are five authored constellations of the network, each with its own seed and its own pair of accented cells. The tile is drawn three by three as path data on a single canvas each frame, which is what lets the pattern pan without a seam. Dragging moves the field and the pan wraps at one tile period, so the field never runs out, and letting go leaves the pan where it stopped. Frame one opens already panned a little off the tile origin, so the piece reads as a window onto the print rather than a poster of it.

At rest the print keeps growing, slowly. Every fourteen seconds one crack extends by one segment, following a precomputed sequence of twenty four steps written for each constellation. The extensions accumulate on top of the network and stay, and a tap moves on to the next constellation in the fixed order of five, restarting that constellation's own sequence from its first step.
