# No.1065 "The Slit Scanner"

Study #131 yoichi-sakamoto, YS2 / PP226: Deleted Reality (2018), the velocity-controlled slit scanner Sakamoto built with Hideaki Tai, from Sakamoto's side. Tai's version of the same mechanism shipped as No.1057; this piece is the same physics under a different instrument discipline, and the differences are the point (see Construction).

## Concept

Three rotor assemblies, 5 thin bars each, spin at fixed speeds in a black void under one global strobe. The renderer is the strobe: bars are drawn bright only at strobe-sampled instants, and recent samples persist at decaying alpha, so what you see is never the spinning itself but the sampled pattern the timing produces. When the strobe frequency passes near a lock ratio for a rotor, that rotor's samples stop stepping and pile onto one standing star, which burns amber for as long as the lock holds. The timing is the subject; the rotors are just the dials it shows on. Dragging takes hold of a motor directly, tapping snaps the strobe onto the nearest lock, and the sound is the machine room: hums pitched by speed, a tick per strobe, a tone when a lock catches.

## Construction

**Assemblies.** Triangle layout, sizes descending as the prompt demands: big rotor at (0.30, 0.35) of the world square, bar length 235 units, hub radius 17; small A at (0.72, 0.28), length 150, hub 12.5; small B at (0.545, 0.73), length 118, hub 10.5. Hubs are filled #2A2E35 discs drawn last, always visible, with a small bar-colour centre pip. Speeds (authored set): 1.50, 1.05, 0.85 rev/s. Angle advances by omega times dt each step with no easing anywhere: velocity is the instrument.

**The sweep.** One global strobe, frequency a piecewise-linear function of cycle time over a 45 s loop: dwell 5.00 Hz (0-10 s), ramp to a 3.75 Hz dwell (13-19 s), ramp to a 5.25 Hz dwell (24-30 s), ramp to a 4.25 Hz dwell (35-41 s), ramp back to 5.00 at 45 s.

**Lock math.** A 5-bar star maps onto itself under a 1/5-turn rotation, so the sampled pattern stands still when 5f/F is an integer (f = rotor speed, F = strobe frequency). The residual pattern crawl is apparent(f, F) = |5f/F - round(5f/F)| * F / 5 rev/s; a rotor counts as locked below 0.045 rev/s (about 16 degrees per second, a held breath of drift). The dwells are tuned one per rotor: 3.75 Hz gives 5*1.50/3.75 = 2 exactly (big rotor, apparent 0.000, locked roughly 12.8-19.4 s including the ramp shoulders); 5.25 Hz gives 5*1.05/5.25 = 1 (small A, locked roughly 23.3-31.2 s); 4.25 Hz gives 5*0.85/4.25 = 1 (small B, locked roughly 33.9-42.3 s). Small B also catches brief transient near-locks as the ramps pass its other ratios. Each strobe sample is stored with the lock flag of its instant; locked samples draw in #FFD75E, the rest in #E8EAEE. Sample persistence is 2.2 s with alpha 0.92 * exp(-age / 0.7).

**The flash.** At each strobe crossing the whole frame gets a white lift of alpha 0.15 * (1 - age / 0.09), decaying over 0.09 s: at or under the prompt's 0.15 alpha at all times, a breath on the frame, never a whiteout. Between strobes the bars also draw as true-motion ghosts at alpha 0.02-0.055, three instants deep. No blur, no trails, no afterimage tricks: samples are instants, and the standing pattern is arithmetic, not an effect.

**How this differs from a generic strobe piece (and from No.1057).** Descending assembly sizes instead of equal rotors; visible machine hubs instead of bare centres; a global flash component, capped at 0.15 alpha; amber lock notation, so the illusion announces itself when it catches; drag as direct motor control rather than inert watching; tap as a snap to the nearest lock frequency; and a specced sound layer. The sweep is dwell-structured, one lock per rotor per cycle, instead of a continuous drift through ratios.

**Interaction.** Watch-only core. Horizontal drag grabs the nearest rotor: its velocity offset follows drag distance at 0.004 rev/s per px, clamped to plus/minus 1.6 rev/s, so under the finger its bars freeze, crawl or reverse; on release the offset decays exponentially (tau about 0.6 s) back to the authored speed, and its lock state and hum pitch follow the true effective speed throughout. Tap adds a frequency bias that snaps the strobe exactly onto the nearest lock candidate (5f/m for m = 1..4, within 3.3-5.7 Hz); the bias then decays with a 6 s tau so the composed sweep resumes. Double-tap (two taps within 0.35 s of sim time) reseeds: new speeds in 0.6-1.9 rev/s, kept at least 0.12 rev/s apart so the locks stay separate, and the sim re-pre-rolls to frame one.

**Sound.** Built lazily inside the first pointer gesture, all calls typeof-guarded and wrapped so a missing AudioContext changes nothing. One triangle hum per rotor at 36 + speed * 48 Hz, gain 0.012 each, pitch tracking the live effective speed including drag offsets; a 1.6 kHz square tick of 0.03 s per strobe; a sine tone (520/610/700 Hz by rotor) on each lock entry. Nothing autoplays; the QA renderer runs the piece in complete silence.

**House mechanics.** Single self-contained file, canvas 2D only. Fixed-step sim at 120 Hz, seeded mulberry32; frame one is the synchronous draw after exactly 1800 pre-roll steps (t = 15.0 s). Nothing is derived from Date.now. Canvas size comes from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on visualViewport and window; draw() re-pins canvas.style.width/height in px every frame. Phone detection uses window.screen (min dimension < 700 px) with the aspect ratio h > w * 1.15 as fallback only; the 1000-unit world is contain-fit, so the full triangle fits at 390 px wide. No gradients, no shadowBlur, no ctx.filter, no offscreen canvas, no microphone. No visible text anywhere except the title element.

## QA log

- Probe before building (node, mirroring the sim constants): at t = 15.0 s the sweep sits at 3.750 Hz, the big rotor's apparent drift is 0.000 (dead lock), small A steps at 0.300 rev/s apparent, small B crawls at 0.100 rev/s apparent (pattern moving, not locked, correctly white). Time since last strobe at frame one is 0.158 s, past the 0.09 s flash decay, so frame one carries no background lift.
- Renders via render-piece.js at 1280x900, 390x844, 720x720: all clean, silent, no exceptions. Thumb rendered twice and byte-compared with cmp: identical; the duplicate was deleted.
- Desktop and thumb read-back: near-black void; three hubbed assemblies in a triangle, descending in size; the big rotor shows one crisp standing 5-bar star in amber with a faint 3-instant ghost fan beneath it; small A shows a bright white stepping star of separated samples; small B a denser white star still slowly crawling; hubs solid with centre pips; nothing fan-like in motion terms, the patterns are sample sets.
- Mobile read-back: the whole triangle fits inside the 390 px width, amber lock and both white patterns legible, nothing clipped.

## Deviations

- The global flash is a 0.09 s decay from the strobe instant rather than a held 0.15 lift for the full strobe interval. Reason: a sustained full-interval lift at 3.75-5.25 Hz would read as a flickering room, not an instrument, and the flash discipline in the brief caps the alpha, which this honours exactly; the flash is a pulse on the instant, like the real strobe.
- Tap-to-lock is implemented as a decaying bias on top of the sweep rather than a rewrite of the sweep clock. Reason: it keeps the 45 s composed loop phase-true (the piece returns to its schedule on its own), while the strobe still lands exactly on the lock frequency at the tap.
- Frame one sits at t = 15.0 s with the flash fully decayed rather than catching a flash pulse. Reason: the gallery frame should show the locked pattern itself; the flash is a live-motion component and is present from the first strobe after load in a browser.
