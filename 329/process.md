# No.329 Ways of Protection

After Arunima Dazess Wangchuk's Ways of Protection at Old Leh, this piece hangs nine elongated kite forms in dark air. The kites sit in three loose depth layers of three, the back layer small and dim, the front layer large and full strength, each one a tall lozenge with a vertical aspect near one to two and a half, strung on a thin wool line that runs off the top of the frame. Two colourways drift across the field. The warm kites are dyed madder red, terracotta and mustard with undyed wool edges. The dark kites are near black, umber and slate blue, held against the ground by their pale edges and their lit windows. No two kites share a sway phase, and nothing in the field is symmetric.

Each kite carries two to four triangular windows, set out in an authored table in the code, kite by kite, and counted and asserted there along with the nine kites and the three layers of three. The windows are not cut out of the fabric. Each one is drawn inside the kite path as three nested translucent triangles, wide and faint, then smaller and stronger, then a bright core, so the light reads as coming through cloth. Every kite drifts slowly along the artist's fear to hope axis, at its own speed and phase, and its window glow follows it, moving between warning rose and wellbeing peach and back again, so at any moment the field holds windows at mixed points along the axis.

At rest a slow layered wind moves through the piece. Two sine layers cross over each kite, stronger at the loose lower end of the fabric than at the head, so the outline deforms gently while the whole kite sways and turns a little on its string. Dragging through the field throws a gust. The kites near the drag take an impulse in the direction of the throw, deform further, and brighten as their windows catch the light, then settle back on slow per kite springs whose decay runs near fifteen seconds, long after the hand has passed. The impulses are clamped, so no kite ever leaves the frame. The first frame opens with every kite already swaying mid phase and its windows already somewhere along the axis. All marks are drawn directly on one canvas each frame, the kite outlines and window points displaced as data by the wind field, with layered translucency only and no text anywhere in the piece.
