# No.189 Crank Cut

## Concept
Sargent's fabrication pieces, staged as one continuous shift: a crankshaft driven rig on the left cuts steel coils under a torch, one coil per revolution, at exactly the correct rate, forever, and the coils stack on the right into a Double Triamond of interlocking triangular rings. Behind everything hangs the dark silhouette of a gyroid, and late in the cycle hexastix rods slide through its hexagonal holes until the two separate sculptures interlock into one. Then the product dissolves and the rig starts again. The machine is the point: the crank's rate never varies except when a tap switches the whole rig to a slow inspection speed.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. A single rig clock drives everything: the crank angle is the clock times one revolution per six seconds, so coils, carriage and flame can never drift apart. The rig is drawn on the left third: iron frame posts and crossbar, a steel pipe feed, a spoked crank wheel whose pin drives a connecting rod to the torch carriage sliding on the pipe, a flickering gold flame and an orange cut glow while cutting runs. Each completed revolution finishes one ring: rings are rounded triangle polylines sampled from quadratic corner arcs, drawn thick in fresh steel #B9BEC7 with a fading hot orange #FF7A1A overlay for their first second, and the just finished ring travels from the cutting chute to its slot over half a second. Seven slots sit in a tight hexagonal cluster so the rings genuinely interpenetrate. The ring currently being cut grows along its own polyline at the chute, its leading edge glowing orange, the metal behind it already cooling to steel. The gyroid is a perforated ghost: a field of #1C1D22 squares sampled from a trigonometric level set, with four hexagonal holes punched through it in ground colour. At 45 rig seconds the cutting stops mid coil; between 45 and 53 the four hexastix rods slide in from the right, staggered, each seating through one hole; the interlocked state holds to 57.5 seconds, then rings, rods and the half cut coil fade out over two and a half seconds, leaving the bare rig, and the loop wraps. Frame one starts the clock at 33 seconds: five rings laid, a sixth half cut at the chute. A tap toggles the rig clock between full rate and quarter rate; drag does nothing here, by design, because the machine is not to be disturbed. Palette exactly per spec. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, full frame composition kept at phone width, rAF typeof guarded, hint lowercase and short.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three. The first frame had a counting problem made visible: at 30 seconds the fifth ring was still travelling, so the cluster showed four settled rings and the fifth sat at the chute looking like a finished spare, with no ring actually being cut. The start time moved to 33 seconds, which settles all five and leaves the sixth exactly half cut. The cluster was also too loose at first, rings floating apart instead of interlocking; ring radius grew and the slot offsets tightened until adjacent rings overlap at their corners the way the triamond weave requires. The torch flame, initially a speck, was enlarged until the gold flame and its orange cut point read at thumbnail size. After fixes: desktop shows the full rig mid stroke, flame lit, five woven rings and the gyroid ghost with its four holes; mobile keeps the same staging compressed, cluster legible, crank and chute both visible.
