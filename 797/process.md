# No.797 "Column Study" — EXPANSION #97 niele-toroni, PP6

## Concept

The late Toroni format from the 2025 Locarno retrospective: the column as the final support. Three tall vertical canvases stand side by side on a gallery floor line, floor to ceiling, with generous wall margins. Down each canvas runs a single file of imprints of the one No. 50 brush at a mechanically regular interval: green on the outer two canvases, orange-red on the center one. Nothing else happens, and that is the point. The view itself drifts slowly down the columns and back over a 36 second loop, so the imprints pass like a meter; stepped vignette bands at the head of the frame and the foot of the canvases imply the columns continue beyond what the frame can hold. Watch-only. No people: the scale is implied by the hang, never shown.

## Construction

- Single self-contained `index.html`, canvas 2D only, no external assets. Strict palette, five tones and nothing else: canvas #EFEDE6, green #2E7D4F, orange-red #C73E1D, floor #B09A7A, wall #F4F4F2. No visible text except `<title>`.
- Room: wall fill full-frame; floor band below a line at 0.88 of frame height. Each canvas gets one depth cue: a floor-tone shadow rect at alpha 0.35 slipped slightly down and right behind it (a palette tone composited, not a new colour).
- Triptych geometry is a pure function of frame size: canvas top at 0.035 H, canvases standing on the floor line; side margins 11% of width and gaps 4.5% on wide frames, margins 7% and gaps 3.5% on phones (screen-based detection, aspect fallback), so the portrait-native piece takes a little more of the tall phone frame. The three verticals dominate at every size.
- Imprints use the Toroni dab construction shared with the rest of this study (as shipped in No.792): a solid banded body, near-opaque through the upper two-thirds where the brush pressed, easing toward the dry bottom edge; vertical bristle ridges of the same paint deepen the load; thin gap strokes score the separations between bristle bundles; a dense load cluster sits at the top edge. Per-dab jitter (rotation ±2°, scale ±5%, alpha ±10%) comes from mulberry32 streams consumed once at setup (seeds 797101 / 797202 / 797303, one per canvas). Placement is never jittered.
- One deviation of material, recorded: in No.792 the gap strokes take the ground tone because those dabs sit on the ground. Here the dabs sit on canvas, so the gaps take the canvas tone #EFEDE6 — the support showing through the paint is the canvas, not the wall behind it.
- The interval: pitch P = canvas height / 5, dab size = 0.34 × canvas width, one centered file per canvas. Each canvas carries a closed ring of ROWS = 7 imprints whose positions repeat every 7 intervals (ring length 7P against a canvas 5P tall). Imprints are drawn clipped to their canvas rect, so paint exists only on the support; rows slide through the clip and wrap around the ring unchanged.
- Drift: a pure function of loop phase. drift = 0.75P × (0.5 − 0.5·cos(2π·phase/36)): the view leaves rest with zero velocity, reaches its lowest point (three quarters of one interval down) at the loop's middle, and returns to rest exactly as the loop closes. All three files drift together — it is the viewpoint that moves, never the paint.
- Vignettes: no gradients (house renderer rule). Four stepped flat bands of wall tone at each end, alphas 0.50 / 0.32 / 0.18 / 0.08, each step 0.028 H deep, strongest at the edge and stepping away: across the head of the frame, and across the foot of the canvases just above the floor line. The steps are the softness.
- Sizing follows the house rules: Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on both visualViewport and window, canvas style width/height pinned in px inside draw() every frame, phone detection via window.screen physical size with the h > w×1.15 aspect fallback only. Page ground is the wall tone.
