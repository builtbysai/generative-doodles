# №1811 "The Sudoku Carillon" (PH4, PP1058, #237 paul-panhuysen)

Date: 2006 · 12 · 27. Gallery note: "Drag through a year of number pieces; on the hour, the panel plays. Drag only."

## What it is

Night at a bus stop in Eindhoven. The shelter is centered, its facade panels glow
with 9x9 number grids in warm white, red, yellow, blue and green against deep night
blues, streetlight orange washing in from both frame edges. The active panel sits
centered at eye level with a small date readout under it ("1 JANUARY", "DAY 1 OF 365").
A small BUS STOP sign hangs from the roof on the right. A bench stands below the
panel band; the dark street below carries a faint reflection of the panels.

## Interaction

Drag is the only control. Dragging horizontally scrubs the strip of 365 panels with
inertia; on release the strip settles to the nearest day. Drag is handled by
pointer and touch events on the canvas. There is no click-to-reload and no other
control. No music notes, no bell imagery; the light sequence is the carillon.

## The panels: seeded sudoku solver

Every panel is a valid 9x9 sudoku solution generated by a seeded backtracking
solver, seeded per day-of-year (mulberry32 seed 1811000 + day), never a random grid.
The solver tries digits in seeded shuffled order, cell by cell, with a step cap;
a pattern-plus-permutation fallback exists in the code but was never needed.
Panels are generated lazily on demand and cached, so only the visible days are
built at load.

Validity check, run against the generator extracted from the shipped index.html
(harness /tmp/check-sudoku-1811.js): all 365 panels checked, all 365 valid
(rows, columns and 3x3 blocks each contain 1-9 exactly once), all 365 distinct,
total generation time 178 ms. Day 1 grid:

Digit colors: 1 warm white, 2 red, 3 yellow, 4 blue, 5 green, 6 pale warm white,
7 deep red, 8 deep yellow, 9 deep blue. Digits are drawn in each cell in a
contrasting dark or light tone.

## The hour and the bell sequence

Accelerated clock: one in-piece hour every 90 seconds of piece time. The strike
starts at the top of each 90 s cycle and runs about 10 s: the 81 cells light in
row-major order (rows first, left to right, top to bottom, which is the
row/column/block order of the prompt), 0.11 s apart, each cell glowing for about
1.4 s, while the whole panel brightens slightly. Then the panel rests until the
next hour.

## Frame one

Fixed phase at t = 60 s, pre-drag, fixed day 1 (1 January). t = 60 sits mid-cycle
(60 mod 90 = 60), well after the strike that ended near t = 10 s and before the
next at t = 90 s, so the active panel is at rest between strikes: no cell glow,
no panel brightening, no fingertip, no drag offset. The animation loop continues
from t = 60, so the first frame the gate captures and the live first frame match.

## House rules

Visual Viewport sizing, resize listened to on visualViewport and window. Canvas
pinned in draw() via canvas.style.width/height in px. Phone detection via
window.screen (min dimension under 700 px) with aspect fallback. One synchronous
frame at load; requestAnimationFrame calls guarded by typeof checks so node-canvas
QA works. Canvas 2D only. Title, gallery date and hint plates drawn on canvas.
No em dashes in visible copy. No AI-signature copy. Deterministic: seeded RNG
only, no Date.now or Math.random anywhere in the frame path.

## Deviations from the prompt

None material. Two small adaptations, both within the prompt: the bell sequence
order is row-major (the prompt's "row/column/block order" read as rows first);
neighbor panels are drawn at 58 percent size and dimmed so the active panel stays
the focus, which is how a horizontal strip of panels reads at eye level.
