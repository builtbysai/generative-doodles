# No.322 Hide and Seek

After Debbie Sharman's hide and seek wallpaper, this piece fills the whole frame with a dense field of droopy petal daisies, gingko fans and filler leaves in stem green and leaf teal, with small cream daisy marks scattered through it, all on a deep teal ground. Exactly one hundred and forty field motifs make the ground, counted and asserted in the code. Hidden in the field are exactly seven butterflies, and planted around them are exactly nine decoys, three worms, three snails and three spiders, each drawn as a small flat doodle in the same greens and cream so the eye has to sort real wings from crawlies.

The butterflies hide by colour, never by outline or hint. Two sit easy, amber at full strength against the deep teal. Three sit mid, amber laid thin over teal linework so they blend toward the leaf colour around them. Two sit hard, drawn in lineweight only in the local field greens, with nothing to mark them out from the leaves beside them. No butterfly is outlined or highlighted before it is found. Tapping a hidden butterfly flashes it amber and leaves it revealed in full amber, and a small count in daisy cream appears low in the corner in the form of a number over seven. It is the only text in the piece, and it shows only after the first find. Tapping anywhere else, including on a worm, a snail or a spider, does nothing at all.

The piece carries five complete authored fields, each with its own hiding spots, decoy placements and scatter. A double tap advances to the next field in the rotation and resets the count to zero. Single taps are held for a short window before they resolve, so the two taps of a double tap are never mistaken for two separate seeks. At rest the piece runs a thirty six second seamless loop. The whole field breathes a few units on slow sine phases and the decoys inch along their short authored paths and back again, so the frame at thirty six seconds matches the frame at zero. The first frame opens with the field already dense and drifted, one decoy caught mid inch, and no butterfly revealed. All seven butterflies, all nine decoys and all one hundred and forty motifs in every field are counted and asserted in the code, and all marks are drawn directly on one canvas each frame from fixed seed tables.
