# No.979 The Flock Obeys

Study #120 daito-manabe, PP140 "discrete figures" (2018-2019): microdrones respond to a dancer's movements by rule, while a neighborhood-search system projects the closest archived pose into a rectangular on-stage frame.

## Concept

Three performers, three kinds of obedience. The dancer is a warm-white dot with a warm rim halo, dancing seeded choreography phrases at center-left: lunges, spins, freezes, glides. Around it, eight cyan drones fly as dancers, never as cameras: they orbit when it spins, scatter when it lunges, settle into a still ring when it freezes, and trail in a loose V when it glides. Each drone carries a tiny rotor-blur streak and a fading cyan trail ribbon, so the formation's recent past stays visible. At the right stands a tall hairline frame labeled "NEAREST POSE". Inside it, the dancer's mathematical doppelganger: a mint stick figure drawn from a library of 24 seeded poses. Twice a second the system summarizes the dancer's current state as a vector and the figure snaps to the nearest archived pose, with zero interpolation. The jump-cut is the mechanism: the database's answer is always a beat behind the body and never blends. Cyan is the machine, warm white is the human, mint is the record.

The only visible text is the frame label and the title. The palette is strict: stage #07080C, dancer #F5F2EA with rim #FFD88A, drones #4FD6FF with rotor #B8E6FF, frame #3A3D46, stick figure #7DF9C6, label #8A8D84.

## Construction

Choreography is closed-form. Time is divided into 120-second cycles; each cycle is a seeded move list (mulberry32) generated move by move so positions are continuous: GLIDE follows a quadratic Bezier to a new point over 5-8 s, LUNGE darts with an ease-out cubic over 1.4-2.2 s, SPIN whirls twice around its entry point over 4-6 s and lands back on it, FREEZE and HOLD stay put. Moves group into phrases of 2-4 for the melodic line. Cycles are built lazily and cached, each starting where the last ended. For cycle 0, generation retries with a bumped attempt seed until the move covering t = 20 s is a SPIN or a GLIDE, so frame one is guaranteed mid-phrase. The dancer's pose vector is (speed, spin, verticality, extension): speed from a numeric derivative of the closed-form position, spin peaking mid-SPIN, extension high for LUNGE, the move's own seeded value for FREEZE. The dancer's travel is bounded to the center-left of the stage so the performance never wanders under the pose frame.

The pose library is 24 forward-kinematic stick figures. Each pose is a parameter set (crouch, lean, two-segment arm and leg angles) with archetype tweaks every sixth pose (both arms up, one up one down, deep crouch, wide stance, kick and reach, arms crossed wide). Joints are computed once at build. Each pose's tag vector is derived from its own geometry: extension from hand and foot span, verticality from crouch and whether both feet leave the floor, speed and spin tags correlated with lean, extension, and a per-pose seeded jitter. Matching is plain Euclidean distance over the four components, recomputed every 60 sim steps (500 ms), and the result is drawn as-is. No lerp exists anywhere in the doppelganger path.

The flock is a fixed-timestep simulation at 120 Hz (dt = 1/120), seeded, pre-rolled exactly 2400 steps for frame one. Each drone steers toward a rule target with a spring-damper (gain 14, damping 4.2, acceleration and speed clamped), plus pairwise separation under 0.028 stage units. Targets are functions of the dancer's current move: SPIN gives a ring at radius 0.135 rotating at 2.2 rad/s; FREEZE and HOLD give a fixed ring at 0.125 with a small vertical bob; LUNGE gives per-drone scatter targets at radius 0.20 in directions seeded per lunge (biased away from the lunge line) and, at the moment a lunge starts, an outward impulse of 0.85 so the burst reads before the steering settles; GLIDE gives a V behind a smoothed dancer heading, one drone directly behind and the rest in widening rows. Trails record every fourth step, capped at 66 points (about 2.2 s), drawn as per-segment strokes with alpha ramping to 0.5.

Interaction arrives as timestamped events consumed in step order. A tap queues a drone launch 0.32 s of piece time later (up to 16 drones); a second tap inside 320 ms retracts queued launches and queues a reseed instead, which rebuilds the library and choreography from a bumped seed and re-runs the 2400-step pre-roll. Dragging switches the flock's anchor to the visitor's pointer (an orbiting ring around it) while held and for 10 s after release, while the dancer keeps dancing alone; then the flock returns.

Sound is lazy WebAudio, created only inside the first pointer gesture and fully typeof-guarded: a low filtered-noise stage bed, a sawtooth rotor-whir bus whose pitch follows the flock's mean height, a quiet pentatonic note at each phrase start, a rising sweep on formation-type changes, and a tiny square click each time the doppelganger's pose actually changes. Nothing in the visual path depends on audio; the QA renders are silent and complete.

Layout follows the house rules: canvas sized from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on both visualViewport and window, canvas style width and height pinned in pixels on every draw, phone detection from window.screen's physical size (min dimension under 700 px) with the h > w * 1.15 aspect as fallback only. On phones the pose frame narrows to 26% of the width but stays at the right; the stage scales beneath it. No gradients, no shadowBlur, no ctx.filter, no offscreen canvas anywhere. Frame one is the synchronous draw after the pre-roll; no wall clock feeds any drawn state (the live loop's rAF timestamps and event timeStamps pace gestures only).

## Deviations

- Sound simplification, as briefed: the rotor whirs are one shared bus whose pitch follows the flock's mean height rather than one oscillator per drone. The bed, phrase melody, snap click, and formation sweep are as specced.
- The single-tap drone launch is delayed 0.32 s of piece time so a double-tap can retract it before it lands; this keeps tap and double-tap unambiguous without any visual difference beyond the launch arriving a beat later.
- Pose-tag vectors are partly derived from pose geometry and partly seeded jitter, rather than measured from real pose data; the library is an archive of plausible dance poses generated for this piece, which is the self-contained reading of the original's database.
