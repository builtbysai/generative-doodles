# No.196 Mock Back

## Concept
Maude, Amanda Willshire's voice-interactive mockingbird, mocks back what visitors say to her worm friends, mixed with tidbits from what earlier visitors said. This telling keeps her mechanism and drops the microphone entirely, as her own prompt allows: taps ARE the voice. Five little worm stations arc across the bottom of a black field. Tap them in a rhythm and you have spoken a phrase; Maude repeats it back transformed, then answers with a stranger's older phrase from her memory. The longer you play, the richer her collage of ghosts.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page, no audio anywhere. Maude is built from hard flat polygons only, never smoothed: an orange fan tail, a hot pink body and head block, a teal wing with two notch lines, a yellow crown cap, a two-blade orange beak that opens while she performs, one ground-coloured eye dot, and the signature dull aluminum belly panel (#9AA0A8) with three rivets, a nod to the 1970s plane belly. Her legs and perch are drawn level, as furniture; only her body leans in when a performance runs. The worms are five-segment undulating glyphs, bright #7CFC00 when they hold a phrase in memory, dimmed #3E6B1F when empty. Input is phrase based: taps on worms within 1.6 seconds of each other accumulate as one phrase of {dt, worm} events; 1.5 seconds after the last tap the phrase closes, joins a session buffer capped at 40 phrases (oldest evicted), and she performs it twice over: first the mock, your pattern replayed at 0.92 speed and pitch-shifted, drawn as note-ribbons arcing from her beak to above the source worm with the target riding 46px higher; then a ghost, a randomly sampled older phrase played stuttered (28 percent of notes gated out, some repeated at +0.1s) at a lower pitch offset. Ribbons are quadratic curves drawn progressively over 0.3s with a soft glow pass, a note head and stem at the travelling tip, coloured by source worm from the palette. Double-tap Maude and she performs a six second greatest hits: fragments of two or three buffered phrases, concatenated at 0.8 speed with random pitch offsets. Left alone for twenty seconds she replays a random memory to herself, the attract loop that teaches the mechanism. The buffer is seeded with four ghost phrases at load, so the piece opens with strangers already in her memory and one ribbon already mid-flight, on a long diagonal to a far worm. Palette exactly per spec. No text, no labels, no hint. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen, rAF typeof guarded.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three. The bird read immediately at every size: loud flat blocks, crown and beak crisp, belly panel with rivets, five worm clusters spaced along their arc, the seeded ghosts making three of the worms bright and two dim. Two faults in the first pass: the performance lean rotated the whole bird including her perch, so the furniture slanted like a sinking shelf; and the frame-one ribbon, aimed at whichever worm the first seeded phrase happened to start on, dropped almost vertically from the beak and read as a string. Fixes: legs and perch are now drawn outside the lean transform, and frame one selects the seeded phrase whose first note flies the longest diagonal, with a soft glow pass added under each ribbon. Re-rendered: perch dead level, Maude leaning into a bright green ribbon that sails from her beak to the far right worm, note head landed just above it. On the phone frame the bird holds the left third, the ribbon still diagonals the full height, and the worm arc reads as five distinct clusters. The phrase recording, stutter and scramble transforms and the attract timer are interaction driven and cannot appear in a still; they were verified by tracing the finalize gate (1.5s after last tap), the buffer cap and eviction, and the performance pruning against the spec.
