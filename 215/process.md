# No.215 Debris Sun

## Concept
David Bowen's SPACEJUNK tilts a field of plant-like mechanisms toward the oldest piece of space debris passing overhead, tracking junk nobody can see with bodies everybody can. This piece keeps that devotion and its discretion. Fifty twigs stand in the dark on a jittered grid in the lower two thirds of the frame, bark-brown, a few broken side-branch nubs on each, all leaning in exactly the same direction like sunflowers straining at one sun. The sun is a simulated ephemeris: a fixed catalog of twelve objects on circular orbits with differing periods and inclinations, launched between 1958 and 1972, and the piece always tracks the oldest one currently above the horizon, marking it only as a tiny dim amber point high in the frame. When it sets, every twig eases down to a resting downward lean and waits for the next oldest to rise. A full tracking event lasts a minute or two. A double tap advances to the next object early. There are no orbit lines, no rings, no labels, no stars: twigs, marker, void.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Object position is angle over time; elevation is the sine of that angle scaled by inclination, azimuth its cosine, and the tracked object is the first catalog entry, oldest first, whose elevation clears the horizon. The shared lean target is the arctangent of azimuth against elevation, clamped; at rest it is straight down. Each twig chases the target through its own critically damped spring with a per-twig rate between 5.5 and 11, so the field arrives together but never in robotic lockstep, and a two-component tremor rides on every shaft: plants, not servos. Twigs are drawn as two tapered passes, #5C4A33 base to #7A6244 tip, with two nub strokes and a soft shadow at the foot. The marker is a small #D8B46A point with a faint radial breath, drawn only while its object is up. Double tap jumps the piece's private sky clock to just before the next catalog rise. Frame one opens mid-tracking, the 1958 object high, all fifty twigs already settled into their unison lean with the tremor on. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. One fault caught before the first render: the pre-settle pass sampled the lean target thirty seconds in the past, which for the opening object lands on the mirror side of the sky, so frame one would have shown the whole field leaning away from its sun; the springs now settle against the same instant the first frame draws. The final renders show exactly the intended stillness: fifty twigs in unison toward the upper left, one dim amber point above them, and nothing else in the dark.
