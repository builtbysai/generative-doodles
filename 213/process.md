# No.213 Fly Brain

## Concept
David Bowen's fly drawing device puts houseflies in a chamber and lets their motion steer a CNC plotter: the insects are the brain, the machine is the dumb body, the drawing is the record. This piece keeps his mapping exactly. On warm paper, left, sits an acrylic sphere of about a hundred flies, flocking, clustering, bursting apart. Right, a sheet accumulates the carving of a small red carriage. Each frame the swarm's dominant mode is read: if one fly is isolated far from its nearest neighbour, the carriage follows that fly and cuts a hairline; otherwise it follows the centroid of the densest cluster on a coarse grid, and the stroke width scales with the cluster's depth, hairline scratches against deep gouges. After about three minutes the sheet scrolls away intact and a fresh one starts. A double tap inside the sphere reseeds the swarm and the sheet together. There are no trails inside the sphere; the only record is the sheet.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The flies are a boids-lite system of exactly one hundred agents: separation, cohesion, weak alignment, a slow lure point roaming the sphere's interior that the swarm condenses on, air drag, and a soft containment field inside the glass with a true bounce only at the rim; collective startles fire at random intervals. Density is measured on a 6 by 6 grid over the sphere; the isolated fly is found by nearest-neighbour distance. The carriage lerps toward its target in sheet-normalised coordinates and emits stroke segments into a plain JS list with per-segment width, capped and scrolled per the three minute rule; no offscreen canvas anywhere. The sphere is a thin circle of #9FB8C4 at low alpha with a white specular arc; the carriage is a small #8A2A1A marker riding the record's head with a faint gantry thread back to the sphere. Frame one pre-runs the simulation fifty seconds at build time, so the flies open mid-cluster and the sheet already carries a developed wandering carving. On phones the sphere stacks above the sheet. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three, over three rounds. Round one was blank but for a red speck in the corner: the pre-run executed before the first resize, with zero width and height, so the sphere had radius zero and every coordinate went NaN; the pre-run now runs after resize, and the sphere geometry was also tightened so it clears the sheet's edge at square sizes. Round two developed but wrongly: the swarm milled into a ring on the glass wall and the carriage traced a giant circle that filled the sheet edge to edge, so the flies gained drag, soft containment, weaker alignment and the roaming lure, and the drawing became what the piece promises: wandering carved lines, thick where the cluster pressed, hairline where a loner flew, the carriage at its head. Final renders hold at all three sizes.
