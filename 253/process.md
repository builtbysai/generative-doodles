# No.253 Dandelion Ball

## Concept
After the Nov 2020 particle study by tanzinchospel.art: a dandelion seed head of soft pastel strands, blue, white and pink, floating on near black with a thin stem below. The ball sways gently in a low wind and sheds the odd seed upward. Drag across it to shake it: a burst of seeds lets go and drifts off, and the head regrows. About sixty percent of the frame stays empty black, as in his render.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, no WebGL, the head built as data and drawn directly every frame. Exactly 2600 strands stand on a fibonacci sphere, each a short curved stroke from a small core to its tip, coloured from the pastel trio (blue 40 percent, white 35 percent, pink 25 percent), alpha weighted by depth, the front strands tipped with a three-hair seed tuft. A low-frequency wind field bends the tips; the whole head sways and rolls a few degrees on slow sines. Detached strands become seed particles with a five-hair parachute and a hair stem, buoyant, wind-blown, fading over six to nine seconds. The head sheds a seed every half second or so on its own. A drag injects velocity into a shake spring that knocks the whole head, and strands near a fast drag path let go in a burst; shaken strands wait two and a half to six seconds before regrowing over four, so a hard shake reads as the ball regrowing over about ten seconds. Composition is contain-fitted in a square design space, centred, the near-black ground filling the rest of the screen. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, pointer coordinates mapped back through the view transform, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. Frame one shows the full head with seven seeds already airborne above it, the stem running to the bottom edge, the black field empty around it, on desktop and phone alike. The drag itself cannot be exercised by the headless renderer, which fires no pointer events; the shake path was reviewed in code, its thresholds (a drag faster than 260 design units per second releases strands within 120 units of the path, capped per move) set against the spring constants so a deliberate swipe sheds a visible burst without emptying the head.
