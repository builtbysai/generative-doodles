# No.279 Apprentice

## Concept
After Njalikwa Chongwe's father-daughter lineage of pots: a large master vessel at the left in terracotta, and to its right a family of smaller vessels in cobalt, emerald and ochre, each child inheriting the master's profile with a slight mutation of form and colour, like a family tree drifting across the generations. Thin cream lines join each parent's shoulder to its child's shoulder and pulse softly while the whole row sways gently. A tap anywhere grows one new generation from the current youngest vessels. At most five generations stay active; when a sixth would grow, the oldest child generation fades out over about a second and its grandchildren are orphaned from their lines. Frame one opens with the master plus two generations already grown, so the lineage reads immediately.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred fit so the row keeps its shape at any aspect, with the near-black ground as the page ground. The palette is held to the six specced colours only: ground #0B0B0D, master terracotta #B85C2E, children cobalt #2E4EA3, emerald #2E8B62 and ochre #C98F2D, and connecting lines in cream #EFE6D2. Each vessel is a lathe side profile built from four profile params, [belly, neck, height, foot], where belly, neck and foot are half widths held as fractions of the vessel's own height. The master stands 360 design pixels tall, 40 percent of the frame height, at the leftmost of five generation columns spaced across the frame; a column with two vessels sets them above and below the centre line. A child is its parent scaled down by a lineage factor of 0.87 per generation so the family reads smaller as it moves right, then every param is mutated by the specced rule, scaled by (1 + gaussian(0, 0.08)) from a seeded Box-Muller stream, and clamped to pot-like ranges: belly fraction 0.20 to 0.34, neck 0.09 to 0.19, foot 0.10 to 0.21, height 110 to 360. The silhouette is drawn from twelve knots of height fraction against half width, smoothstep interpolated and sampled at 56 steps per side, with an explicit horizontal segment across the foot so the base sits flat, and a ground coloured mouth ellipse at the rim. Colour inheritance steps one place inside the child palette each generation: the master's two children start at cobalt and emerald, and later children advance one step from their parent's colour, cycling cobalt, emerald, ochre. The master makes two children; later generations make one child per parent, so each column holds at most two vessels and nothing overlaps. Connecting lines are stroked at 1.5 pixels in cream with alpha 0.16 plus 0.22 times a slow sine pulse phased per child, multiplied by the fade alpha of both parent and child generations, and drawn under the vessels. New generations fade in over 0.8 seconds. Sway is a horizontal sine offset of 4 pixels per vessel on its own seeded phase. The opening clock starts at 4 seconds so frame one opens with the sway and pulse already mid cycle. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seed, no audio, no text, no offscreen canvas and no filters or shadowBlur.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported the pointerdown handler plus both resize listeners, and a grep confirms exactly the six specced palette colours, no shadowBlur, no gradients and no em dashes. One fix. The first render's vessels had lumpy, stacked profiles and feet that sloped a few pixels across the base: the profile used only eight widely spaced knots, so the smoothstep easing bulged at each knot, and the path ran straight from the bottom left corner up the right side, tilting the foot. The knot set was expanded to twelve points with gentler width steps and the path now runs horizontally across the foot before rising, the same correction recorded for No.275. Re-rendered and re-inspected at all three sizes: feet sit flat, profiles read as smooth believable pots, and the final frame one reads as specced: the terracotta master at the left at 40 percent of frame height, cobalt and emerald children in the second column, emerald and ochre grandchildren in the third, thin cream lines linking every parent to its children, the right of the frame left open for the generations a tap will grow. On the phone the centred fit keeps all five vessels in frame with the lines still legible and nothing clipped. No further fixes were needed.
