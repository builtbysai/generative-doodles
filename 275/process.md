# No.275 Zigongo Bands

## Concept
After Njalikwa Chongwe's banded vessels: a single pot in side profile, centred on a near-black ground, built from stacked horizontal bands of flat colour: coral red, cobalt and emerald separated by thin black zigzag divider lines, with one cream band in the repeating stack. The bands slide slowly upward as if the vessel is being pulled from clay, rising exactly one band height per loop and wrapping from the top back to the bottom, while the profile itself breathes: the belly swells and the neck narrows on the same slow cycle. Watch only: there is no interaction, the pull and the breath are the whole event.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred fit so the vessel keeps its shape at any aspect, with the near-black ground as the page ground. The palette is held to the six specced colours only: the near-black ground, coral, cobalt, emerald, divider black and cream. The vessel stands 720 design pixels tall, 80 percent of the frame height, centred at x 640 with its rim at y 90 and its foot at y 810. Its silhouette is a piecewise lathe width function: fourteen knots of height fraction against half width, smoothstep interpolated and sampled at 72 steps per side, giving a narrow neck, a swelling shoulder, a wide belly at about three fifths of the height and a settled foot. Breathing scales the width by 1 plus 0.04 times a 24 second sine times a weight function that is plus one at the belly and minus one at the neck, so the belly swells as the neck narrows and the two trade places half a loop later. The bands are horizontal strips 90 pixels tall, clipped to the profile path and filled flat, colours cycling coral, cobalt, emerald, cream in a fixed order; the cream band therefore appears twice in the eight band stack. The strip offset is (T / 24 mod 1) times one band height, so the whole stack rises one band height per 24 second loop and wraps seamlessly at the boundary. Each band boundary carries a divider: a triangle wave polyline with a 12 pixel peak to peak throw and a 22 pixel step, given a slight seeded wobble from a fixed seed, stroked in divider black at 5 pixels and clipped to the profile so it stops at the silhouette edge. Flat fills only: no gradients, no crackle, no glow. The opening clock starts at 6 seconds, a quarter of the loop, so frame one opens with the bands already displaced 22.5 pixels from their rest stack and the belly at full swell. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no interaction handlers, no audio, no text, no offscreen canvas and no filters or shadowBlur.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported resize listeners only, and a grep confirms no interaction handlers, no shadowBlur, no gradients and exactly the six specced palette colours in the script. One fix. The first render's foot was cut on a diagonal: the profile path ran straight from the bottom left corner up to the right side's second point, so the bottom edge sloped about 7 pixels across the foot and the vessel read as tilted. A pixel measurement confirmed the centre held at x 640 on every row while the bottom edge varied by column. The path now runs horizontally across the foot at the base before rising up the right side; re-rendered and re-measured, the bottom edge sits flat at the same row across the whole foot and the span is symmetric about the centre. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: an upright banded vessel at 80 percent of frame height on the near-black ground, the stack running coral, cobalt, emerald, cream twice with a wrapped coral sliver at the foot showing the quarter loop displacement, black zigzag dividers at every boundary stopping at the silhouette edge, flat fills throughout. On the phone the centred fit keeps the whole vessel in frame with the bands and zigzags still legible and nothing clipped. No further fixes were needed.
