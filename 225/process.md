# No.225 Messy Desk

## Concept
McTavish's Thought Machine set out to render visible an awkward, industrious attempt to read and think and write: a messy desk near her office, a cogitation machine under construction, on the principle that messy thinking is what makes us human. This piece is that desk on kraft paper. Thirteen fragments lie in deliberate clutter, denser toward the edges: note-cards, print-out fragments, two highlighter-yellow stickies, one highlighter swipe across a line, coffee rings and hand-drawn arrows underneath. The card texts are part of the piece, written for it, terse and strange ("remember: the machine is not the music", "buy thread. red. more red.", "unsent: dear machine, thank you"). A reader cursor, a small ink ring, wanders the desk on its own: it travels to a fragment, underlines it in red, ties it with a red thread curve to the last fragment it read, pauses, and thinks again. The thread holds the last few readings. Drag a fragment and it follows the hand; release it and it snaps back elastically, unless the thread currently holds it, in which case the drop becomes its new home.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Cards are JS objects with home positions as fractions, small rotations, kinds (card, print-out, sticky) and text lines; homes are converted to clamped pixel positions on resize so every fragment stays on the desk at any size, and phones get a dedicated portrait arrangement of the same thirteen fragments in a loose two-column scatter. The cursor is a four-state machine (wander at a fixed travel speed, underline over three quarters of a second, thread as the new curve eases in, pause) and keeps at most four thread curves, drawn as quadratics between current card centres so threads follow dragged cards. Displaced fragments integrate an underdamped spring (stiffness 110, damping 8.5) for a genuine elastic overshoot; fragments in the current thread set skip the spring and adopt the drop point as home. Frame one pre-seeds two finished thread arcs and then pre-runs the simulation until the cursor is caught mid-underline, so the page opens mid-thought at every size. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The desktop and thumb frames were right early: clutter with intent, rings and arrows under the cards, the reader mid-underline with its red line half drawn. The phone frame took two rounds. First the fraction layout, compressed into 390 pixels, buried several cards under each other and ran text past the frame edges; clamped pixel homes fixed the edges. Then the portrait arrangement replaced the compressed landscape one, with slightly larger cards, and the final phone render shows all thirteen fragments legible in their two loose columns, threads arcing between them and the cursor at work. No clean grid anywhere; every mark feels placed.
