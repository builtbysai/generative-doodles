# No.282 Basin Rings

## Concept
After Njalikwa Chongwe's top-down basin: concentric glaze rings seen from directly above, an ochre rim, a broad celadon ring and a dark-red centre pool, with a fine crackle over everything, turning with glacial slowness like a kiln seen from above. Each ring turns at its own pace while the crackle holds still. The piece is watch-only: nothing to tap, drag or read, and nothing ever reloads.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred-fitted so the square basin keeps its shape in the middle of the frame, with the near-black as the page ground. The palette is held to the five specced colours only: ochre, celadon, dark red, the crackle brown-black at low alpha, and the ground. The rings fill 90 percent of the frame height, an outer radius of 405 around the design centre. Three curated seeds (282101, 282207, 282313) each generate a ring set with the same grammar: an ochre rim band, a broad celadon band, alternating middle bands in the three glaze colours including one thin dark-red band, and a dark-red centre pool. Band thicknesses jitter a few pixels per seed, so a crossfade visibly re-forms the rings while the grammar stays. Neighbouring bands share each boundary radius exactly, so no ground ever shows between bands. The glaze-bleed feather at each boundary is layered translucency only: thin strokes straddling the join in the colours of the two bands that meet there, a couple of pixels each side, with a third fainter stroke outside. The centre pool is never perfectly symmetric: a centred red disc fills the middle first, then the pool disc itself is laid over it in the same red with its centre shifted a little up and to the right per seed, so the pool bulges slightly off centre and the celadon ring around it varies in thickness. Each band carries a handful of short arc marks inside it, in palette colours at low alpha, and those marks carry the rotation: every band has its own slow angular speed, alternating in sign and differing in magnitude, so the rings drift against each other barely perceptibly. Ring angles run on the continuous clock, so the turn never jumps. The crackle is a fine seeded network held purely as polyline data, about 300 short random-walk polylines in two scales, clipped to the basin disc and drawn fixed at low alpha over everything each frame: it never rotates with the rings and never crossfades with the sets. No offscreen canvas, no gradients, no filters or shadowBlur, no ripple and no shine. The loop is 48 seconds in three 16 second segments, one per seed: each set holds alone for 6 seconds, then crossfades over the remaining 10 seconds into the next set, the incoming set drawn over the outgoing one at a smoothstepped alpha, and the third set crossfades back into the first so the loop is seamless. Frame one opens at 3 seconds, mid-hold of the first set, with the rings fully formed and the crackle already over them. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with resize listeners on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no text, no hint, no pointer handlers, no audio, no em dashes.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported both resize listeners and no pointer listeners, and a grep confirms exactly the five specced palette colours and no em dashes. Two fixes during QA. First render: each band edge carried its own slight wobble, so neighbouring edges mismatched and thin black ground crescents showed at some boundaries, and the offset pool left an uncovered dark crescent on its far side. The bands were rebuilt on shared exact boundary radii, the crackle walks were shortened and made finer, and the pool gained its centred under-disc, then the piece re-rendered. Second fix: the pool feather was first drawn as a circle around the offset pool centre, which read as a faint arc inside the pool, and one long pool mark read the same way; the feather moved to centred strokes at the pool boundary drawn over the fill, and the pool marks were shortened and faded. Final frame one reads as specced at all three sizes: a full basin of ochre, celadon and dark-red rings filling the frame, the red pool a little off centre, fine crackle over everything, no gaps, no text. On the phone the centred fit sets the basin in the middle of the tall frame on the ground colour and the rings stay legible. No further fixes were needed.
