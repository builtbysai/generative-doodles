# No.174 Self-Organizing Swarm

## Concept
The one Fathauer work meant to be touched: his self organizing tiling toys. About 120 flat tiles, equilateral triangles in both polarities, squares and hexagons, in OpArt primaries on warm white, resting in neat edge to edge clusters. Drag through them and they scatter like a shaken frame; let go and they drift back and re cluster into tilings, colours settling into bands. No text or hint anywhere in the piece; the gallery note carries the cue.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The settled layout is generated per seed as a set of exact tilings: a honeycomb patch of flat top hexagons, two or three triangle fields clipped from a triangular lattice (both up and down cells, so both polarities, coloured in row bands), blocks of squares, a hex flower (one hexagon with a triangle on every edge), a centre plus ring honeycomb, and loose singles in the gaps. Separate placement tables are used for landscape and portrait screens so the field fills either frame. A hard trim keeps the count at the 120 tile physics cap, removing loose singles first. Each tile is a light rigid body: position and angle are offsets from its home, pulled back by weak springs (the magnets), damped, with circle collider separation between neighbours so a disturbed field re settles into clean edge to edge clusters instead of an overlap mess. Dragging applies the pointer's velocity and a radial push to tiles within reach, with a spin term; double tap reseeds a new layout and bursts the field. Untouched, the field stirs itself once per 60 second cycle and settles again. First frame is the fully settled layout, no motion. Palette exactly per spec: ground #F5F3EC, red #D92B2B, blue #1E4FD8, yellow #F2C230, white #FFFFFF, 2px outlines #1A1A1A. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, touch-action none on the canvas so dragging never scrolls the page.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three; the harness draws the settled state, which is the specified first frame. The first layout scattered small clusters across an oversized field and read as islands; worse, the triangle field density was underestimated (a triangular lattice holds about 2.31 cells per unit area), the build overshot the cap by two to one, and the safety trim gutted the corner clusters. The layout was rebuilt with calibrated field radii (about 7.3 cells per unit of radius squared), a tighter placement table per orientation, and a bottom left triangle field added to balance the frame. Final renders: all clusters present and exact, banding visible in every cluster, both triangle polarities present, no stray text.
