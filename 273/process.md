# No.273 Crackle

## Concept
After Shupiwe Chongwe's naked-raku experiments: a field of dark ceramic spheres and ovoids resting on a pale ground like eggs in a nest of light, each wrapped in a unique voronoi crackle network with tan veining, cooling-rate made visible. Eight spheres and ovoids of varied sizes are scattered across the lower two-thirds of the frame, with generous pale space above them, and each sphere's crackle density is different: dense where it cooled fast, sparse where it cooled slow. The spheres themselves never move. A tap re-fires the kiln: the crackle networks regenerate with new densities and vein paths, crossfading over about four seconds with staggered timing per sphere, like cooling ceramic. Watch only between taps.

## Construction
Canvas 2D in a 1280 by 900 design space, centred fit on the pale ground, which is also the page ground so the letterbox and the scene are one continuous field. The palette is held to the four specced colours only: raku black, crackle tan, pale ground and contact shadow. Each sphere is a circle or ellipse silhouette in raku black over a contact shadow of two flat ellipses in the shadow colour, a wide faint one and a tighter stronger one, with no blur. Its crackle network is computed as data: seed points are scattered in the unit disc from a fixed seed, each seed's voronoi cell is clipped out by successive perpendicular-bisector half-planes against every other seed, and the cell edges falling inside the disc are stored as polyline segments and drawn directly each frame in crackle tan at low width, clipped to the silhouette. The per-sphere cooling parameter sets the seed count, about 11 cells for the slowest-cooled sphere to about 29 for the fastest. Roundness comes from a soft wash of crackle tan at low alpha high on the body, a fainter bounce low on it, and a highlight arc in crackle tan at low alpha hugging the upper left rim. A re-fire settles any fade still running, draws a new cooling rate per sphere from a seeded generator, builds a new vein set per sphere, and crossfades old to new with each sphere's fade starting 0.34 seconds after the previous one and lasting 1.6 seconds, so the last sphere settles about four seconds after the tap. The tap is a deliberate press and release in much the same place within 700ms, detected on pointerdown and pointerup with coordinates converted to design space through the stored centred-fit transform; nothing ever reloads. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, synchronous first frame with the first firing fully cooled and readable, rAF typeof guarded, fixed seeds, no text, no offscreen canvas and no filters or shadowBlur.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three; the renderer reported the two resize listeners plus canvas pointerdown and window pointerup, and a grep confirms exactly the four specced palette colours in the script and no shadowBlur, no offscreen canvas and no em dashes. One fix after the first pass: the eight spheres sat in two fairly regular rows, so their centres were re-staggered in height (the tall ovoid raised, two front spheres lowered) until the group reads as a scatter across the lower two-thirds rather than a formation; re-rendered and re-inspected at all three sizes. Final frame one reads as specced: eight black spheres and ovoids on pale ground under generous pale space, crackle density clearly varying from the dense network on the large central sphere to the sparse one on the top right sphere, contact shadows settled under every piece. On the phone the centred fit keeps the whole scatter in frame at once on the matching pale ground, small but complete, with the crackle still legible on the larger spheres. No further fixes were needed.
