# No.315 Ditzy Field

After Coral Cockatoo's ditzy prints: a full field of teeny gum blossoms on cream paper, printed in vermilion only, like a screen compressed to its smallest scale for studs. Two colours in the whole piece, the paper and the one ink, nothing else.

The field holds exactly one motif species, asserted in the code. Every mark is the same gum blossom glyph, the blossom reduced to its print silhouette: an operculum cap dome with its little knob, a cup at the heart, and a radial burst of stamens tipped with dots. No petals anywhere. The glyphs sit on a seeded jittered scatter with a different rotation for each one, never a uniform grid, and the scatter runs past the edges of the frame so the field is full-bleed at any aspect.

At rest the field breathes on a thirty second seamless loop. A slow swell wave crosses diagonally, lifting the motifs near its crest from ditzy scale toward full size and letting them settle as it passes, and the thirtieth second matches the first exactly. Frame one opens with the wave already mid-field, so part of the field is visibly swelling at load.

Pressing and dragging works like a cutter pressed into clay. The motifs under the finger swell on a local spring to full size while the rest of the field stays at its own scale, then ease back down when the pointer lifts. The swell is always local, never a zoom of the whole field. Nothing reloads. The field is plain canvas 2D path data redrawn each frame on a single canvas, with all positions and rotations from a fixed seed.
