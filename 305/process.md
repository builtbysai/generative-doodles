# No.305 Flash Sheet Twelve

After Ali Fisher's twelve print mix and match series, the sheet gathers twelve of her recurring motifs onto one page of aged paper: a Rosie style cowboy, Japanese cranes, Pharaoh's horses, a parrot, butterflies, a classic panther, a Victorian gift card border piece, a snake, a croc, a willy wagtail, a banksia pod and a mermaid. They sit in a three by four grid of twelve cells with thin ink rules between them and generous paper margins, drawn in hand linework with flat colour in vermilion, teal, mustard and dusty rose against the ink.

Each cell carries a small label underneath its motif. The labels are not set in a font at all. Every letter is a short stroke polyline drawn from a fixed table of hand lettered forms, so the words sit in the same ink as the drawings and wobble with the same hand.

The sheet is a toy as much as a print. Dragging a cell onto another slot swaps the pair, both sliding across the paper on an eased path while the arrangement is held as plain data. Tapping a cell lifts it slightly by translation alone, with no shadow faked or real, and the motif inside performs its one signature move: the snake coils tighter, the butterflies flap twice, the parrot ruffles, the mermaid flicks her tail, the croc works its jaw, the wagtail pumps, and each of the other six answers with its own authored motion. On the first frame the sheet rests in its authored order with the butterflies already mid flap, so the page reads as alive before it is touched.

The palette is held to the aged paper, the ink, and the four flat colours named above, with nothing else introduced. All motion runs from fixed seeds and fixed timing, so the sheet behaves the same way on every visit.
