# №9049 · The Shadow of One Letter - process notes

## Concept
Seed 397 (from the 2026-10-03 Dom Sylvester Houedard study, Study B): a wireframe solid drawn in a second voice, red and hand-drawn, casting a shadow built from exactly one repeated glyph. The shadow's edge and density do the drawing. The rule is absolute: a second glyph anywhere in the field kills the piece, because the eye starts reading characters again and stops reading light.

## Construction
- One glyph only: the middle dot `·`, set in Georgia. No other character appears in the shadow field.
- The solid: cube, icosahedron, or octahedron (seeded pick), projected with mild perspective, drawn in oxide red (#b03a28) with hand-wobbled edges (3 segments per edge, small random offsets) and anchor dots at vertices.
- The shadow: a skewed elliptical field under the solid. Glyph size and alpha follow local darkness: dense dark umbra core under the solid's mass, ragged penumbra edge from a low-frequency noise modulation. Jittered placement keeps it from going mechanical.
- Default seed 42 (the cube): the clean geometry against the organic shadow is the strongest contrast. Click for another casting; ?seed= shareable.

## Why this variant won
Four seeds compared. The icosahedron versions were busy, the wireframe competing with the shadow's texture. The cube (seed 42) lets the two voices stay separate: red geometry above, tonal glyph field below. The shadow reads as light, not as text.

## Concept family
"one-glyph tonal shadow": a wireframe object in a drawn voice casting a shadow rendered as a density field of exactly one repeated glyph. Checked against the ledger before building; no prior piece uses a glyph-field shadow.
