# No.209 Jiggle Choir

## Concept
Asia Ward's harvested toy creatures live by four verbs: jiggle, shuffle, squeak, recoil. Shown as a group they become a choir that never quite sings together. Five small patchwork creatures, variations on the toy-mashup grammar with different fabric swatches, different limb counts and one single big glass eye among them, each run the verb state machine on their own clock, so the group twitches, wanders and squeaks out of phase. Every forty five seconds or so they all squeak at once, rings overlapping, before drifting apart again. Their tempo is tele-driven by the time of day: livelier near midday, drowsy in the small hours. Tap a creature for a solo squeak; drag through the group to scatter them, and they shuffle back into formation.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. One parameterised creature builder draws every singer fresh each frame from its parameter set, five fabric leads taken in order from the spec palette with the second fabric rotated per creature, dashed #D8CFA8 stitch seams on every part, radial-gradient glass eyes with white catchlights, and arms counted per creature from one to three. Each creature owns a precomputed verb timeline in the manner of No.203, seeded per creature with its own phase offset, so frame one opens mid-choir with everyone in a different verb. The piece clock itself runs scaled by a tempo multiplier computed once from the local hour, 1.0 near midday falling to 0.3 at night. Chorus moments are windows on the same clock: inside a window every creature squeaks at once. Scatter is a spring impulse system over per-creature offsets, with a hopping bob while a creature is travelling home, which reads as the shuffle gait. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, creatures scaled down on phones but kept in the middle band, rAF typeof guarded, no text anywhere.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The anchor creature opens mid-squeak with its rings already expanding, the one-eyed creature stands at the right, and the five fabric sets are clearly distinct at desktop and thumbnail size. One build fault was caught before rendering: the return-home bob was computed but never applied to the creature geometry, so scattered creatures would have glided flat; the bob is now part of the geometry used by both drawing and hit testing. Mobile keeps all five in frame in the middle band with margins of black around them.
