# No.304 Tile Trial Grid

After Ali Fisher's sgraffito tile trials. Twenty handmade tiles sit in a four by five grid on a dark timber ground, each tile a little irregular, its corners and edges pushed a few pixels out of true so no two are alike and none is digitally perfect. Hairline grout gaps separate them.

Every tile has a crackle glaze ground, drawn as a fine jittered mesh of thin dark lines clipped inside the tile shape, like the web of cracks in a fired glaze. Most tiles are white crackle, a few carry her gloop crackle tints in celadon and amber. The crackle is fully drawn from the first frame.

Each tile is carved with one motif from a vocabulary of exactly four: a flower, a bird, a skull and a banksia pod. The carving is done with data only, on a single canvas. Each motif is a stroke path in the clay colour, drawn over the glaze with a slightly wider dark line beneath it, so the mark reads as a groove cut through the glaze to the clay body underneath. The twenty tiles open with a varied mix, five of each motif across the grid.

One corner tile, the top left one, is cracked and mended. A jagged gold seam runs across it with one short branch, the only gold in the piece, in the spirit of a careful repair that shows its join instead of hiding it.

Tapping a tile re-carves it. The old motif gives way to the next one in the cycle, flower to bird to skull to banksia pod and back to flower, never a random choice. The new motif arrives as a clay coloured reveal that travels along its own stroke, as if the cutting tool were moving across the tile in real time. Dragging shifts the whole tabletop a little, up to about twenty eight design pixels in any direction, and on release it eases back home. Nothing reloads.

Left alone, the piece keeps working slowly. Every twenty seconds one tile grows its crackle by a single extra crack segment. The order is a fixed seeded rotation through all twenty tiles, worked out in advance, so the piece develops the same way on every play and no chance is involved at runtime.

The palette is held to seven colours only: the clay body, white crackle, celadon, amber, the carved dark line, the timber ground and the gold of the one mended seam. There is no text in the piece.
