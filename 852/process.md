# No.852 — Cover Your Ears to Hear

EXPANSION #104 laurie-anderson, PP13 "The Hands Table" (the Handphone Table, 1978: sound conducted through a wooden table into the listener's elbows and skull; the body is the speaker). Title suggestion shipped as the piece title.

## Concept

A dark screen and a long wooden table in profile across the lower third. Two worn elbow spots glow softly with bone-white rests. Nothing else happens. The piece is an instrument waiting: it is silent, visually and audibly, until a visitor performs the listening gesture — two simultaneous touch points, one on each rest, elbows-down as it were. Only then do whisper-blue sound glyphs bloom in the darkness above the table and the close, inside-the-skull sound begin. Lift either hand and everything cuts instantly, mid-bloom. The silence is structural, not a pause between sounds: without the gesture there is no piece to hear, only a table to look at.

## Construction

- **Scene.** Canvas 2D, single self-contained page. Background black #0C0B09. The table spans the full width: top slab in table wood #8A6A48 with 30 seeded grain strokes (mulberry32, fixed seed, drawn as slightly wavy horizontal lines in worn-pale #B89A68 and background-black at low alpha), a worn top edge line, an apron and two legs below. Two worn elbow spots (pale ellipses) sit in the slab face under the rests. Each rest is a bone-white #EDE8DE disc with an inner wear ring; its soft glow is built from three layered flat-alpha bone discs (no gradients, no shadowBlur), breathing on a slow 1.25 rad/s pulse that equals exactly 1.0 at t = 0.
- **The gesture (two-pointer model).** Pointer events are tracked by pointerId. A pointer going down within a generous grab radius of a rest claims that rest. Listening begins only when two distinct pointers hold the two rests at once, and ends the instant either pointer lifts, cancels, or is released — `refreshHolds` recomputes hold state on every pointer event and `setListening` opens/closes a hold interval at that moment. A single pointer on one rest only brightens that rest (readiness): no glyphs, no sound. Dragging while holding moves the touch midpoint; that midpoint, normalized to a lean value in [-1, 1], shifts the glyph field sideways and pans the audio.
- **Glyphs.** Blooms are a closed-form function of the recorded hold intervals and the piece clock t: while a hold is open, a bloom spawns every 0.8 s, alternating between the rests; each bloom is three concentric whisper-blue #7A8A9A rings whose center rises and radius grows over a 3.4 s life with a (1-p)^2 fade. Because blooms are evaluated only for intervals still open at t (an ended interval contributes nothing), lifting a hand erases them mid-bloom rather than letting them finish. The whole field is offset by the current lean.
- **Sound (only while listening).** The AudioContext is created lazily inside the first completed two-point gesture, all use typeof-guarded, and never autoplays. The voice is a whisper texture, deliberately not intelligible speech: a looped noise buffer through a lowpass, split into three bandpass formants (520 / 1150 / 2350 Hz) whose center frequencies wander on slow seeded LFOs so the murmur shifts like breath through a skull, plus a faint slow sawtooth line (196 Hz, vibrato, bow-swell gain LFO) under a heavy 640 Hz lowpass for the violin ghost. A stereo panner follows the lean midpoint. Master gain is modest (0.38). On release of either point the gain is driven to zero with a 0.03 s time constant — the cut is effectively instant. Before the gesture, and in any environment without an AudioContext, the piece is silent.
- **Determinism.** Frame one is the synchronous `draw(0)` at load: the waiting state, no holds, no glyphs, glow pulse at its t = 0 value. No `Date.now()` anywhere in drawn state; the live clock is elapsed rAF time. Grain is seeded data generated once from a fixed seed. No offscreen canvas, no ctx.filter, no gradients, no shadowBlur. rAF is typeof-guarded; with no rAF the piece still draws frame one and answers pointer redraws.

## Deviations and choices

- **Single-pointer compromise (recorded honestly).** The instrument is a two-pointer hold; a lone mouse can only claim one rest, which brightens it (readiness) but produces neither glyphs nor sound, because the structural rule — no sound without the full gesture — is the piece. A visitor with two input channels (touchscreen plus mouse, two fingers on a trackpad-and-screen setup, or simply two fingers on any touchscreen or phone) can complete the gesture. On a phone, the rests are spaced for two thumbs. This is a real limitation for single-mouse desktop visitors and it is kept deliberately: the prompt forbids any sound without the gesture, and weakening the gesture to one pointer would break the Handphone Table's premise that the body completes the circuit.
- **Whisper instead of words.** The prompt's whispered story is realized as formant-filtered noise murmur rather than synthesized speech: it reads as a voice inside the skull without putting intelligible text in the piece (house visible-text rule) and without depending on speech-synthesis voices.
- **Apron gap removed in QA.** The first build left a small black slit between the slab and the apron/legs, which made the tabletop appear to float; the apron and legs now meet the slab directly and the slab is slightly thicker, so the table reads as one object.
