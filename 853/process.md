# No.853 "Until the Ice Gives"
EXPANSION #104 laurie-anderson, PP14 "The Melt Clock" (Duets on Ice, 1974-75).

## Concept
Anderson performed Duets on Ice wearing skates frozen into blocks of ice, playing a duet with a self-playing violin, telling stories until the ice melted: the ending is physics, not dramaturgy. This piece rebuilds that contract. Two ice blocks with skate blades frozen into them stand center-bottom on a dark stage. Above them a machine staff carries the self-playing violin's looping phrase in amber. The visitor plays the second violin by dragging across the staff; taps call up one deadpan story fragment at a time. A melt parameter drains linearly over 240 seconds: the blocks shrink, drips fall faster, the pool widens. When the ice is gone the machine stops mid-phrase, the stage fades to black, and only the meltwater line remains. There is no replay button and no outro. A double-tap after the end starts a new performance, and that is the only way back.

## Story fragments (original, written for this piece, in her register)
1. "The ice was not in a hurry. Neither was I."
2. "He said the song would last exactly as long as the winter."
3. "Nobody watched the clock. The clock was made of water."
4. "I played one note until the note changed its mind."
5. "She asked how long it would take. I said, until it gives."
6. "By the last verse the stage was a puddle, and the puddle had perfect pitch."

## Construction
- Single self-contained index.html, canvas 2D, no external assets. Strict palette: ice blue #BFDCE5, meltwater #6FA8BE, deep ice #2A4A5A, stage black #0A0C0E, machine amber #C98A2B, user white #F0EDE4. No gradients, shadowBlur, ctx.filter, or offscreen canvas; ice translucency is layered flat alpha tones.
- Canvas sized from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on visualViewport and window; canvas style width/height pinned in px inside draw() every frame. Phone detection via window.screen physical size (min dimension < 700 px) with aspect fallback (h > w*1.15); phones get wider blocks, a higher floor, and a tighter staff.
- The melt/end model: local time lt runs from the performance epoch. meltFrac = 1 - lt/240, clamped; it never pauses and is a pure function of lt. Block height scales as meltFrac^0.8, width as 0.70 + 0.30*meltFrac; the pool width grows as 0.16 + 0.50*(1 - meltFrac) of the stage width. At lt = 240 the machine clock freezes on its current step (the note is cut, audio scheduling is gated on lt < 240), and everything except the pool fades out over 6 s. The pool is drawn outside the fade: it is the last thing on stage.
- Machine phrase: a fixed 16-step loop (0.5 s per step, 8 s per pass) of pentatonic degrees with rests, anchored at +2.5 s so frame one is mid-phrase with five steps already played and step 5 (a note) current and ringed. Played steps draw full amber, upcoming steps dim. The audio scheduler in the rAF loop follows the same closed-form step index as the drawing, so sound and image can never drift apart.
- User violin: drags inside the staff band record marks {lt, staff-x fraction, pentatonic degree, stroke id}; vertical drag position quantizes to the 8-degree pentatonic ladder shared with the machine. Marks draw as white bars joined by a thin line within a stroke and decay slowly toward a floor alpha. State is only the recorded event lists plus t: every frame is a closed-form replay.
- Drips: a mulberry32-seeded schedule generated once at load; spawn intervals tighten from about 2.3 s to about 0.7 s as the melt advances. Each drip's spawn height and x come from the analytic block geometry at its spawn time, and its fall is a closed-form gravity curve ending in a small splash tick at the pool line.
- Taps vs double-taps: a quick, unmoved pointer release is a tap and shows the next story fragment for 6 s with a fade envelope. Two taps within 0.35 s count as a double-tap; during the performance it does nothing (so the ending cannot be skipped), and after the end it resets the epoch and clears marks, taps, and fragments for a fresh performance.
- Audio: created lazily inside the first pointerdown, all AudioContext use typeof-guarded and wrapped in try/catch. Machine and user notes share one pluck voice (triangle plus sine octave through a lowpass, exponential decay), machine quieter and darker (0.15 gain, 1500 Hz) than the user (0.20, 2300 Hz), through a modest master gain and compressor. Nothing autoplays; the QA renderer has no AudioContext and the piece renders complete in silence.
- QA hook: a ?seed=<seconds> query offset shifts the piece clock at boot. It is inert in production (no parameter means offset 0) and exists so melt states can be rendered deterministically for review.

## QA log
- Renders via node ~/workspace/tools/render-piece.js: qa-desktop.png (1280x900), qa-mobile.png (390x844), thumb.png (720x720). All read back: dark stage, amber machine staff mid-phrase above (played notes bright, current note ringed, upcoming notes dim), two layered ice blocks center-bottom with sheen lines, internal melt streaks, blade struts visible through the ice, and the young pool at the base. Mobile holds the whole composition with nothing clipped.
- Determinism: thumb.png rendered twice and byte-compared with cmp: identical. Frame one is the synchronous draw(0): melt at 100%, no user line, no fragment. No Date.now anywhere in drawn state; the clock is rAF elapsed time anchored after frame one.
- Throwaway state renders via the seed hook (rendered, read back, deleted): seed=120 (melt 50%): blocks at about 57% height and narrower, pool roughly three times wider, blade struts emerging. seed=250 (past the end): stage fully black except the full-width meltwater line, exactly the specced end state. seed=45 and seed=200 spot checks confirmed the staff advancing through later passes, a falling drip visible on a block face at 45 s, and the skates standing nearly free at 200 s.
- Deviations: none from the prompt's substance. Two recorded choices: (1) the ?seed= time-offset QA hook described above; (2) during the 6 s end fade the machine's final frame is held frozen rather than continuing to loop silently, so the cut reads as a cut in the image as well as in the (gesture-gated) audio.
