# No.206 Instant Life

## Concept
Sammy the Sea Monkey, Ward's sixteen foot brine shrimp of heat welded recycled PETG, is monumental micro-fauna, and the honest way to draw a sixteen foot animal on a small screen is to not fit it in. Instant Life builds the whole shrimp as one long scene, three viewports wide, and shows only a window of it: translucent membrane over a segmented trunk, eleven leg pairs paddling along the belly side, eyestalks and claspers at the head, a tail fan at the far end. It swims upside down, the way real brine shrimp do, belly and legs toward the top of the frame, head entering from the upper left, tail trailing down to the lower right. The window pans slowly along the body on a forty second loop, head to tail and back. Drag and the window slides with you, up toward the head or down toward the tail; let go and it carries a little of your momentum before the slow pan takes it again. No glass, no waterline, no scale reference: the size is in the cropping.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The animal lives in world space, three body units long, the spine a falling curve from upper left to lower right, body radius a profile fat amidships and thinning to the tail. The trunk is fifteen overlapping elliptical slabs in #14333E; the eleven leg pairs are stalk strokes with slim paddle leaves, each phase lagged behind its neighbour so a paddling wave travels the belly; the head is a broader slab carrying two swaying eyestalks with black compound eyes and white glints, and two clasper hooks underneath that open and close on a slow sine. The membrane is a single filled outline hugging the trunk at forty percent alpha in #A8CCD8, stroked with the weld seam colour, with bright seam lines across the skin at every segment joint, the whole skin displaced by a subtle swimming ripple. The camera is a single offset with a direction: it traverses the spare width in twenty seconds each way, clamps and reverses at the ends, takes drag deltas directly (vertical per the prompt, horizontal accepted too) and keeps a decaying velocity after release. Frame one opens on a mid-body window, legs and membrane filling the frame edge to edge, no whole animal anywhere in sight. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen, rAF typeof guarded, no text, no props, nothing drawn offscreen.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three, plus two extra phase renders from throwaway copies with the camera moved to the head and tail ends (the shipped file was not altered for these). Round one faults: the spine sloped the wrong way, so the tail rode up out of the frame instead of trailing lower right; the animal was too small for its window, leaving the bottom half of the frame empty black; and the paddle leaves were balloon sized. Round two reversed and gentled the spine, raised the body scale to a full frame height unit, slimmed the leaves and stalks, pulled the eyestalk tips back inside the world so the eyes survive the head window, and shrank the tail fan inside the world bounds. Final frames: mid-body fills the view with legs arcing over the trunk, the head window shows both glinting eyes and the clasper sweep, the tail window tapers to the fan at the corner, and mobile holds the same monumental crop.
