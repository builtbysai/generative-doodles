# No.182 Kogame Curvature

## Concept
Goodman-Strauss's woven Klein quartic lesson: in a kogame weave, curvature is not imposed on the surface, it is counted into it. Where the triangular holes between strips have five sides the weaving has no choice but to cup upward, six sides lie flat, seven flare into the hyperbolic plane. The piece makes the hole count a knob the viewer turns with a finger: drag and the surface under your hand changes its geometry. At rest it is a mixed landscape, a flat plain with one sphere-cupped hill and one hyperbolic flare, so the three states can be compared at a glance.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The weave is two families of flat paper strips on a 13 by 10 junction grid (9 by 14 on portrait phones): horizontal strips coloured by row, vertical strips by column, each family cycling a seeded shuffle of the seven specified colours. Strips are drawn cell by cell as warped quads; at each crossing the over family alternates in checkerboard order, and the over strip is underlaid with a slightly larger ground quad so a clean 2px ground gap separates it from the strip passing beneath. The surface is a height field: every junction carries a continuous curvature value (+1 cup, 0 flat, -1 flare), bilinearly interpolated, and strip corners are displaced by it, raised regions lifting and swelling, flared regions sinking and spreading, with a ground-colour slope shade for the light. At every crossing a small ground polygon is punched whose side count is the rounded local hole count, 5, 6 or 7, so the control is literally drawn on the field. The base landscape is two gaussian blobs (cup upper middle, flare lower left) over a gentle cosine plain. Untouched, a 60 second morph bias cycles the whole field flat to flare to cup to flat, cosine eased between keys; dragging pauses the morph, and each accumulated half-pitch of horizontal drag steps the hole count under the finger (right toward 7, left toward 5) with a 2.2 cell falloff, the curvature easing after it. A short tap reshuffles both families' colour orders from a new seed. Palette exactly per spec: the seven strip colours on deep warm gray #17171B, nothing else. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, field full-bleed at every size, rAF typeof guarded, hint lowercase and short. The internal clock is offset 25 seconds and the field is pre-rolled to its targets at build, so the first frame is the settled mixed landscape, mid morph.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three. The first pass failed visually in an instructive way: strips at 80 percent of pitch read as a grid of coloured squares rather than a weave, and the curvature was a fine jitter instead of three legible regions. Fixes: strip width dropped to 68 percent of pitch so both families read as continuous ribbons crossing; the landscape's cosine modes were quieted and the two blobs strengthened so cup, plain and flare are regional; warp amplitude and radial swell roughly doubled; slope shading capped lower so gold strips no longer go muddy. After the fix the desktop frame shows ribbons bowing over the cupped hill and spreading in the flare, the phone frame buckles visibly down its right side, and the punched holes read as dark polygons whose roundness varies by region. Honest trait: at thumbnail size the 5 and 7 sided holes differ more in roundness than in countable sides; the side count is legible at full size, and the morph makes each state unmistakable in motion.
