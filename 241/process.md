# No.241 Seeing the Night Bluely

## Concept
After Woodfill's 2018 proposal for the KCTV tower: record the day's sky, broadcast it the next night. A wide horizon band of forty horizontal colour strata plays back a single day's arc, dawn amber through morning and noon pale through dusk violet to night, drifting almost imperceptibly over a near black field, with one thin registration line marking the tower, the broadcast source. A full day plays over six minutes, the night holds for thirty seconds, then a new sky starts with different weather. It is a broadcast signal, not a landscape: no sun, no moon, no clouds. Colour as an adverb. Watch only.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The sky is a five stop arc (dawn, morning, noon, dusk, night) sampled as a function of the day's phase; the band renders it as exactly 40 flat strata, each stratum sampling the arc at a slightly phase-shifted phase, the top of the band leading and the bottom trailing by a fifth of the arc across the band, so the day visibly drifts downward and any single frame holds a legible slice of the whole arc. Each six minute cycle is seeded with a weather: clear keeps the full gamut, overcast compresses every stop toward grey, storm drags every stop low and dark. After the arc, the band holds night for thirty seconds before the next seeded sky. The registration line is a single hairline at 62 percent of the band width running the full height of the field, with a small cross tick at mid height. The opening clock starts 78 seconds into the third sky, a clear one, mid morning. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen with the aspect fallback, rAF typeof guarded, no handlers, no text.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 from the shipped script and inspected all three. The first frame one landed in an overcast cycle with too narrow a strata phase spread and read as a blank grey slab, so the opening clock was moved to a clear seeded sky and the phase spread widened; frame one now shows the band grading from noon pale at the top through morning to dawn amber at its base, the registration line and its tick visible against the dark. On the phone frame the 4:1 band goes full width as a bright horizon strip in the black field. No further adjustments were needed.
