# No.181 Light Organ 24

## Concept
Goodman-Strauss's Four Dimensional Light Orchestra: the 24-cell, all 96 edges of it, drawn as arcs of light, where the object never changes and only the lighting does. Each lighting state recolours the edges by class, and the recolouring is a different sub symmetry of the whole made visible. Lighting as the symmetry operator, exactly his MoMath instrument, minus the parts a gallery page should not ask for.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. The 24-cell is built from its definition: the 8 permutations of (plus or minus 1, 0, 0, 0) and the 16 half integer vertices, edges between every pair at distance 1, which yields exactly 96 edges (verified by count at build time, 24 per class). Each edge carries an axis class: for an edge from an axis vertex, that coordinate; for an edge between half integer vertices, the coordinate they differ in. Vertices rotate in four 4D planes (XW and YZ drifting slowly, ZW under drag, XZ drifting), drag mapping to the XW and ZW plane angles, then project stereographically from 4D to 3D and perspectively to the screen. Each edge is stroked as a quadratic bezier through its two projected endpoints and the projected edge midpoint on the sphere, the circular arc the prompt specifies; a full strength pass plus a thin core pass give the tube its body, over a permanent faint idle underlay of all 96 edges in #3A3A40. Lighting states: in state s, axis class s mod 4 rests at idle and the others take the four colour palette rotated by s, so each state lights a different coordinate symmetry; states crossfade over 1.5 seconds, advance every 10 seconds through a 60 second scripted cycle, and a tap jumps to the next state boundary. Depth is carried by the stereographic scale of each edge, mapped to alpha. Deviation, deliberate and recorded: the prompt feeds state changes from a microphone, but gallery pieces do not request mic permission, so no mic is requested anywhere in the piece; the shipped behaviour is drag, tap, and the scripted quiescent sequence, which is the prompt's own stated fallback. The internal state clock is offset by half a block so the first frame sits inside state 0, mid hold, fully lit. Palette exactly per spec: ground #050505, idle #3A3A40, lit cycle #E4572E, #F2B233, #4CAF7D, #2E6BE6. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, sculpture centred at about 80 percent of frame height with black margins, rAF typeof guarded, hint lowercase and short.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three, plus five orientation test renders during tuning. Two real bugs were caught by the renders, not by reading code: the state crossfade weights made the first frame display the previous state (the frame showed red, which state 0 cannot produce), proven by a pixel census of the render (green channel count exactly zero), and fixed by offsetting the state clock half a block; the census after the fix shows gold, green and blue present and red correctly absent, since red's class rests in state 0. Orientation was tuned across variants: the axis aligned view is degenerate (both w axis vertices project to the centre), the 45 degree XW view collapses many edges onto shared circles, and the shipped base orientation (0.6, 0.35, 0.4, 0.2 radians across the four planes) is the one where the weave, the vertex nodes and the arc tubing all read at once. The phone frame centres the same sculpture with generous black margins. Honest trait: a family of edges passes near the projection pole and bows into very large arcs at some orientations; that is what stereographic projection does to those circles, and the slow 4D drift carries the sculpture through and out of those moments.
