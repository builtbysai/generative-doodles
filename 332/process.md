# No.332 Cartographies of Cloth

## Concept

After Arunima Dazess Wangchuk's 2025 textile work: a map you could wear. Felted topographic contour lines in rust, terracotta and burnt orange wander across a cloth ground, and exactly one dark blue-black river ribbon runs through them on the steepest-descent path, entering at one frame edge and leaving at another, never touching a corner. Idle, the terrain hangs perfectly still, a finished cloth map. Dragging raises the land under the pointer; the contours re-form around the new hill and the river finds a new way down, and when the touch lifts the redrawn terrain simply stays, the way a crease stays in cloth.

## Construction

One canvas, everything as JS data, nothing pre-rendered and no offscreen layer. The elevation field lives on a fixed 87 by 61 node grid: four octaves of seeded value noise over a broad diagonal fall, high toward the top-left, low toward the bottom-right. Contours are marching squares extracted from the field as path segments on every draw, fifteen levels, cycled through the three band colours; the felt look is data only, per-segment stroke width jitter from a fixed 1024-entry seed table plus short fibre speckle strokes laid just off the contour paths at low alpha.

The river is steepest descent from the grid's highest border node, with corners and a three-cell margin around them excluded as starts by construction and asserted in code. Raw noise is full of pits, so the descent runs on a depression-filled copy of the field: a Barnes priority flood seeded from the three non-entry edges only, which makes the entry edge a watershed divide rather than an outlet, and a hair of each node's flood order is added to its filled height so filled flats still drain. One refinement beyond the spec's letter, recorded here: if a heavy drag walls off a corner behind a plateau, the walk can pool against a lip with no lower neighbour; it then spills once over the lowest unvisited neighbour and resumes descending, with visited marks keeping it a single line. The path is Chaikin-smoothed, extended past both frame edges, and filled as one ribbon polygon. Dragging adds a gaussian bump (sigma 120 design px, clamped so scribbling cannot run away) to the field in design coords via the stored cover transform; contours and river are recomputed on the next frame and hold on release, no spring back. House rules throughout: Visual Viewport sizing with resize on visualViewport and window, canvas pinned every frame, cover-fit full-bleed at 1280x900 design space, synchronous developed first frame, typeof-guarded rAF, fixed seeds, no text or UI in the piece.

## QA log

Rendered with node ~/workspace/tools/render-piece.js at 1280x900 (qa-desktop.png), 390x844 (qa-mobile.png) and 720x720 (thumb.png), and all three inspected. First pass failed the gate: greedy descent on the raw field knotted into a space-filling tangle at the top edge, and descent on a filled field seeded from all four edges pooled in an entry-edge hollow after 18 steps. Fixed with the non-entry-edge flood described above; verified by instrumented runs: initial river 107 nodes, entering the top edge at node (15, 0) and exiting the bottom edge at (69, 60), no corner node touched. Simulated drags (a stroke across the middle, repeated heavy bumps near the entry and the corner, a mountain dead centre) all re-routed without exceptions and with start and exit never inside the corner margin; the extreme corner-plateau case produces a short 9 to 15 node river off the plateau fringe, which is the terrain's honest drainage and was accepted. Final desktop and thumb renders confirmed byte-identical to the inspected good versions. Palette audit: only rust #A9502E, terracotta #C96F4A, burnt orange #D97B3F, cloth ground #E7DCC8 and river #1E2A33 appear. No em dashes in the file. Not pushed; staged for fly's gate.
