# No.810 "The Stack" — EXPANSION #99 jacob-kassay, block 3 "The Stack"

## Concept

Kassay's 2009 Eleven Rivington debut: a pile of silvered panels stacked flat on a low white plinth in a dark gallery room. Each panel face is foggy silver with burnt, tarnished edges; the stack's side shows the layered sheets. The composition is the stack shrinking: drag across it and the top panel arcs off and comes to rest leaning beside the plinth, dark tarnished underside out. Drag again for the next. Double-tap and every panel eases back; the plinth is whole again. When all seven are flipped, the plinth stands empty until the double-tap. The view never moves; only the panels do.

## Construction

- Single self-contained `index.html`, canvas 2D only. Palette strict: silver between #A9A9A9 and #BFBFBF (weave tone #BDBDBD sits inside that range), tarnish #5A3A1E, plinth #F2F2F2, room #141414, floor #232323. Every intermediate tone is one of these at partial alpha or a mix inside the silver range. No gradients, no shadowBlur, no ctx.filter, no offscreen canvas; softness is layered flat alpha shapes (fog ellipses, nested inset tarnish strokes for the burnt edges, ellipse contact shadows).
- Room: room-tone field, floor band from 70% height with three faint seam lines, a dim plinth-white wall wash at 4.5% alpha behind the plinth, floor shadow ellipse. Plinth: top parallelogram, front face with a 9% room-tone shade, right face at 10%.
- Stack: 7 panels. Each stacked panel draws its right side strip (silver, tarnish overlay) and its front strip (silver with a tarnish creep band at the lower rim and a 1px room-tone separator), so the layered sheets read along both edges. Only the topmost stacked panel draws its full top face: seeded fog blobs, weave streaks, and four nested inset tarnish strokes as the burnt edge. Per-panel surface data (tone position in the silver range, ±0.3%-of-width placement jitter, blobs, streaks, underside mottling, edge strengths) is precomputed from mulberry32 streams seeded per panel.
- Event model: interaction is a recorded list of events on the piece's own rAF timeline — `{flip, t}` and `{reset, t}`. A panel's pose is a closed-form function of the list and t: replaying the list rebuilds each panel's current animation segment (from/to/start/duration), and the flip amount is an easeOutCubic over that segment. Nothing integrates frame to frame. A flip takes the top remaining panel; flips alternate sides (right, left, ...) and same-side panels fan with a small outward step and slightly reduced height. Reset returns all flipped panels with a 0.07 s stagger, 0.95 s each.
- Flip pose: the panel's corners lerp from its stack top-face quad to its leaning quad, plus a sine arc lift (8.5% of height at mid-flip) and a small outward push. The leaning panel stands on its long edge on the floor, tucked against the plinth with its inner portion overlapping the plinth front (28% on desktop, 62% on phones), base x clamped inside the frame. Its upright height is the panel's stack depth (the slant in the stack is that depth foreshortened), so the panel keeps its own size. The face crossfades silver to dark tarnished underside through a smoothstep on the flip amount (0.30–0.70), with a tarnish rim offset behind the face as its thickness.
- Interaction: pointerdown/move/up on the canvas. A horizontal drag crossing max(42px, 9% of width) records one flip (once per gesture). A tap (under 12px of movement) followed by a second tap within 0.38 s records a reset. Event times come from the rAF clock, never the wall clock. The double-tap reset is the prompt's own specced mechanic, not a page reload.
- House rules: canvas sized from the Visual Viewport API with resize listeners on visualViewport and window; canvas.style.width/height pinned in px every draw; phone detection via window.screen (min dimension < 700px) with the h > w*1.15 aspect fallback — phones get a wider plinth/panel fraction and a deeper lean overlap; page ground is the room tone. rAF is typeof-guarded; frame one is the synchronous draw at t = 0. No audio, no visible text except `<title>`.

## QA log

- Rendered with `node ~/workspace/tools/render-piece.js`: qa-desktop.png (1280x900), qa-mobile.png (390x844), thumb.png (720x720). thumb.png rendered twice and `cmp` byte-compared: identical (determinism gate passed; second render deleted). Re-verified after every fix round.
- Frame one read back at all three sizes: dark room, white plinth low-center, a neat stack of seven silver panels with layered sheet edges and tarnish rims, top face foggy with burnt edges; mobile keeps the whole composition with nothing clipped.
- Throwaway flipped-state renders via the inert `?seed=flipsN` QA hook (pre-records N flips at negative times, settled before frame one; no effect in normal viewing): flips2 desktop — two panels leaning either side, five remaining stacked, undersides dark and readable. flips7 desktop — all seven flipped, plinth empty, fans of 4 right / 3 left, all panels inside the frame. flips7 mobile — flipped panels in frame, none clipped. Throwaways deleted after inspection.
- Fix round 1: the first leaning placement started each panel at the plinth's outer edge, so panels ran off both frame edges, and the underside mottling read as large cartoon spots. Leaning quads rebuilt to tuck against the plinth with overlap (clamped in-frame), and the underside mottling reduced to smaller, fainter blobs (14 per panel, alpha 0.05–0.15).
- Fix round 2: the QA hook's flip spacing (1.6 s from −6 s) left later flips scheduled after t = 0, so flips7 only showed four settled flips. Hook respaced to −9.5 s + 1.35 s steps so all requested flips settle before frame one; piece behaviour unchanged.
- Deviations: (1) Stack count fixed at 7, the middle of the prompt's 6–8 range. (2) Flips alternate sides and fan, where the prompt shows panels arcing "aside" without specifying accumulation; alternating keeps the shrinking stack centred and every flipped panel visible. (3) The flipped panel's upright height uses its stack depth as drawn (the slant foreshortening read back as a real dimension), so a leaning panel is wide and moderately tall rather than a full square; recorded here as a construction choice, consistent across all seven panels. (4) The inert `?seed=flipsN` render hook follows the study's established QA pattern (cf. No.785) and is inert in normal viewing.
