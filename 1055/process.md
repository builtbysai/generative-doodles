# No.1055 "The Carts Drive Themselves"

Study #129 katsuhiko-harada, PP216 "The First Autonomous Night" (Coded Field 2019, the first fully autonomous driving device used for live performance).

## Concept

A dark temple courtyard at night, seen from above. Four glowing carts drive themselves through it on seeded routes, threading between the temple at the center and a wandering crowd of small audience lights. No operators, no remote controls: the subject is the autonomy itself, and autonomy reads clearest at a meeting. When two carts would arrive at a crossing together, the lower-priority cart slows, stops short of the crossing, waits, and resumes once the way is clear. The yield is the cart's own decision, precomputed into its schedule the way a real dispatch system would plan it. The crowd is not a pedestrian simulation: the devices are lights first, drifting on slow currents, changing nothing but the field the carts thread through.

## Construction

- World: logical 1000x1000 courtyard, contain-fit, full-bleed. Temple drawn in dark lines (#1C1E24) at center with nested rectangles and a cross; five simple roofline gables at the edges hold the venue.
- Routes: four interleaved rectangles (wide, tall, offset square, offset wide) with seeded corner jitter of plus or minus 3 units, eight waypoints each (corners and edge midpoints). The rectangles are chosen so every pair of routes crosses several times, while parallel edges of different routes stay at least 50 units apart, so side-by-side running never registers as a meeting. Every route clears the temple.
- Schedules: each cart's motion is a closed-form piece list in local time (go at constant velocity, waypoint waits of 0.55 s, yield decel/wait/accel triples with 0.7 s ramps, and a final home wait) summing to exactly the 75 s loop. Cruise time is budgeted at 62 s of the loop; distance along the route maps to a point on the polyline, so cart position is a pure function of loop phase.
- The yield model (the core): meetings are engineered, not hoped for. The builder computes the actual crossing points of the route geometry and sets cart phases along a chain (cart 1 meets cart 0, cart 2 meets cart 1, cart 3 meets cart 2) by inverting the base schedules, so pairs genuinely arrive at shared crossings together. It then resolves conflicts iteratively: scan all pairs over the loop at 0.05 s steps, take the earliest approach closer than 42 units, and insert a yield stop for the higher-index cart 82 units before the conflict point on its own route (2.2 s wait, extended by 1.6 s increments if the same crossing still conflicts after re-planning). Repeat until the loop scans clean, with random-phase attempts as fallback. Finally all phases rotate by one common shift so a yield wait opens at t = 23.5 s; a common rotation preserves relative timing, so the resolution stands.
- Yield verification (probe run against the shipped math, seed 1055216): the un-yielded schedules produced 2 conflict events; resolution took 3 scan rounds and inserted 2 yield stops, both on cart 1 (route distances 124 and 1367, 2.2 s each). After resolution the minimum pairwise distance over the full loop, sampled at 0.02 s, is 42.56 units, at or above the 42 unit conflict threshold everywhere. Loop closure is exact (0.000 gap per cart), and no schedule overflows the 75 s budget (cart 1, carrying both yields, totals 71.65 s before its home wait).
- Frame one (t = 24 s): cart 1 is stopped inside its first yield (global wait window 23.5 to 25.7 s) at (-188, -308), short of its crossing with cart 0; cart 3 is in a waypoint wait at the right edge; carts 0 and 2 are mid-route with trails behind them. The second yield window runs 59.8 to 62.0 s.
- Crowd: 120 dots on a seeded fixed-step drift sim at 30 Hz (layered sine currents, toroidal wrap, hard exclusion from the temple rectangle), drawn as small cool lights with a slow twinkle. Dots are colored by the piece palette only; they do not avoid the carts, and the carts' routes thread the field by construction.
- Trails: per-cart recorded position buffers sampled every 0.32 s over a 6.5 s span, drawn as fading warm segments. Never a canvas fade.
- Interaction: drag makes you a pedestrian: the nearest cart within 120 units reroutes laterally around your finger, and if you block its path (close ahead of it) its own clock holds while you stand there, then resumes; the drag also pulls a gentle current in the crowd. Tap a cart to draw its full route bright for 5 s (the plan, revealed). Double-tap reseeds the whole night: routes, phases, yields and crowd are rebuilt and pre-rolled so the new night opens developed.
- Sound (first gesture only, lazy and typeof-guarded): one soft sine glide per cart, pitched up while cruising and dipping in pitch and level while that cart yields or is held for a pedestrian; faint lowpassed night air; sparse quiet ticks voiced from crowd positions. Silent in QA.
- House rules: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px in draw(), phone detection via window.screen with aspect fallback, strict six-color palette, no gradients, no shadowBlur, no ctx.filter, no offscreen canvas, no microphone, zero visible text besides the title.

## Deviations

- Drag-yielding is live interaction state (per-cart held clock shift and lateral detour), not part of the closed-form schedule: a pedestrian is by definition unplanned. Pre-input frames, including frame one, are untouched by it. Schedule yields between carts are fully closed-form.
- The crowd flow is continuous and does not repeat dot-for-dot at the 75 s boundary; carts, yields and trails close exactly on the loop. The crowd is atmosphere (lights), and its honesty note belongs here rather than claiming a seamless crowd loop.
- Parallel-edge clearance is 50 units by construction while the conflict threshold is 42, so two carts can run side by side on neighboring edges without either yielding; that is deliberate, since dispatch yields belong at crossings, not along shared corridors.
- On phones the courtyard contain-fits (letterboxed by the courtyard's own ground color) rather than cropping, following the No.1046 presentation, so no cart or route ever leaves the frame.
