# No.195 FRESH FISH CRATE

## Concept
Amanda Willshire builds fish the way a contour map reads: flat steel silhouettes plasma-cut and welded front to back in layers, the colour living on the layers themselves, gold where the heat took the front steel, red where rust was forced into the fins. And her fish travel in crates stenciled FRESH FISH CRATE. The piece opens at the end of that ritual: the crate lid, hinged along the top edge, has just swung up and over, the stencil riding it, and the fish is already swimming. After four seconds the crate is gone entirely and the fish owns the frame.

## Construction
Canvas 2D, single self contained page. Three parametric silhouettes (trout, bass, tarpon) are hand-set point rings: a body spline with a tail/fork parameter, the tarpon's fork deepest, the bass's body deepest. Each fish is sliced into fourteen depth slabs: back layers largest and deepest in rust shadow #3B1F0E, mid layers raw steel #6B7280, the three front layers heat-tint gold #C9A227, every slab stroked with a 1px dark rim and offset a few px along the depth axis, so the stack reads as a topographic contour set. Fins (tail wash at 60 percent, dorsal, pectoral) are drawn in forced-rust #8B2500 on the front layer only; the eye is a single ground-coloured dot. Nothing is painted to imitate fish skin. The swim is a sine tail-beat propagated point by point through every layer with a per-layer phase lag, so the whole stack ripples and the tail travels most. Dragging pulls the stack apart: drag distance drives an explode factor through a stiff, damped spring (60/11), spreading each layer along the depth axis into the separated contour set; release and the spring settles it back with a breath of overshoot, the heavy metallic settle. Double-tap cycles the silhouette pool with a quick horizontal flip dip. The crate is drawn only while the piece clock is under 4.35s: side and bottom planks around the fish, and the lid in its own basis transform, its top edge pinned to the crate's top edge while its free edge swings from hanging closed to flipped up and away; the stencil is painted pre-flipped so it reads upright on the settled lid, the way a flung-open lid lands. The clock starts at 3.2s, so frame one is the reveal, not a closed box. Palette exactly per spec; the stencil is the only text in the piece. House rules held: Visual Viewport sizing with both resize listeners, canvas pinned in px every frame, phone detection via window.screen, full width fish on phones, rAF typeof guarded.

## QA log
Rendered at 1280x900, 390x844 and 720x720 and inspected all three, through three rounds. Round one faults: the fourteen layers were sliced too timidly (1 percent scale steps, 1.6px offsets) and read as one solid gold slab; the crate was oversized (1.14 by 4.3 depth units) with empty air around the fish; and the lid, rotated about the crate's top-centre as a rigid panel, swept a corner down across the tail. Round two: scale steps widened to 1.4 percent, offsets to 2.6px, gold confined to the front three layers, crate tightened to 1.08 by 3.2; the contour stepping then showed clearly at the belly and tail, most legibly on the phone frame. Round three replaced the lid mechanics entirely: the panel is now drawn in a hinge basis (top edge fixed, free edge swung by angle), so it flips up and away without ever crossing the fish, and the stencil is painted pre-flipped so it settles upright and readable on the flung lid in all three frames. Final frames: gold trout fully revealed and mid-swim, rust fins, striped layer crescents fanning at the tail, the open crate framing it for its last second. The explode spring, swim and silhouette cycling are time and interaction driven; they were verified by tracing the spring integrator and the drag/double-tap handlers (double-tap is gated on sub-10px pointer travel so a drag can never trigger a reseed).
